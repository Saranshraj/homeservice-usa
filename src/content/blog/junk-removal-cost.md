Hiring a junk removal company typically costs **$150 to $600 for most households**, with full truckload jobs running $400 to $800 or more. If you just have a few items, a minimum load usually starts around $75 to $150. Those numbers hold across most of the country, though prices in coastal metro areas like New York, Los Angeles, and Seattle can run 20 to 40 percent higher than the national average.

If you are staring down a garage full of old furniture, a basement packed after a parent moves out, or a renovation leaving mountains of debris, you are not alone. Millions of American homeowners use junk removal services every year, and knowing what to expect before you call can save you a real headache on the bill.

## What You Are Actually Paying For

Junk removal pricing is based almost entirely on how much space your stuff takes up in the truck, measured in fractions of a standard load. Most companies use a truck that holds roughly 10 cubic yards (the equivalent of about three pickup truck loads). Here is how that typically breaks down in 2026:

| Load Size | Cubic Yards | Typical Cost Range |
|-----------|-------------|-------------------|
| Minimum load (a few items) | 1/8 truck | $75 to $150 |
| Small load | 1/4 truck | $150 to $250 |
| Medium load | 1/2 truck | $250 to $400 |
| Large load | 3/4 truck | $350 to $600 |
| Full truckload | Full truck | $400 to $800+ |

The crew also does the heavy lifting, which is included in the price. That labor value is worth remembering when you compare quotes.

## Prices by Region

Where you live makes a real difference. Labor and disposal tipping fees vary significantly across the United States.

| Region | Typical Range (Half Load) |
|--------|--------------------------|
| Northeast (NY, MA, CT, NJ) | $300 to $500 |
| West Coast (CA, WA, OR) | $275 to $475 |
| Midwest (IL, OH, MI, MN) | $200 to $350 |
| Southeast (FL, GA, NC, TN) | $175 to $325 |
| Southwest (TX, AZ, NV) | $200 to $375 |
| Mountain West (CO, UT, ID) | $200 to $350 |

California stands out as one of the priciest states due to strict landfill regulations and higher disposal fees, particularly in the Bay Area and Los Angeles metro. Rural areas tend to run cheaper, but you may pay a travel fee if you live far from a service provider.

## What Affects the Final Price

A few specific factors can push your quote higher or lower than the ranges above.

**Type of items you have.** Most standard household furniture, appliances, boxes, and debris fall under general pricing. But certain items cost more to dispose of properly. Electronics (called e-waste) contain hazardous materials and require special handling. Mattresses are bulky and often face recycling fees. Tires, paint cans, and propane tanks are frequently refused outright or charged extra.

**Accessibility.** If the crew has easy access from a driveway or ground floor, pricing is straightforward. Add stairs, a tight attic, a crawl space, or items buried under other items, and many companies add a labor surcharge, typically $25 to $75 extra.

**Hazardous materials.** Junk removal companies are not licensed to handle asbestos, lead paint, biohazardous waste, or large quantities of chemicals. Those require specialized contractors. The EPA has strict guidelines on proper disposal; you can find guidance at [epa.gov/hazwaste](https://www.epa.gov/hazwaste). Do not try to include these in a standard junk removal job.

**Distance to disposal facilities.** Companies pay tipping fees at transfer stations and landfills. If the nearest facility is farther away, that cost gets passed along.

**Day and time.** Weekend and same-day service often costs more. If you can be flexible, booking a midweek slot may get you a better rate.

## Items Most Junk Removal Companies Accept

Here is what most crews will haul away without issue:

- Old furniture: sofas, beds, dressers, tables, chairs
- Appliances: refrigerators, washers, dryers, dishwashers, stoves (may carry a Freon fee for refrigerators)
- Yard waste and debris in bags or bins
- Construction debris: drywall, lumber, flooring (not asbestos-containing materials)
- Exercise equipment
- Hot tubs (usually a specialty job, $300 to $600 extra)
- Boxes and general household clutter
- Electronics and e-waste (often for a small fee)
- Mattresses (expect a $20 to $50 recycling fee per mattress)

## Items They Usually Refuse

Most companies will not haul:

- Asbestos or materials suspected to contain it
- Paint cans, solvents, motor oil, and other chemicals
- Propane tanks
- Medical waste
- Biohazardous materials
- Batteries in large quantities
- Ammunition

For hazardous household waste, check with your local municipality. Most counties hold periodic free HHW (Household Hazardous Waste) collection events. The EPA has a tool to find your nearest facility at [epa.gov/hwgenerators](https://www.epa.gov/hwgenerators/learn-about-household-hazardous-waste).

## Big Jobs: Estate and Whole-Home Cleanouts

When you need an entire home cleared, pricing shifts to a full-job estimate rather than a by-the-load rate. Estate cleanouts, hoarder cleanouts, and foreclosure cleanouts can run $500 to $2,500 or more depending on the size of the home and amount of material.

For a typical 3-bedroom home that is moderately full, expect $600 to $1,200 for a full cleanout. Some companies offer a flat per-room rate, usually $100 to $200 per room.

If a family member recently passed away and you are handling the estate, some companies specialize in compassionate estate cleanouts and can coordinate with donation organizations to redirect usable items.

## Renting a Dumpster Instead

For large renovation projects or when you need time to sort through things yourself, renting a roll-off dumpster is often more economical than hiring a crew.

| Option | Pros | Cons | Typical Cost |
|--------|------|------|-------------|
| Junk removal crew | Done for you, includes labor | More expensive per load | $150 to $800 |
| Dumpster rental | Work at your own pace, fill it over days | You do the loading | $300 to $550 for one week |
| Bagster (bag pickup) | No scheduling, buy at hardware store | Small capacity (3 cubic yards) | $30 for bag + $150 to $200 for pickup |

Dumpster rentals typically include a 7-day rental period with a set weight limit. Going over the weight limit adds $50 to $100 per ton. If you are doing a full kitchen gut or major flooring project, the weight adds up fast with tile and concrete.

## Saving Money on Junk Removal

A few smart moves can lower your bill meaningfully.

**Donate first.** Organizations like Habitat for Humanity's ReStores accept used appliances, furniture, and building materials in decent condition. The Salvation Army and Goodwill pick up large items in many areas. What they take, you do not pay to remove.

**Sell what you can.** Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist move furniture, appliances, and tools quickly. Even $50 or $100 from a few items reduces what has to go.

**Separate your items.** Cardboard, clean wood, metal, and yard waste can often go to your municipal recycling center for free or very low cost. Scrap metal in particular has value. Many metal recyclers pay $5 to $30 per appliance, which some junk removal companies will pass along as a discount.

**Get multiple quotes.** Prices can vary 20 to 40 percent between companies for the same job. Most companies offer free estimates, and many will come out to give you an on-site quote.

**Book in advance.** Same-day and next-day service cost more. Booking a week out often saves 10 to 15 percent.

## Questions to Ask Before You Book

Before you confirm, get clear answers to these:

1. Is the estimate based on volume in the truck, item count, or weight?
2. Does the price include all labor, loading, and disposal fees?
3. Are there extra charges for appliances, electronics, or mattresses?
4. What happens if the load is larger than estimated?
5. Is the company licensed and insured? (This matters if anything gets damaged.)
6. Do they donate or recycle, or does everything go to the landfill?

Reputable companies should be happy to answer all of these clearly. If a company is vague or refuses to give a written estimate, that is a red flag.

## Post-Disaster Cleanouts

After a flood, fire, or severe storm, cleanup takes on a different urgency. FEMA sometimes provides assistance for debris removal following federally declared disasters. If your area has been declared a disaster zone, check [fema.gov/assistance](https://www.fema.gov/assistance) for details on what costs may be covered. Keep all your receipts and documentation.

After flooding especially, water-damaged items need to come out quickly to prevent mold. The EPA recommends removing wet materials within 24 to 48 hours to stop mold growth; see [epa.gov/mold](https://www.epa.gov/mold) for guidance on what to toss versus what might be salvageable.

## DIY Junk Hauling: When It Makes Sense

If you have a truck or SUV and the energy, handling a small amount of junk yourself can save $100 or more. Dropping off items at your local transfer station typically costs $25 to $75 for a standard truck or SUV load, depending on the facility and weight.

This works well for single large items like an old mattress or furniture piece. It makes less sense for a full cleanout when you factor in multiple trips, your time, and any rental vehicles.
