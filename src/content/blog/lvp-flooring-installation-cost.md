Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) flooring typically costs **$3 to $10 per square foot installed**, or roughly **$3,000 to $10,000 for a 1,000-square-foot area**. If you install it yourself, you can cut that down to $1 to $5 per square foot in materials only. LVP has exploded in popularity because it looks like real hardwood, handles moisture better than wood or laminate, and costs a fraction of what solid hardwood runs. If you are weighing your flooring options, this guide breaks down exactly what drives the price and where you can save money without cutting corners.

## What Is LVP Flooring?

LVP stands for luxury vinyl plank. It is a multi-layer synthetic flooring product engineered to mimic hardwood. Each plank has a rigid or semi-rigid core (usually stone plastic composite or wood plastic composite), a photographic print layer showing a wood grain design, and a clear wear layer on top that resists scratches and moisture.

Unlike traditional vinyl sheet flooring from the 1970s and 80s, modern LVP is thick, durable, and visually convincing. It floats above the subfloor with a click-lock installation, meaning no glue or nails in most cases. That is one reason it is popular with DIY homeowners.

## What Drives the Price Up or Down

### 1. Plank Thickness

LVP comes in thicknesses ranging from 4mm to 12mm. Thicker planks feel sturdier underfoot, hide minor subfloor imperfections better, and are generally more comfortable to walk on. Here is what you will typically find on shelves:

- **4mm to 5mm**: Entry-level. Works in low-traffic areas like closets or guest rooms. Budget-friendly at $0.75 to $1.50 per square foot.
- **6mm to 8mm**: Most popular range for bedrooms and living areas. $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot in materials.
- **8mm to 12mm**: Premium thickness for busy households, dogs, and high-heeled shoe traffic. $3.00 to $5.00+ per square foot.

### 2. Wear Layer Thickness

The wear layer is the clear protective coating on top. It is measured in mils (thousandths of an inch), not millimeters. A thicker wear layer means better scratch and stain resistance.

- **6 mil**: Suitable for low-traffic residential spaces. Fine for a guest bedroom.
- **12 mil**: Good for most residential rooms.
- **20 mil**: Best for high-traffic areas, homes with pets, or light commercial use.
- **30 mil or more**: Commercial-grade. Usually overkill for homes but available in premium products.

### 3. Core Type

**WPC (Wood Plastic Composite)**: Has a foamed core that feels slightly cushioned. Better sound insulation. More forgiving over imperfect subfloors. Generally $2.50 to $5.00 per square foot in materials.

**SPC (Stone Plastic Composite)**: Denser and more rigid. Better for temperature-variable spaces like sunrooms or garages. More dimensionally stable. Typically $2.00 to $4.50 per square foot.

### 5. Subfloor Condition

If your subfloor needs patching, leveling, or repairs before installation, that adds cost. Installers typically charge $1 to $3 per square foot for subfloor leveling compound. Severely damaged plywood subfloors may need partial or full replacement, adding $3 to $8 per square foot.

Concrete subfloors can have moisture issues. A moisture barrier or self-leveling compound may be needed, adding $0.50 to $2.00 per square foot.

### 6. Labor and Geographic Location

Labor rates vary significantly across the US. Expect to pay:

- **Southeast and Midwest**: $1.00 to $2.00 per square foot for installation
- **Mid-Atlantic and Midwest urban areas**: $1.50 to $3.00 per square foot
- **West Coast and Northeast**: $2.50 to $4.00 per square foot
- **Major metro areas (NYC, LA, SF, Seattle)**: $3.50 to $5.00 per square foot

Contractors usually have a minimum charge of $200 to $500 even for small rooms, so tiny bathroom projects often hit the minimum rather than the per-square-foot rate.

## DIY vs. Hiring a Pro

This is one of the more DIY-friendly flooring projects out there, but it is not without challenges.

**DIY is a good fit if you:**
- Have a simple rectangular room with minimal obstacles
- Are comfortable using a miter saw, utility knife, or jigsaw
- Are patient enough to carefully prep your subfloor
- Want to save $1,000 to $3,000 on a medium-sized project

**Hire a pro if you:**
- Have lots of doorways, transitions, built-ins, or irregular room shapes
- Are installing on a staircase (this is genuinely difficult and injury risks exist)
- Have an unlevel subfloor that needs professional attention
- Are working under a tight timeline, like a renovation before a move-in

A licensed flooring installer can typically do 500 to 800 square feet in a single day. If you DIY, plan for a full weekend for a medium-sized room, including subfloor prep and trim work.

## What to Expect on Installation Day

If you hire a professional installer, here is the typical process:

1. **Subfloor inspection and prep**: They check for high spots, low spots, squeaks, and moisture. This may add time and cost.
2. **Acclimation**: Many installers bring planks in and let them sit for 24 to 48 hours so they adjust to your home's temperature and humidity.
3. **First row installation**: The planks click together with the longest wall as a reference point. Spacers maintain a gap along walls to allow for expansion.
4. **Main installation**: Rows click into each other diagonally and snap flat.
5. **Transitions and trim**: Thresholds between rooms, T-moldings at doorways, and quarter-round trim along baseboards are installed last.
6. **Cleanup**: A good installer sweeps and hauls away packaging.

## Hidden Costs to Budget For

People often forget to account for these add-on costs:

- **Underlayment**: Unless it is built into the plank, you may need a foam or cork underlayment pad. Budget $0.25 to $0.75 per square foot.
- **Transition strips**: Each doorway or room transition needs a strip. These cost $10 to $30 each and you may need 6 to 10 in a typical home.
- **Baseboard removal and reinstall**: Some installers charge $1 to $2 per linear foot to remove and reinstall baseboards.
- **Furniture moving**: Installers often charge $25 to $75 per room to move heavy furniture, or you can do it yourself.
- **Old floor removal**: Removing carpet and pad runs $0.50 to $1.50 per square foot. Removing tile or glued-down vinyl can cost $1.50 to $3.00 per square foot.
- **Stair nosing**: If you are doing a staircase, nosing pieces cost $20 to $50 each per step.

A full project budget for a 1,200 sq ft home replacing old carpet with mid-grade LVP might look like:

- LVP materials at $2.50/sq ft: $3,000
- 10% overage: $300
- Underlayment at $0.40/sq ft: $480
- Labor at $1.75/sq ft: $2,100
- Carpet removal at $0.75/sq ft: $900
- Transition strips (10 pieces): $200
- Baseboard reinstall: $300
- **Total estimate: $7,280**

## Questions to Ask Before Hiring a Flooring Contractor

Before signing anything, ask these questions:

1. Do you carry liability insurance and workers' compensation? Ask to see the certificate.
2. Will you be doing the work yourself, or subcontracting it?
3. Does your quote include subfloor prep, or is that extra?
4. What happens if you find moisture or damage under the old floor?
5. What warranty do you provide on the installation? (Materials have their own manufacturer warranty.)
6. How long will the project take, and will my home be livable during it?
7. Do you provide the underlayment, or do I need to purchase it separately?

## Health and Safety Notes

LVP manufacturing has historically involved concerns about volatile organic compound (VOC) emissions, particularly formaldehyde. The Consumer Product Safety Commission and EPA have set standards for acceptable levels.

When shopping, look for products certified to:
- **GREENGUARD Gold certification**: Indicates low chemical emissions, suitable for sensitive environments including schools and healthcare
- **FloorScore certification**: Industry standard for indoor air quality compliance

After installation, ventilate your home for 48 to 72 hours. Open windows and run fans. If anyone in your household has asthma or chemical sensitivities, give it a full week before spending extended time in freshly floored rooms.

## Warranty: What to Look For

Most LVP products offer:

- **Wear warranty**: Ranges from 10 years on budget products to lifetime residential on premium lines
- **Waterproof warranty**: Covers the core, not typically the seams
- **Fade warranty**: Important if the floor gets direct sunlight
- **Structural warranty**: Covers the click-lock system

Read the fine print. Many warranties require professional installation to remain valid. Warranties also typically exclude damage from flooding, pet accidents left unaddressed for extended periods, or improper cleaning products.
