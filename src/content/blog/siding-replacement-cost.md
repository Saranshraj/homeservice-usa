Replacing the siding on your home costs between **$7,500 and $22,000** for most American homeowners in 2026, with the national average landing around **$12,000 to $14,000** for a standard 1,500 to 2,000 sq ft two-story home. Vinyl siding is the budget-friendly option at **$3 to $10 per square foot installed**, while fiber cement (like James Hardie board) runs **$7 to $15 per square foot**, and brick or stone veneer can reach **$20 to $35 per square foot**. If your siding is cracked, warped, or letting moisture into your walls, this is one home repair where procrastinating really does compound the damage. The good news: insulated vinyl siding qualifies for a **30% federal tax credit up to $1,200** under the Inflation Reduction Act.

## What Drives the Cost of Siding Replacement

The two biggest factors in your final bill are the size of your home and the material you choose. But several others matter a great deal.

### Home Size and Shape

Contractors price siding by the square foot of wall surface, not the square footage of your floor plan. A one-story ranch with 1,500 sq ft of living space might have only 1,200 to 1,400 sq ft of exterior wall, while a two-story colonial of the same footprint could have 2,400 to 2,800 sq ft of wall. Homes with multiple gables, dormers, bay windows, or decorative trim take more labor and material to cover, so expect 10 to 20 percent higher costs on complex architecture.

### Removing the Old Siding

Tear-off and disposal of your existing siding adds **$1,000 to $3,000** to most projects. Some contractors can install new siding directly over old in certain situations, but building codes in most states now require removal so inspectors can check the sheathing and moisture barrier underneath. Skipping this step might save money today and cost you far more when hidden rot spreads.

**If your home was built before 1978:** Old siding may contain lead paint. Federal law requires contractors to follow EPA lead-safe work practices under the [Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting Rule](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program). This adds cost, but it is not optional and it protects your family.

### Sheathing and Moisture Barrier

Once the old siding comes off, your contractor will inspect the sheathing (the plywood or OSB panel layer underneath). If there is rot, water damage, or pest damage, repairs run **$3 to $8 per square foot** for the affected sections. Budget an extra **$500 to $2,500** as a contingency for this, especially on homes over 20 years old or in high-humidity climates like Florida, Louisiana, or the Pacific Northwest.

A quality house wrap or moisture barrier (products like Tyvek or similar) should be included in any full siding replacement. If your contractor does not mention it, ask.

### Labor Rates by Region

Labor is typically 40 to 60 percent of the total bill, and it varies dramatically by state.

## Siding Material Breakdown: What You Are Actually Getting

### Vinyl Siding

Vinyl is by far the most popular siding material in the United States, covering roughly 30 percent of single-family homes. It is affordable, low-maintenance, and comes in hundreds of colors and profiles. Standard vinyl runs **$3 to $7 per square foot installed**, and it does not require painting.

Insulated vinyl adds a layer of rigid foam backing that improves energy efficiency and reduces dents. It costs more at **$5 to $10 per square foot**, but that insulated version is what qualifies for the federal tax credit (more on that below).

Vinyl is not a great option in areas with extreme temperature swings, as it can crack in prolonged cold or warp in intense heat. If you are in Phoenix or Minneapolis, ask your contractor specifically about temperature-rated products.

### Fiber Cement Siding

Fiber cement, dominated by the James Hardie brand, has become the premium choice for US homeowners who want the look of wood without the maintenance headaches. It is made from cement, sand, and cellulose fibers, and it resists rot, insects, and fire in ways wood cannot match.

Fiber cement runs **$7 to $15 per square foot installed**, with the higher end reflecting two-coat factory painting (HardieColorPlus), detailed trim work, and complex home profiles. It is heavier than vinyl, which means more labor and specialized fasteners.

The [James Hardie website](https://www.jameshardie.com) and your local siding contractor can both pull regional pricing guides. Fiber cement is worth the premium on homes in humid coastal or rainy climates where rot is a real risk.

### Engineered Wood Siding

Engineered wood products like LP SmartSide combine wood strands with resin binders and a zinc-borate treatment to resist moisture and insects. It looks and feels more like real wood than vinyl, costs less than fiber cement, and is easier to cut and install.

Expect to pay **$5 to $12 per square foot installed**. Engineered wood holds paint well and comes pre-primed from the factory. It is a solid middle-ground choice for most homeowners outside of extremely wet climates.

### Natural Wood Siding

Cedar clapboard, pine shiplap, and redwood siding are beautiful, but they demand real maintenance. You will need to repaint or restain every 3 to 7 years, and any missed maintenance opens the door to rot and insect damage. Wood siding costs **$7 to $14 per square foot installed** and requires a bigger time and money commitment over its lifetime.

That said, if you live in a historic district or simply love the look and feel of real wood, no other material matches it. Just go in with eyes open about the upkeep.

### Metal Siding

Steel and aluminum siding have made a strong comeback in contemporary and modern home design. Metal is extremely durable, fire-resistant, and can last 40 to 50 years with minimal maintenance. Steel siding costs **$7 to $12 per square foot installed**, while aluminum runs slightly lower at **$6 to $10**.

Metal dents more easily than fiber cement, and horizontal profiles can show fasteners more visibly than other materials. It is an excellent choice for high-wind areas and homes in the western wildland-urban interface where fire resistance matters.

## The Federal Tax Credit: Do Not Leave Money Behind

If you are replacing your siding and considering the upgrade to **insulated vinyl or other insulation improvements**, you may qualify for a significant federal tax credit.

Under the [Inflation Reduction Act (IRA)](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/energy-efficient-home-improvement-credit), the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit covers 30 percent of the cost of qualifying insulation and air sealing materials, up to **$1,200 per year**. Insulated vinyl siding products that meet specific R-value requirements can qualify.

Key rules to know:

- The credit applies to your **primary residence** only. Rental properties and second homes do not qualify.
- The 30 percent credit applies to **materials only**, not installation labor.
- The $1,200 cap is annual, meaning you can potentially claim it again in future years if you make additional improvements.
- You will need to file **IRS Form 5695** with your tax return for the year the work was completed.

Check the [ENERGY STAR Rebate Finder](https://www.energystar.gov/rebate-finder) for your state, as many utilities and state programs stack additional rebates on top of the federal credit. Some states offer **$500 to $2,500 in additional rebates** for specific siding products.

## Signs You Need New Siding Now (Not Next Year)

A lot of homeowners put off siding replacement because it feels optional. It is not always optional. Here is what pushes it from "eventually" to "now":

**Warping or buckling:** Siding that bows outward or shows visible waves has lost its seal against moisture. Water is getting behind it.

**Cracks or gaps:** Any visible cracking, especially horizontal cracks in lap siding, is an entry point for rain, pests, and cold air. Gaps at corners or around windows are equally urgent.

**Fading that will not respond to cleaning:** Some color loss is cosmetic. But chalky, heavily faded siding is often brittle underneath and past the end of its service life.

**High heating and cooling bills:** If your energy costs have crept up and your HVAC equipment is in good shape, the culprit may be your home's envelope. Old or damaged siding with missing moisture barriers dramatically reduces insulation effectiveness. The [Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/weatherize/air-sealing-your-home) estimates that air sealing and insulation upgrades can cut heating and cooling costs by 10 to 20 percent.

**Bubbling or peeling paint:** Paint should not bubble on siding unless moisture is trapped behind it. This is a strong indicator of a water infiltration problem that will only get worse.

**Mold, mildew, or rot:** Surface mold can sometimes be cleaned. Mold that returns quickly, or soft spots in wood trim and corners, means moisture has penetrated the sheathing. Get a contractor out immediately.

## How to Get Quotes Without Getting Burned

Siding is one of the more common home improvement categories for contractor fraud. Here is what to do before you sign anything.

**Get three quotes in writing.** Prices can vary by 30 to 50 percent for the same job in the same neighborhood. Any contractor who pushes you to sign the same day is a red flag.

**Verify the license.** Every state requires siding contractors to hold a contractor's license. Check your [state's contractor licensing board](https://www.contractors-license.org) or search through the [National Association of Home Builders](https://www.nahb.org) to verify. An unlicensed contractor doing work that requires permits leaves you with no legal recourse and no valid homeowner's insurance claim if something goes wrong.

**Understand what is included.** Ask each contractor whether the quote includes: tear-off and disposal of old siding, moisture barrier replacement, sheathing repair allowance, all trim and corner pieces, and cleanup. Quotes that look cheap often exclude these items.

**Never pay more than 30 percent upfront.** A standard payment schedule is 30 percent at signing, 30 to 40 percent at material delivery, and the balance at completion after your walkthrough. Any contractor demanding more than half the total before work starts should raise a concern.

**Check for manufacturer certification.** James Hardie, LP SmartSide, and other major manufacturers certify contractors who have completed their installation training. These contractors are more likely to install products to spec and your warranty stays intact.

## DIY Siding: Realistic Assessment

Siding installation is physically demanding and more technically complex than it looks on YouTube. You need to manage proper overlap, flashing around windows and doors, corner trim, and consistent fastener spacing to prevent warping. Mistakes create water infiltration problems that take years to show up and cost far more to fix than the original installation.

That said, small sections on a single-story home are within reach for a handy homeowner with carpentry experience. The [Family Handyman](https://www.familyhandyman.com) has solid guides for vinyl siding repair and partial replacement.

**For a full replacement, hire a licensed professional.** The labor savings are real but so are the risks, and most manufacturer warranties are void without professional installation.
