**The short answer:** Professional bathtub refinishing costs between **$335 and $650** for a standard white or off-white tub in most US markets. Color changes or custom finishes push that to **$500 to $900**, and walk-in or jetted tub refinishing can run **$650 to $1,200**. Replacing a bathtub entirely costs **$1,200 to $5,500** including demolition and installation, which is why refinishing makes strong financial sense for tubs in structurally sound condition.

If your tub looks dingy, scratched, or just plain dated, refinishing can transform it for a fraction of the replacement cost. This guide walks you through realistic prices, how long the results last, what situations call for full replacement instead, and how to pick a contractor who will not leave you with a peeling, bubbling mess two years later.

Here is a quick breakdown of typical refinishing costs by tub type and scope in 2026:

| Color change (any non-white color) | $665 | $500 to $900 |
| Jetted or whirlpool tub | $825 | $650 to $1,200 |
| Walk-in tub | $900 | $700 to $1,200 |

These prices cover professional labor and materials combined. Most jobs take three to five hours for the refinishing itself, plus a 24-hour cure time before the tub can get wet again.

## Refinishing vs. Replacement: The Real Numbers

This is the decision most homeowners are actually wrestling with. Here is how the math looks side by side:

| Factor | Refinishing | Replacement |
|---|---|---|
| Total cost | $335 to $900 | $1,200 to $5,500 |
| Time to complete | Half a day plus 24 hours cure | 2 to 5 days |
| Lifespan of results | 5 to 15 years | 20 to 30 years |
| Mess and demolition | Minimal | Significant; drywall and tile often damaged |
| Rental or flip projects | Excellent choice | Usually overkill |
| Old home with cast iron tub | Strong candidate | Losing a valuable asset |
| Structural damage or cracks | Not appropriate | Necessary |
| Fiberglass tub over 30 years old | May not bond well | Better option |

The math is simple: refinishing costs roughly 10 to 25 percent of a full replacement. For a rental property, a home you plan to sell within five years, or a tub that is structurally solid but cosmetically rough, refinishing is almost always the better financial move.

A full replacement means tearing out the surrounding tile, which usually gets destroyed in the process, cutting into drywall, possibly rerouting plumbing, and then retiling everything around a new unit. That is why [bathroom remodel costs](/blog/bathroom-remodel-cost/) escalate quickly once you start pulling things apart.

## What Drives Refinishing Costs Up or Down

### Tub Material and Condition

Porcelain-on-cast-iron and porcelain-on-steel tubs hold refinishing well and tend to last longer between treatments. Fiberglass tubs can be refinished, but older, oxidized fiberglass may not bond as reliably, and the technician will need to do more prep work.

Existing chips, cracks, or rust spots need to be repaired before refinishing. Each repair adds $75 to $200 to the job. A tub with four or five chips is still a good candidate. A tub with spalling all the way through or hairline cracks in the porcelain is not.

### Color and Finish

Standard white, almond, and bone are the cheapest to refinish because the products are stocked in volume. Custom colors require special tinting or multiple coats, which adds time and materials cost. A vivid color change from harvest gold or avocado green to white is one of the most common reasons people refinish, and most contractors do it regularly for $500 to $800 total.

### Size and Configuration

Oversized soaking tubs, clawfoot tubs, and walk-in tubs require more coating material and more labor time. Jetted tubs need careful masking of the jets and sometimes jet body cleaning beforehand, which adds to the quote.

### Geographic Location

Labor rates vary significantly across the US. The Northeast and West Coast tend to run 15 to 30 percent above the national average, while the South and Midwest tend to run slightly below. See the state pricing section below for regional ranges.

### Ventilation and Access

Refinishing involves strong chemicals, including etching acids and spray coatings that release volatile organic compounds (VOCs). Contractors must ventilate the space properly. If your bathroom has poor ventilation, the job takes longer and requires more equipment. This rarely changes the quoted price dramatically, but it can affect whether a contractor agrees to do a basement bathroom or a poorly ventilated space.

## How Long Does Refinishing Last?

This is where homeowner expectations often differ from reality. A professional refinishing job typically lasts **5 to 10 years** with proper care. Some last longer; some start peeling within two years. The difference usually comes down to three factors.

**Product quality.** There is a significant difference between industrial-grade two-part polyurethane topcoats used by professional services and the single-part coatings found in DIY kits. Professional coatings bond to the substrate much more aggressively.

**Surface preparation.** Acid etching, sanding, and degreasing before application determine how well the new finish adheres. This is where shortcuts by low-bid contractors cause premature failure.

**Your maintenance habits.** Abrasive cleaners, scrubbing pads, and harsh chemicals will degrade a refinished surface faster than a factory finish. Using mild, non-abrasive cleaners and avoiding anything labeled "heavy duty" or "scratch-free" makes the finish last significantly longer.

For comparison: a properly cared-for refinished tub should comfortably reach the 8-to-10-year mark before it needs to be redone or replaced. That is a reasonable lifespan for a $400 to $600 investment.

## DIY Refinishing Kits: Are They Worth It?

Hardware stores and home centers carry refinishing kits from brands like Rust-Oleum and Homax for $30 to $80. These are tempting for their low cost, but most professional contractors and most homeowners who have tried them are skeptical.

The limitations are real. Single-part coatings do not bond as well as the two-part systems professionals use. The spray application in a kit rarely achieves the smooth, uniform finish of professional spray equipment. Without the acid etch prep step, most DIY refinishing begins showing wear within 18 to 36 months.

If your situation involves a rental unit you plan to sell within two years, or you just need a short-term cosmetic improvement on a tight budget, a DIY kit can work temporarily. For a primary home where you want results that last, professional refinishing is worth the investment.

## When Refinishing Is NOT the Right Call

Not every tub is a good candidate for refinishing. Here are the situations where replacement is the smarter move.

**Structural cracks or holes.** Surface chips can be filled, but cracks that run through the tub wall or pan indicate structural failure. A refinished coating over a cracked tub will flex and peel, and the crack will continue to grow.

**Fiberglass that has been previously refinished multiple times.** Each coat adds a layer, and by the third or fourth refinishing, the surface can become too thick and brittle to hold another coat properly. A good contractor will tell you this honestly. A dishonest one will take your money and apply a coat that fails within a year or two.

**Active mold or water damage under the tub.** If you have soft spots in the floor around the tub or visible mold growing under the tub ledge, refinishing is putting a cosmetic fix on a deeper problem. Fix the water issue first. You may need [mold remediation](/blog/mold-remediation-cost/) before any cosmetic work makes sense.

**Homes built before 1978.** If your home was built before 1978, surfaces near the tub may have been painted with lead-based paint. The [EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting program](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program) requires certified contractors to follow specific lead-safe work practices. Make sure any contractor you hire holds EPA RRP certification if there is any possibility of lead paint exposure during prep work.

**You want a fundamentally different tub.** Refinishing changes the surface color and texture. It cannot change the tub's size, depth, shape, or configuration. If you want to convert to a walk-in tub for accessibility or to a walk-in shower, refinishing is not part of that project.

## How to Find a Reputable Refinishing Contractor

This is an industry with both skilled craftspeople and fly-by-night operators. Corners are easy to cut, and the consequences show up two years later when your new finish is peeling off in chunks. Here is what to look for, building on the guidance in our [how to find a good contractor](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/) guide.

**Ask about the products they use.** Professional-grade two-part polyurethane coatings outperform generic single-component products by a wide margin. If a contractor cannot tell you what brand of coating they use, that is a yellow flag.

**Ask about the prep process.** A proper refinishing job includes cleaning and degreasing, acid etching or mechanical roughening, filling any chips, and masking before spray application. A job that skips the etching step will peel sooner.

**Get a written warranty.** Reputable refinishers offer a one-year to five-year warranty on labor and materials. A warranty is only as good as the company's staying power, so ask how long they have been in business.

**Check for licensing and insurance.** Most states require contractors to carry liability insurance. Some states have specific licensing requirements for refinishing contractors. Your state contractor licensing board can verify this.

**Read recent reviews, especially negative ones.** Look for reviews mentioning peeling within one to two years. Those are the most telling signs of poor prep work, and they reveal far more about a contractor than a glowing five-star review.

## The Bottom Line

Bathtub refinishing is one of the highest-value cosmetic improvements you can make to a bathroom. At $335 to $650 for most standard jobs, it costs a fraction of replacement and delivers a clean, updated look that can last a decade when done right. The key is hiring a contractor who uses quality coatings and does not skip the prep work.

If your tub is structurally sound, has no active leaks or mold issues, and you are not fundamentally changing your bathroom layout, refinishing is almost always the right financial call. If your tub is cracked, repeatedly refinished, or sitting on top of water damage, put that budget toward a proper replacement instead.
