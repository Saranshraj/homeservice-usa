**The short answer:** Kitchen cabinet painting costs between **$1,200 and $7,000** for most US homeowners in 2026, with the average full kitchen running **$2,500 to $4,500** when hiring a professional painter. A standard 10x10 kitchen with 20 to 30 linear feet of cabinets typically costs **$3,000 to $3,800** for a spray-applied professional finish, including labor, primer, paint, and hardware reinstallation. At roughly 10 to 15 percent of what new cabinets would cost, painting is one of the highest-return upgrades you can make to a kitchen.

If your kitchen feels dated but a full remodel is not in the budget right now, cabinet painting is worth a serious look. It can take a 1990s oak kitchen to something that looks current in three to five days, with almost none of the mess and disruption of a renovation. This guide covers what you will actually pay, what makes prices swing up or down, and how to tell a professional job from a sloppy one that will peel in a year.

## Average Kitchen Cabinet Painting Costs by Kitchen Size

The single biggest driver of cost is how many cabinet doors and drawers you have. Most contractors price by linear foot or by door count, so larger kitchens cost more, almost always.

| Kitchen Size | Linear Feet | Number of Doors | Professional Cost |
|---|---|---|---|
| Small (galley or apartment) | 10-15 ft | 10-15 doors | $1,200 to $2,500 |
| Medium (10x10 standard) | 20-30 ft | 20-30 doors | $2,500 to $4,500 |
| Large (open-concept or L-shape) | 30-45 ft | 30-45 doors | $4,500 to $6,500 |
| Extra-large (chef's kitchen, island) | 45+ ft | 45+ doors | $6,500 to $9,000+ |

Most single-family homes in the US fall in the medium category. If your kitchen is from a standard production builder, the 10x10 benchmark is a reliable starting point.

## Professional Painting Methods: Spray vs. Brush and Roll

How your cabinets are painted matters almost as much as the paint itself. Professional painters typically use one of two methods, and the choice affects both the cost and the final appearance significantly.

| Method | Cost Range | Finish Quality | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Spray application (off-site or in-home) | $2,500 to $7,000 | Factory-smooth, no brush marks | Most kitchens, visible grain-free finish |
| Brush and roll (on-site) | $1,200 to $4,000 | Slight texture, visible strokes possible | Budget jobs, textured wood look acceptable |
| DIY brush and roll | $150 to $400 (materials only) | Variable; often shows brush marks | Confident DIYers with time and patience |

Spray application almost always produces a better result because the atomized paint settles evenly and dries without brush marks or lap lines. Many professional cabinet painters remove the doors, take them to a shop, spray them flat, and reinstall them. This is the gold standard, and you should expect to pay for it.

## What Is Included in a Professional Cabinet Painting Quote?

Before you compare quotes, make sure you know what each one covers. Reputable contractors should include all of these in a full-service job:

- Full cleaning and degreasing of all cabinet surfaces
- Sanding and scuff prep so paint adheres properly
- High-quality primer (oil-based or bonding primer for problem surfaces)
- Two finish coats of professional-grade paint
- Removal and reinstallation of all doors and hardware
- Light caulking at seams and gaps where needed
- Basic touch-up within 30 days of completion

What is usually **not** included:

- New hardware (hinges, pulls, knobs): budget $100 to $500 separately
- Cabinet interior painting (most jobs only paint the exterior faces and door fronts)
- Repairs to damaged cabinet boxes or frames
- Painting the kitchen ceiling or walls

If a quote seems unusually low, ask what prep steps are included. Skipping degreasing and proper priming is the most common reason painted cabinets peel within a year or two.

## Cabinet Painting vs. Refacing vs. Replacing: A True Cost Comparison

This is the question most homeowners come to this page asking. Here is an honest side-by-side comparison:

| Option | Typical Cost Range | Lifespan | Disruption | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Painting | $1,200 to $7,000 | 8 to 15 years (with quality prep) | 3 to 5 days | Good cabinet boxes, cosmetic update |
| Refacing (new doors + veneer) | $4,500 to $12,000 | 15 to 20 years | 3 to 5 days | Solid cabinet boxes, new look, new hardware |
| Full replacement | $15,000 to $45,000+ | 20 to 30+ years | 1 to 3 weeks | Damaged boxes, layout change, gut remodel |

Painting makes the most sense when your cabinet boxes and frames are structurally sound and the existing doors are flat (not raised-panel profiles with intricate detail that is difficult to spray evenly). Refacing is better if you want new door styles. Full replacement is the right call when your boxes are warped, damaged, or you want to completely change the layout. If you are already planning a [kitchen remodel](/blog/kitchen-remodel-cost/), factor all three into your planning.

## What Drives the Price Up or Down

Several factors can push a quote well above or below the averages above:

**Cabinet material.** Solid wood cabinets paint beautifully. MDF (medium-density fiberboard) also takes paint well, though edges can swell if not sealed properly. Laminate and thermofoil cabinets require special adhesion primer and may cost 10 to 20 percent more because of the extra prep.

**Current cabinet condition.** Heavily greased cabinets near a stove, cabinets with old layers of paint or stain, or surfaces with chips and dings require more prep time, which raises labor costs.

**Paint quality.** Contractors use professional-grade paints that are not available at retail stores. Benjamin Moore Advance, Sherwin-Williams Emerald Urethane Trim Enamel, and similar alkyd-urethane hybrid paints are the industry standard. Cheaper paint means cheaper results.

**Your region.** Labor costs vary significantly across the US. The Pacific Coast, New England, and major metros on both coasts tend to run 20 to 35 percent higher than the national average. The Southeast and Midwest are often at or below average.

**Number of coats and colors.** Going from a dark stain to a bright white requires an extra coat of high-hide primer. Dramatically changing colors also takes additional coats to achieve full coverage.

## DIY Cabinet Painting: Is It Worth It?

Honest answer: cabinet painting is one of the harder DIY painting projects, not one of the easier ones. The results are very visible in a high-traffic area, and mistakes like brush marks, drips, or poor adhesion are hard to fix after the fact.

**DIY material costs for a medium kitchen:**
- Cleaner and degreaser (TSP substitute or Krud Kutter): $15 to $30
- Sandpaper and sanding sponges: $20 to $40
- Bonding primer (1 to 2 gallons): $50 to $100
- Finish paint (2 to 3 gallons): $80 to $150
- Brushes, foam rollers, and sprayer (if renting): $50 to $200
- Painter's tape and drop cloths: $30 to $50
- **Total materials: $250 to $570**

**Time commitment:** Plan for two to three weekends, minimum. Proper prep alone takes a full day. Doors need to dry flat between coats. Rushing produces poor results.

If you are comfortable with a careful brush-and-roller approach and you have done finish painting before, DIY is a legitimate option that can save $2,000 or more. But if you have never painted cabinets, one bad coat on 25 doors is a lot of regret to sand out. The [Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov) also recommends proper ventilation when using oil-based or alkyd-urethane paints, which are the best-performing options for cabinets.

## How Long Does Cabinet Paint Last?

A professionally painted kitchen with proper prep and quality materials should last **8 to 15 years** before needing a full repaint. Some kitchens hold up even longer with careful use and occasional touch-ups.

Factors that shorten paint life:
- Cooking grease and steam (high-BTU gas ranges are the worst offenders)
- Harsh cleaning products (bleach-based sprays strip paint over time)
- Doors that slam instead of close softly
- No topcoat or sealer applied over the finish coat

Factors that extend paint life:
- Cabinet doors with soft-close hinges
- Using a damp cloth instead of spray cleaners
- Annual inspection and touching up any chips or dings before they spread

If the paint job was done correctly, touching up chips is simple and the repair is nearly invisible.

## How to Vet a Cabinet Painter

Cabinet painting is a specialty. Not every interior painter does excellent cabinet work, and the difference shows immediately. Here is how to evaluate contractors before signing anything:

**Ask to see photos of completed kitchen projects**, not just rooms or trim work. Cabinet work is its own discipline.

**Ask about their prep process.** A professional should mention degreasing, sanding, and priming unprompted. If they say they will just wipe down and paint, walk away.

**Ask what paint they use.** Professional painters should be able to name the product. Benjamin Moore Advance, Sherwin-Williams Emerald Urethane Trim Enamel, and Cabot Problem Solver are common professional choices.

**Ask whether doors are sprayed in-shop or in-home.** Either can work, but in-shop spray jobs typically produce a cleaner finish.

**Check state licensing.** Most states require painters to carry a contractor's license and liability insurance. Verify through your state contractor licensing board before signing. The [National Association of Home Builders](https://www.nahb.org) maintains a directory of licensed contractors by state.

**Get at least three quotes.** Cabinet painting quotes vary widely. Three quotes let you identify outliers, both suspiciously low and unreasonably high.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I paint over stained cabinets?**
Yes, but it takes more prep. Stained wood needs thorough sanding and a high-quality bonding primer to ensure the finish paint adheres and does not peel. Oil-based stains especially require an oil-based or shellac-based primer before any water-based topcoat.

**Should I paint cabinet boxes (the frames) and interiors too?**
Most homeowners only paint the exterior-facing surfaces: door fronts, drawer fronts, and the exposed face frames. Painting interiors adds cost and time but is rarely necessary unless the interior finish is damaged or very dark.

**Can I change the cabinet color to white if they are currently dark?**
Yes. Going dark to light is common. It requires extra coats of a high-hide primer and possibly two finish coats, which adds $200 to $500 to the project cost.

**Do painted cabinets look cheap?**
When done professionally with quality materials, no. Painted cabinets are standard in designer kitchens at every price point. The result depends almost entirely on prep quality and paint quality, not on the fact of painting itself.
