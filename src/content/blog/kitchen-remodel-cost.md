A kitchen remodel costs between **$5,000 and $75,000** for most US homeowners in 2026, depending on the scope of work. A minor cosmetic refresh (new paint, hardware, lighting) runs **$5,000 to $15,000**. A mid-range remodel with new cabinets, countertops, and flooring averages **$25,000 to $45,000**. A full gut renovation that changes the layout, moves plumbing, and installs custom cabinetry can easily reach **$60,000 to $100,000** or more in high-cost cities. The national average for a full kitchen remodel sits around **$27,000 to $35,000**, according to the [National Association of Home Builders](https://www.nahb.org). That number sounds scary, but the good news is that kitchens return 60 to 80 cents on every dollar you spend when you sell your home.

Many homeowners plan a kitchen remodel alongside a [bathroom remodel](/blog/bathroom-remodel-cost/) to save on contractor mobilization costs, and [new hardwood flooring](/blog/hardwood-floor-installation-cost/) is a common upgrade to bundle in at the same time. Before signing with any contractor, read our [guide to vetting contractors](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/), since kitchen remodels are one of the most common sources of homeowner-contractor disputes.

## Why Kitchen Remodels Cost What They Do

Your kitchen is the most complex room in the house. It has plumbing, electrical, gas lines, ventilation, and cabinetry that requires precision carpentry. When you remodel a bedroom, you are mostly dealing with paint and flooring. When you remodel a kitchen, you are often dealing with six or seven different licensed trades. That complexity is why costs add up so fast, and why it is so important to understand exactly what you are buying before you sign any contract.

## Kitchen Remodel Cost by Scope

Here is an honest breakdown of what each level of remodel costs in 2026:

| Remodel Level | What's Included | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|---|
| Minor / Cosmetic | Paint, hardware, lighting, minor fixes | $5,000 to $15,000 |
| Mid-Range | New cabinets, countertops, appliances, flooring | $20,000 to $45,000 |
| Major / Full Remodel | Layout changes, new plumbing, electrical, custom work | $40,000 to $80,000 |
| Luxury | Custom cabinets, high-end finishes, structural changes | $80,000 to $150,000+ |

The single biggest factor driving scope costs: **are you moving any walls, plumbing, or electrical?** Moving a sink or island three feet in any direction can add $2,000 to $5,000 to your bill. Opening up a wall between the kitchen and dining room often adds $3,000 to $10,000 or more depending on whether it is load-bearing.

Let's look at where the money actually goes in a mid-range kitchen remodel. Most contractors use these percentages as rough guides, though your specific project will vary:

| Labor (installation) | 20 to 35% | $5,000 to $18,000 |
| Countertops | 10 to 15% | $2,000 to $7,000 |
| Appliances | 10 to 20% | $2,500 to $12,000 |
| Flooring | 7% | $1,500 to $5,000 |
| Plumbing | 4 to 7% | $1,000 to $4,000 |

| Painting | 3 to 5% | $500 to $1,500 |

### Cabinets: $4,000 to $25,000+

Cabinets typically eat the largest single chunk of your kitchen budget, and the price range is enormous. Stock cabinets from big-box stores like Home Depot or IKEA run **$100 to $300 per linear foot**. Semi-custom cabinets (available in more sizes and finishes) run **$200 to $600 per linear foot**. Fully custom cabinets built by a local cabinet shop can reach **$500 to $1,500+ per linear foot**.

For a typical 10-by-12-foot kitchen with 25 linear feet of cabinetry, that works out to:
- **Stock cabinets installed:** $4,000 to $8,000
- **Semi-custom installed:** $8,000 to $18,000
- **Full custom installed:** $15,000 to $40,000

If your budget is tight, refacing existing cabinets (replacing doors and drawer fronts while keeping the boxes) runs **$1,500 to $9,000** and gives you most of the visual impact at a fraction of the cost.

### Countertops: $1,500 to $8,000+

Material choice drives countertop cost more than anything else:

| Material | Cost per Square Foot (Installed) |
|---|---|
| Laminate | $15 to $40 |
| Tile | $20 to $55 |
| Butcher block | $30 to $70 |
| Solid surface (Corian) | $45 to $85 |
| Quartz | $60 to $120 |
| Granite | $60 to $130 |
| Marble | $80 to $180 |
| Quartzite | $70 to $150 |

For a 30-square-foot kitchen countertop (a standard small-to-mid kitchen), quartz or granite will run **$1,800 to $3,900 installed**. Marble and quartzite push into the **$2,400 to $5,400** range.

Quartz is the most popular choice in 2026 because it is non-porous, does not need sealing, and resists stains and scratches better than granite. Granite still has a loyal following for its natural variation and slightly lower price.

### Appliances: $2,500 to $20,000+

New appliances are often what make a kitchen feel brand new, but the range here is staggering. A basic appliance package (refrigerator, range, dishwasher, microwave) from a mid-tier brand like Whirlpool or LG runs **$3,000 to $7,000**. Professional-grade or luxury brands like Wolf, Sub-Zero, or Thermador can push **$15,000 to $30,000** for the same set.

ENERGY STAR certified appliances earn you a federal tax credit of up to **$840 total** under the Inflation Reduction Act. That breaks down as up to $840 for a heat pump clothes dryer and up to $840 for a heat pump washer. Check the [ENERGY STAR website](https://www.energystar.gov) for the current list of qualifying appliances and rebate amounts in your state. Many utilities layer additional rebates on top of the federal credit, so the savings can be meaningful.

### Flooring: $1,500 to $7,000

Kitchen flooring needs to be durable, waterproof, and easy to clean. The most popular options:

| Flooring Type | Cost per Square Foot (Installed) |
|---|---|
| Sheet vinyl / LVP | $3 to $8 |
| Porcelain tile | $7 to $18 |
| Hardwood | $10 to $20 |
| Natural stone | $15 to $35 |

Luxury vinyl plank (LVP) has become the dominant kitchen flooring choice in the US because it is waterproof, comfortable underfoot, easy to install, and affordable. For a 150-square-foot kitchen, LVP runs **$450 to $1,200** for the material alone. Professional installation adds **$1 to $3 per square foot**.

### Plumbing and Electrical: $1,800 to $7,000 combined

If you are not moving any plumbing or electrical, costs are relatively modest: **$500 to $1,500** for a new sink and faucet installation, and **$500 to $1,200** to add outlets or upgrade lighting circuits.

But if you are moving the sink, adding a kitchen island with a sink, or upgrading from a 30-amp to a 50-amp circuit for a new induction range, costs climb. Moving plumbing typically runs **$800 to $3,000** depending on how far lines need to travel. Adding a dedicated 240-volt circuit for an electric range or oven runs **$300 to $700** per circuit.

The [Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov) requires GFCI outlets within 6 feet of any kitchen sink, so if you are remodeling, your electrician will need to install these if they are not already present.

If you live in San Francisco or Manhattan, expect to pay 40 to 60 percent more than the national average. If you live in rural Georgia or Ohio, you may come in 20 to 30 percent below.

## How to Save Money Without Cutting Corners

The kitchen remodel industry is full of places to overspend and places to save strategically. Here is where it makes sense to trim:

**Do spend money on:** Cabinets (cheap cabinet boxes flex and fail), countertops (you touch these constantly), plumbing fixtures (cheap faucets drip within a year), and a licensed, insured contractor.

**Where you can save:**
- **Buy appliances during sale events.** Black Friday, Labor Day, and Memorial Day sales can take 20 to 40 percent off major appliances.
- **IKEA cabinets with upgraded doors.** IKEA cabinet boxes are actually quite durable. Pair them with aftermarket doors from a company like Semihandmade and you get a custom look at stock prices.
- **Skip the kitchen island if it requires plumbing.** A portable or non-plumbed island gives you counter space without the $2,000 to $5,000 plumbing cost.
- **Reface instead of replace.** If your cabinet boxes are solid, refacing saves thousands.
- **Laminate countertops have come a long way.** Modern laminate mimics quartz and granite convincingly. It runs a fraction of the cost and holds up well with normal care.
- **Time your project.** Contractors are often busiest April through August. Scheduling for October through February may get you a 10 to 15 percent discount.

## DIY vs. Hire a Pro: What You Can Realistically Do Yourself

Not everything in a kitchen remodel requires a licensed contractor. Here is an honest breakdown:

| Task | DIY Friendly? | Savings If You DIY |
|---|---|---|
| Painting walls and cabinets | Yes, with prep | $500 to $2,000 |
| Backsplash tile installation | Yes, with patience | $400 to $1,500 |
| Cabinet hardware replacement | Absolutely | $100 to $400 |
| Flooring installation (LVP) | Yes, click-lock systems are beginner-friendly | $300 to $1,200 |
| Appliance installation | Sometimes (check local codes) | $100 to $300 |
| Countertop installation | Not recommended (seams, weight) | Not worth the risk |
| Plumbing work | No (permits required in most states) | Risk of costly water damage |
| Electrical work | No (permits required, safety risk) | Risk of fire, failed inspection |
| Cabinet installation | Only if very handy | $1,500 to $5,000 |

If you are comfortable with power tools and have a few weekends to spare, tackling the backsplash and flooring yourself can save **$1,500 to $3,000** on a mid-range remodel. That is real money.

## Does a Kitchen Remodel Add Value to Your Home?

Yes, but not dollar for dollar. According to [Remodeling Magazine's 2025 Cost vs. Value Report](https://www.nahb.org), a mid-range kitchen remodel recoups approximately **68 to 74 percent** of its cost in added home value. A minor kitchen refresh recouped closer to **77 to 85 percent**, making it one of the best bang-for-buck home improvements.

Translation: if you spend $35,000 on a mid-range remodel, expect it to add roughly $24,000 to $26,000 to your home's value at resale. The rest of the value is in daily enjoyment of a nicer kitchen. If you plan to stay in your home for five or more years, the lifestyle value often matters more than the return on investment calculation.

Luxury remodels ($75,000+) recoup less (50 to 65 percent), because you can over-improve for your neighborhood. Before spending $100,000 on a kitchen in a neighborhood where homes sell for $250,000, talk to a local real estate agent about ceiling values.

## Red Flags to Watch for When Hiring a Kitchen Contractor

Kitchen remodels are one of the most common categories for contractor fraud. A few things that should stop you in your tracks:

- **Demanding more than 30 percent upfront.** A reasonable deposit is 10 to 30 percent. Anyone asking for 50 percent or more before work starts is a warning sign.
- **No written contract.** Never proceed on a verbal agreement, no matter how much you like the contractor personally.
- **No permit pulling.** Most kitchen remodels that involve plumbing, electrical, or structural work require permits. A contractor who says "we do not need permits, it is faster that way" is setting you up for trouble at resale.
- **Pressure to decide immediately.** Legitimate contractors are busy, not desperate. High-pressure tactics are a scam signal.
- **No license or insurance verification.** Verify your contractor's license through your state licensing board before signing anything. A list of state licensing boards is available through [HUD](https://www.hud.gov).

For more on protecting yourself, see our guide on [how to find a good contractor and avoid getting scammed](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor).

## Planning Your Kitchen Remodel: A Quick Timeline

Most homeowners underestimate how long a kitchen remodel takes. Here is a realistic timeline:

- **Design and planning:** 2 to 6 weeks
- **Getting permits:** 1 to 4 weeks (varies by city)
- **Ordering cabinets:** 3 to 12 weeks (stock is fast, semi-custom and custom take longer)
- **Actual construction:** 3 to 8 weeks for a full remodel
- **Punch list and finishing:** 1 to 2 weeks

Total: **plan for 3 to 6 months from first call to completed kitchen** for a full remodel. Budget an extra 10 to 15 percent as a contingency fund. Unexpected costs (hidden water damage, outdated wiring discovered inside walls, uneven floors) are common in any renovation project.
