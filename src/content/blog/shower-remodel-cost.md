A shower remodel in the United States typically costs between **$3,500 and $15,000**, with most homeowners spending around **$6,800** for a mid-range project. A basic prefab shower stall swap runs as low as $1,200, while a fully custom tile walk-in shower with frameless glass can reach $20,000 or more. Where you live, what you tear out, and the materials you choose drive the final number more than almost anything else.

This guide breaks down real costs, explains what drives prices up or down, and helps you figure out whether to hire a contractor, use a handyman, or tackle any of it yourself.

## What Makes Shower Remodels Expensive

Before diving into line items, it helps to understand the three biggest cost drivers.

### 1. Tile vs. Prefab Surfaces

Prefab acrylic or fiberglass surround kits (brands like Swanstone and American Standard) cost $300 to $900 for materials, while professional installation brings the total to $1,200 to $3,000. They go in fast and look clean, but they limit your design choices.

Custom tile is where costs escalate. Basic ceramic tile runs $1 to $4 per square foot, but a 3-by-5-foot shower surround takes roughly 100 square feet of tile plus backerboard, waterproofing membrane, mortar, and grout. Labor for tile setting runs $8 to $15 per square foot in most markets. Choose large-format porcelain slabs or natural stone and you can easily spend $30 to $60 per square foot for materials alone.

### 2. Plumbing Changes

Moving a drain, shifting a valve, or adding a second showerhead requires a licensed plumber. Plumbing labor averages $95 to $200 per hour depending on your region, and moving a drain can add $500 to $2,500 to a project. If your existing plumbing is copper or PVC in good shape and the new shower sits in the same footprint, plumbing costs stay low. If you are moving things around, budget accordingly.

### 3. Waterproofing and Subfloor Condition

This is where many budgets surprise people. When contractors open up a shower, they sometimes find rotted subfloor, compromised joists, or old waterproofing that was never right to begin with. Subfloor repairs run $500 to $3,000 depending on how far the damage has spread. Waterproofing membrane installation (required under any tile installation per most building codes) adds $200 to $600.

Skipping proper waterproofing to save money is one of the most expensive mistakes in home renovation. A shower leak that gets into the subfloor can cause mold and structural damage costing $10,000 or more to fix later.

## Cost Breakdown: Full Tile Shower Remodel

Here is a realistic line-item breakdown for a mid-range full tear-out and retile of a 36-by-36-inch corner shower.

| Line Item | Cost Range |

| Demolition (tile, old pan, surround) | $400 to $900 |
| Plumbing rough-in (no relocation) | $300 to $700 |
| Backer board and waterproofing | $350 to $700 |
| Shower pan or liner | $400 to $1,200 |
| Tile (materials, 100 sq ft) | $600 to $4,000 |
| Tile labor | $900 to $1,800 |
| Grout, thin-set, caulk, supplies | $150 to $350 |
| Shower door or enclosure | $600 to $2,500 |
| Valve and showerhead fixtures | $200 to $1,200 |

A skilled contractor in a mid-cost market will typically quote this project at $6,500 to $9,000. In high-cost metros like San Francisco, Seattle, or New York City, expect $10,000 to $16,000 for the same scope.

## Tub-to-Shower Conversion Costs

Converting an existing bathtub into a walk-in shower is the most popular remodel among homeowners aged 55 and older. The scope is larger than a simple swap: the tub must be removed, plumbing reconfigured, and the floor area rebuilt from scratch.

The [AARP Public Policy Institute](https://www.aarp.org) has noted that walk-in showers increase safety and reduce fall risk, making this one of the most meaningful aging-in-place modifications available.

**Typical costs:**

- Basic prefab tub-to-shower kit: $3,000 to $6,000 installed
- Custom tile walk-in with bench and grab bars: $9,000 to $16,000
- ADA-compliant curbless shower (barrier-free): $10,000 to $20,000

If your bathroom has only one bathtub and you are thinking about resale, keep at least one tub in the house. Buyers with children often expect it, and real estate professionals consistently note that homes with zero tubs can sell for less in family-oriented markets.

## Walk-In Shower Costs

Walk-in showers without a door have become very popular because they eliminate the hassle of cleaning glass doors and create an open, spa-like feel. A basic walk-in in a tub footprint runs $7,000 to $12,000. A larger dedicated walk-in space (4 by 6 feet or bigger) with multiple showerheads, a built-in niche, and frameless glass panels runs $12,000 to $22,000.

Key add-ons and their typical costs:

- **Built-in tile niche:** $300 to $700
- **Bench or seat:** $500 to $1,500
- **Frameless glass enclosure:** $1,200 to $4,500
- **Rain showerhead:** $150 to $600
- **Steam unit:** $2,500 to $6,000
- **Heated tile floor:** $1,500 to $4,500
- **Grab bars (ADA style):** $150 to $600

## Shower Pan Replacement Only

If your tile and walls are in good shape but the shower floor is cracked, stained, or leaking, you may only need a new pan. This is considerably less expensive than a full remodel.

Prefab acrylic or fiberglass pans cost $150 to $700 for materials. A custom-tiled pan with a mortar bed and waterproofing runs $800 to $2,500 in materials and labor. Total project cost, including demo and installation: $800 to $3,500 for a standard-size pan, more for custom sizes.

One important note: if your existing shower has a leaking pan, do not wait. Water intrusion behind the walls causes mold and wood rot that multiplies repair costs quickly. The [EPA estimates](https://www.epa.gov/mold) that mold begins growing on wet materials within 24 to 48 hours.

Labor rates are the biggest source of price variation across the country. Materials cost roughly the same nationwide (with some shipping variation), but contractor labor can differ by 40 to 60 percent depending on your market.

## DIY vs. Hiring a Pro: What You Can Do Yourself

You can save real money on a shower remodel if you are willing to do specific parts yourself. Here is an honest look at what is DIY-friendly and what is not.

### Reasonable DIY Tasks

- **Demolition:** Renting a dumpster and doing your own demo saves $400 to $900. It is dusty, heavy work, but doable for most able-bodied homeowners.
- **Painting:** If any adjacent walls need repainting, that is always DIY-friendly.
- **Installing a prefab shower surround kit:** Many kits are designed for homeowner installation. [The American Standard website](https://www.americanstandard-us.com) publishes installation guides for their shower systems.
- **Caulking:** Replacing old, moldy caulk yourself takes an afternoon and a $15 tube of silicone caulk.
- **Fixture swaps:** Replacing a showerhead yourself is simple if the supply line location stays the same. No permit required in most jurisdictions.

### Hire a Pro For These

- **Tile installation:** Bad tile jobs fail within a few years. Setting tile properly requires skill, and waterproofing errors are invisible until the damage is catastrophic.
- **Plumbing changes:** Most states require a licensed plumber for any work involving supply lines or drain relocation. Unpermitted plumbing work can create insurance and resale problems. Check your state licensing board requirements.
- **Electrical work:** If you are adding a GFCI outlet, in-floor heat, or a steam unit, this must be permitted and inspected in virtually every US jurisdiction. The [CPSC reports](https://www.cpsc.gov) that bathroom electrical failures are a leading cause of home fires.
- **Structural changes:** If your conversion involves moving walls or any load-bearing modification, that requires a licensed contractor and a permit.

## Permits: When You Need One and What They Cost

Shower remodels often require permits, depending on scope. Simple cosmetic work (retiling an existing shower in the same footprint without moving plumbing) may not. Adding plumbing, electrical, or structural changes almost always does.

Permit costs typically run $150 to $500 for bathroom work. While it can be tempting to skip the permit process to save time and money, unpermitted work can:

- Void your homeowner's insurance for related claims
- Create problems when you sell, requiring disclosure or costly retroactive permitting
- Lead to fines if the work is discovered by inspectors

The [International Residential Code (IRC)](https://codes.iccsafe.org), which most US states adopt with local amendments, specifies ventilation, waterproofing, and plumbing requirements for shower installations. Your local building department can tell you exactly what requires a permit in your area.

## How to Save Money Without Cutting Corners

**Keep the same footprint.** Every inch you move a drain or valve adds plumber time and cost. Work within the existing plumbing layout whenever possible.

**Choose mid-range tile strategically.** Spend more on the floor (where durability matters) and use less expensive tile on the walls. A designer accent strip can add visual interest without covering every wall in expensive stone.

**Get three bids.** Shower remodel pricing varies significantly by contractor. Getting three competitive bids is one of the most effective ways to ensure you are paying a fair price. The [National Association of Home Builders (NAHB)](https://www.nahb.org) recommends comparing bids on scope, not just price.

**Avoid the dead of winter or peak season.** Contractors in many markets are busiest from April through October. Scheduling work in November through February can sometimes yield 10 to 15 percent lower pricing.

**Buy materials yourself.** Some contractors mark up materials 20 to 40 percent. You can sometimes save by purchasing tile and fixtures directly through a tile distributor or home improvement store and having the contractor supply only labor.

---

## Red Flags When Getting Contractor Bids

Be cautious if a contractor:

- Asks for more than 30 to 40 percent upfront before work begins (a reasonable deposit is fine; a full payment upfront is a warning sign)
- Cannot provide proof of license and insurance in your state
- Does not mention permits when the project scope clearly requires them
- Offers a dramatically lower bid without a clear explanation of what is different
- Pressures you to decide immediately

Verify contractor licenses through your state's licensing board website. Most states maintain free searchable databases.
