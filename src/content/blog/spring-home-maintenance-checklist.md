Spring is the most important season for home maintenance, and the tasks you complete between March and May can save you thousands of dollars in repairs. A typical spring maintenance checklist costs between **$300 and $1,200** if you hire professionals for key inspections, or as little as **$50 to $150 in supplies** if you do most tasks yourself. Catching small problems now, before summer heat and humidity arrive, is what separates homeowners who avoid big repair bills from those who get blindsided by them.

This guide walks you through 28 essential spring tasks, organized by area of the home, with honest cost estimates and links to authoritative sources so you know what to expect.

## Why Spring Maintenance Matters More Than Any Other Season

Winter is hard on homes. Freezing temperatures stress pipes, roofing materials, caulk, and foundations. Spring thaw brings moisture intrusion, pest activity, and the consequences of any damage you could not see under snow. According to the [Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)](https://www.fema.gov/), water damage is the most common and costly home insurance claim in the US, and much of it is preventable with seasonal inspections.

The [National Association of Home Builders (NAHB)](https://www.nahb.org/) recommends spending 1 to 2 percent of your home's value on maintenance annually. For a $350,000 home, that is $3,500 to $7,000 per year. Spring is when you spend the bulk of that budget.

## Exterior Checklist

### 1. Roof Inspection

**What to do:** Walk the perimeter of your home and look up. With binoculars or from a ladder (safely), check for missing shingles, lifted flashing, and granule loss in gutters.

**Cost:** A professional roof inspection runs **$150 to $350**. If you find damage, minor repairs cost **$300 to $1,500**; a full replacement averages **$9,000 to $14,000** depending on size and material.

**Why it matters:** A single missing shingle can allow water infiltration that leads to decking rot, attic mold, and ceiling damage costing far more to fix. The [Insurance Institute for Business and Home Safety](https://ibhs.org/) notes that roof failures account for a significant share of weather-related insurance losses each year.

**Related:** [How Long Does a Roof Last by Material?](/blog/how-long-does-a-roof-last-by-material)

### 2. Gutters and Downspouts

**What to do:** Clear winter debris, check for sags or separations, and confirm downspouts direct water at least 6 feet from the foundation.

**Cost:** DIY with a ladder and gloves costs nothing beyond time. Professional gutter cleaning runs **$120 to $250** for a typical single-story home, **$175 to $350** for two stories.

**Why it matters:** Clogged gutters during spring rains send water straight to your foundation. Foundation repair costs **$2,000 to $15,000** or more. Gutters are cheap insurance.

### 3. Exterior Caulk and Sealants

**What to do:** Inspect all caulk around windows, doors, and where different siding materials meet. Replace any that is cracked, shrunk, or missing.

**Cost:** A tube of exterior caulk costs **$5 to $15**. This is almost always a DIY job.

**Why it matters:** The [US Department of Energy (DOE)](https://www.energy.gov/) estimates that air sealing and caulking can reduce heating and cooling costs by 10 to 20 percent annually.

### 4. Foundation Inspection

**What to do:** Walk your foundation perimeter looking for cracks wider than a credit card (about 1/8 inch), horizontal cracks, or areas where soil has settled away from the foundation.

**Cost:** A professional foundation inspection runs **$300 to $1,000**. Hairline cracks can be sealed for **$500 to $1,000**; serious structural repairs cost **$4,000 to $15,000** or more.

**Note:** Horizontal cracks in poured concrete or block foundations are more serious than vertical ones and warrant a structural engineer's opinion.

### 5. Driveway and Walkways

**What to do:** Fill cracks in concrete or asphalt before they expand. Freeze-thaw cycles are the primary cause of driveway deterioration.

**Cost:** DIY crack filler costs **$10 to $40** per tube. Professional driveway sealing runs **$100 to $300** for an average driveway. Full replacement averages **$3,000 to $10,000**.

### 6. Deck and Patio

**What to do:** Check deck boards for rot, loose fasteners, and structural posts for soft spots at the base. Test railings for stability. Apply stain or sealer if the wood is thirsty (sprinkle water; if it soaks in instead of beading, it is time to seal).

**Cost:** Deck staining runs **$550 to $1,200** professionally, or **$150 to $300** in materials if you DIY. Replacing rotted boards costs **$100 to $500** per board installed.

### 7. Exterior Paint

**What to do:** Look for peeling, bubbling, or fading paint. Catching paint failure early prevents wood rot underneath.

**Cost:** Full exterior repaints run **$2,500 to $9,000** depending on home size. Spot touch-ups are DIY-friendly at **$30 to $80** per gallon.

## HVAC and Energy

### 8. AC Tune-Up

**What to do:** Schedule a professional AC tune-up before you need air conditioning. This is the single highest-value spring task for most US homeowners.

**Cost:** A professional HVAC tune-up runs **$80 to $200**. Skipping it and having the system fail mid-July typically means emergency service calls at **$150 to $500** plus repair parts.

**What's included:** Refrigerant check, coil cleaning, capacitor inspection, filter replacement, and a thermostat calibration.

The [Environmental Protection Agency's ENERGY STAR program](https://www.energystar.gov/) recommends annual HVAC maintenance as a top way to improve energy efficiency.

### 9. Replace HVAC Filters

**What to do:** Replace all air filters. In homes with pets or allergy sufferers, use MERV 11 or higher.

**Cost:** Filters cost **$10 to $50** each depending on size and quality. Change them every 60 to 90 days.

### 10. Check Refrigerant Lines

**What to do:** Look at the refrigerant lines running from the outdoor unit to the home. Insulation should be intact. Fraying or missing insulation reduces efficiency.

**Cost:** Replacing refrigerant line insulation is inexpensive, typically **$20 to $60** in materials.

### 11. Clear Around Outdoor AC Unit

**What to do:** Remove leaves, dirt, and debris from around the condenser unit. Trim any bushes or plants to maintain at least 2 feet of clearance on all sides.

**Cost:** Zero. This is a 15-minute DIY task.

### 12. Test Ceiling Fans

**What to do:** Reverse ceiling fans to spin counter-clockwise (in summer mode) to push cool air down. Tighten any loose blades or wobbling mounts.

**Cost:** Zero. A ceiling fan installation runs **$100 to $250** if you need a new one.

## Plumbing

### 13. Inspect Outdoor Faucets and Hose Bibs

**What to do:** Turn on outdoor faucets and check for drips or leaks at the valve. Frost-free spigots can still crack if the interior shutoff was left open during a hard freeze.

**Cost:** Replacing a hose bib runs **$75 to $225** installed. Repairing a cracked one is often similar cost.

### 14. Check for Slow Drains

**What to do:** Run water in every sink, shower, and tub. Any that drains slowly has a developing clog to address before it gets worse.

**Cost:** DIY drain clearing with a snake or enzyme cleaner costs **$10 to $50**. Professional drain cleaning runs **$100 to $250**.

### 15. Water Heater Maintenance

**What to do:** Flush sediment from your water heater by connecting a hose to the drain valve and running water until it runs clear. This extends the water heater's life and improves efficiency.

**Cost:** Zero for a DIY flush. Professional water heater maintenance runs **$80 to $200**.

The [DOE recommends](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/water-heating) flushing water heaters annually to prevent sediment buildup, which reduces efficiency and can shorten the appliance's lifespan.

### 16. Check Under Sinks for Leaks

**What to do:** Open every cabinet under every sink and look for moisture, staining, or soft cabinet floors. Check supply lines for corrosion.

**Cost:** A corroded supply line replacement costs **$30 to $100** DIY. Ignoring a slow leak can lead to mold remediation bills of **$1,500 to $5,000** or more.

## Electrical and Safety

### 17. Test Smoke and Carbon Monoxide Detectors

**What to do:** Press the test button on every detector. Replace batteries in all units. Replace any detector that is 10 years old or older.

**Cost:** Replacement detectors cost **$20 to $50** each. The [US Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC)](https://www.cpsc.gov/) recommends combination smoke and CO detectors on every level of the home.

### 18. Inspect Electrical Panel

**What to do:** Look in your electrical panel for any signs of scorching, corrosion, or tripped breakers that keep tripping. This is a visual inspection; do not touch components.

**Cost:** A licensed electrician inspection costs **$100 to $200**. Panel replacement, if needed, runs **$1,500 to $3,500**.

### 19. Check Outdoor Outlets and GFCI Devices

**What to do:** Test all GFCI outlets by pressing the test button and confirming power cuts, then pressing reset to restore. All outdoor and bathroom outlets should be GFCI protected.

**Cost:** GFCI outlet replacement costs **$50 to $130** installed. The [National Fire Protection Association (NFPA)](https://www.nfpa.org/) notes that GFCI protection prevents hundreds of electrocution deaths annually.

### 20. Inspect Extension Cords and Power Strips

**What to do:** Check for fraying, cracked insulation, or plugs with loose connections. Discard any damaged cords, not tape them.

**Cost:** Quality surge-protecting power strips run **$20 to $60**.

## Interior and Basement

### 21. Check Attic for Winter Damage

**What to do:** Go into the attic and look for daylight through the roof deck, water stains on rafters or insulation, and signs of animal entry. Bring a flashlight.

**Cost:** Zero for a visual inspection. Attic insulation replacement runs **$1,500 to $3,500** for a typical attic.

### 22. Test Sump Pump

**What to do:** Pour a bucket of water into the sump pit and confirm the pump activates and discharges the water. Spring is peak sump pump failure season.

**Cost:** Professional sump pump testing costs **$50 to $150**. Sump pump replacement runs **$500 to $1,200** installed.

**Pro tip:** If your sump pump is more than 7 to 10 years old, consider replacing it proactively. A pump failure during a heavy spring rain can flood a finished basement in hours.

### 23. Inspect Basement Walls for Moisture

**What to do:** Look for white, chalky deposits (efflorescence) on basement walls, which indicate water moving through concrete. Also check for damp spots or standing water.

**Cost:** Waterproofing a basement runs **$5,000 to $15,000** for interior systems. Catching minor seepage early and addressing grading outside is far cheaper.

### 24. Check Windows and Window Screens

**What to do:** Open and close every window. Check for broken locks, damaged weatherstripping, and torn screens. This is also a good time to wash windows inside and out.

**Cost:** New window screens cost **$20 to $60** each. Weatherstripping replacement runs **$10 to $30** per window DIY.

### 25. Deep Clean Range Hood and Dryer Vent

**What to do:** Clean the grease filters on your range hood (usually dishwasher safe) and clean or replace the dryer vent duct. A clogged dryer vent is a leading cause of house fires.

**Cost:** DIY dryer vent cleaning kits cost **$20 to $40**. Professional cleaning runs **$90 to $170**. The [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov/) reports approximately 15,000 dryer fires per year, and most are caused by lint buildup.

## Lawn and Landscaping

### 26. Inspect Irrigation System

**What to do:** Turn on your sprinkler system for the first time and walk each zone, checking for broken heads, misdirected spray, and leaks at the valve box.

**Cost:** Repairing a broken sprinkler head costs **$5 to $15** DIY. Professional sprinkler system start-up and inspection runs **$50 to $150**.

### 27. Fertilize and Aerate Lawn

**What to do:** Apply a slow-release spring fertilizer matched to your grass type (cool-season grasses like bluegrass and fescue need different formulas than warm-season grasses like bermuda and zoysia). Aerate if the lawn is compacted.

**Cost:** DIY fertilizer costs **$30 to $80** for a typical lawn. Professional lawn fertilization runs **$50 to $150** per application.

### 28. Trim Trees Away from the House

**What to do:** Cut back any branches within 10 feet of your roof. Overhanging branches scrape shingles in wind and provide a highway for squirrels and other pests.

**Cost:** Trimming a single tree runs **$250 to $750** professionally. Full tree removal costs **$400 to $2,500** depending on size.

## How to Prioritize When Budget Is Tight

Not every homeowner can tackle all 28 tasks at once. Here is a priority order based on potential damage from deferred maintenance:

1. **Roof and gutters first.** Water intrusion causes the most damage in the shortest time.
2. **HVAC tune-up second.** This prevents a system failure in peak summer, when repair costs and wait times are highest.
3. **Sump pump test third.** Especially critical in the Midwest, mid-Atlantic, and Pacific Northwest where spring rains are heavy.
4. **Smoke and CO detectors fourth.** Life safety first; do not defer this.
5. **Everything else.** Spread remaining tasks across April and May.

## Regional Differences to Know

Spring arrives at different times across the US, so adjust this checklist to your region:

- **Southeast (FL, GA, SC):** Start in late February. Hurricane prep begins in April. Focus on roof, windows, and storm shutters.
- **Midwest (IL, OH, MI):** Heavy spring rain and thaw risks. Sump pump and foundation checks are critical in March.
- **Pacific Northwest (OR, WA):** Moss on roofs is a major spring issue. Treat with zinc strips or professional moss removal ($200 to $600).
- **Mountain West (CO, UT, MT):** Late freezes possible through April. Do not uncover outdoor spigots too early.
- **Southwest (AZ, NM, TX):** Monsoon prep in the Southwest starts in May. Check weatherstripping, roof, and drainage.

## Hiring Help: What to Know

If you hire professionals for spring maintenance, always:

- Verify licenses through your state's contractor licensing board. The [National Association of Home Builders](https://www.nahb.org/) has a contractor finder tool.
- Get at least two written estimates for work over $500.
- Never pay more than 30 percent upfront for a job that has not started.
- Check reviews on multiple platforms, not just the company's own website.
