**The short answer:** Replacing a garage door costs **$700 to $4,500 installed** for most US homeowners in 2026, with the national average landing around **$1,200 to $1,800** for a standard single-car steel door with basic hardware and professional installation. Double-car doors run **$1,200 to $3,500 installed**. Custom wood, glass-panel, or carriage-style doors push the high end to **$4,500 to $10,000 or more**. Labor alone typically runs **$200 to $600**, so the door itself accounts for most of the bill. The good news: garage door replacement is one of the highest-ROI home improvement projects in the US, returning about **103 percent** of cost at resale according to Remodeling Magazine's Cost vs. Value report.

If you are upgrading your garage door opener at the same time, this is also a good moment to consider whether your electrical panel can support an [EV charger](/blog/ev-charger-installation-cost/) or [whole-home generator transfer switch](/blog/whole-home-generator-cost/) in the same space. Older garages sometimes need [electrical panel upgrades](/blog/electrical-panel-upgrade-cost/) to support these additions safely.

## Garage Door Cost by Material

The material you choose affects price, durability, maintenance, and curb appeal significantly.

### Steel: $600 to $3,000

Steel is by far the most popular choice for American homeowners, and for good reason. It is durable, low-maintenance, available in a wide range of styles, and reasonably priced. Single-layer steel is the cheapest option but offers no insulation. If your garage is attached to your living space, the insulated two- or three-layer steel doors are worth the upgrade for energy efficiency and noise reduction.

Steel is also paintable, so you can match it to any home color scheme.

### Wood: $1,000 to $5,000 (door only)

Real wood doors look beautiful and can be custom-built to any size or style. The trade-offs are significant: wood requires regular maintenance (painting or staining every few years), warps in humid climates, and costs substantially more than steel. If you live in the Southeast or Pacific Northwest, wood demands more upkeep than in drier climates.

Wood composite (engineered wood wrapped in polyurethane) offers a similar look at lower maintenance cost and is worth considering as a middle ground.

### Aluminum: $700 to $2,500

Aluminum is lightweight, rust-resistant, and ideal for coastal homes where salt air degrades steel. It is less durable against dents than steel and less thermally efficient unless paired with insulation. Full-view aluminum doors with tempered glass panels have become a popular contemporary design choice and sit at the higher end of the aluminum price range.

### Fiberglass: $900 to $2,000

Fiberglass is an excellent choice for humid or coastal environments. It does not rust or rot, holds paint well, and can mimic wood grain texture convincingly. The main limitation is that fiberglass becomes brittle in very cold weather, so it is not the best choice in northern states with harsh winters.

## Garage Door Opener: An Often-Forgotten Cost

If your existing opener is older than 10 years or incompatible with your new door, budget for a replacement. A new garage door opener costs:

- **Chain drive:** $150 to $250 (loud but reliable; best for detached garages)
- **Belt drive:** $200 to $350 (quieter; ideal for attached garages near living spaces)
- **Screw drive:** $175 to $300 (minimal maintenance; struggles in extreme temperatures)
- **Direct drive:** $200 to $400 (ultra-quiet; single moving part)
- **Smart opener (Wi-Fi enabled):** $250 to $600 (app control, alerts, integration with Alexa or Google Home)

Installation of a new opener by the same technician is often discounted to **$75 to $150** when done alongside a door replacement.

## Labor Cost for Garage Door Replacement

Professional installation typically costs **$200 to $600** for a standard door, depending on your location and the complexity of the job. Factors that raise labor costs:

- **Old door removal and disposal:** Add $50 to $100 if not included in the quote
- **Spring replacement:** Torsion springs (the horizontal bar above the door) typically last 10,000 to 15,000 cycles. New springs cost $75 to $250 including labor
- **Track and hardware replacement:** If the existing tracks are damaged or incompatible, expect to add $150 to $300
- **Framing repair:** If the rough opening has wood rot or damage, a carpenter needs to repair it before the door is installed

Always ask your installer for a written quote that itemizes the door, hardware, springs, opener (if applicable), removal of the old door, and labor separately. This makes comparing quotes much easier.

## Do You Need a Permit to Replace a Garage Door?

In most US jurisdictions, a straight door replacement (same size, same opening) does not require a permit. However, permits may be required if:

- You are changing the size of the opening
- You are replacing the door with a hurricane-rated door (some jurisdictions require inspection)
- You are doing structural framing work around the opening

When in doubt, ask your installer and check with your local building department. Unpermitted structural work can create complications when you sell your home.

## DIY vs. Hire a Pro

Garage door replacement is one of the more genuinely dangerous DIY projects in home improvement. The torsion spring is under enormous tension and can cause serious injury if handled incorrectly. Each year, approximately **20,000 Americans are injured** by garage door components, many during DIY installation or repair, according to the [Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC)](https://www.cpsc.gov).

**What you can DIY:**
- Cosmetic repairs (painting, replacing weather stripping, replacing decorative hardware)
- Installing a new smart opener on an existing working door
- Lubricating springs, rollers, and hinges (annual maintenance)

**What should be left to professionals:**
- Full door replacement (especially torsion spring installation)
- Spring replacement (dangerous; leave this to a pro)
- Track adjustment and alignment
- Any structural work around the opening

Labor for a professional installation runs $200 to $600, which is modest relative to the risk of doing it yourself incorrectly. Most garage door companies also warranty their installation work, which adds real value.

## How to Get the Best Price on a Garage Door

**Get three quotes.** Garage door pricing varies more than most homeowners expect. Get quotes from at least two to three companies. Big-box stores (Home Depot, Lowe's) offer doors with installation packages that are worth comparing against local garage door specialists.

**Buy the door and labor together.** Garage door companies typically charge less for installation when they supply the door. Buying a door online and having a company install it often costs more overall.

**Ask about end-of-season sales.** Garage door companies often run promotions in late fall and early spring when demand is lower.

**Check manufacturer rebates.** Brands like Clopay, Amarr, Wayne Dalton, and Overhead Door sometimes offer rebates on insulated doors. ENERGY STAR certified garage doors may also qualify for rebates through your local utility.

**Do not over-insulate.** A fully insulated three-layer door makes a meaningful difference in an attached garage you use as living space. For a detached garage, single- or two-layer insulation is usually sufficient and saves $300 to $700.

## Signs You Need a Full Replacement vs. a Repair

Sometimes your garage door just needs a repair, not a full replacement. Here is how to tell the difference:

**Repair (not replace) when:**
- A single panel is damaged but the rest of the door is in good shape (panel replacement runs $250 to $800)
- The springs have broken (spring replacement runs $150 to $350 and does not require a new door)
- The opener has failed but the door itself is fine
- The door is making noise but is structurally sound (lubrication and adjustment fix most noise issues)

**Replace when:**
- The door is more than 20 to 25 years old
- Multiple panels are damaged or rusted
- The door has significant structural damage (hit by a vehicle, storm damage)
- The frame around the door is rotting or damaged
- You want improved insulation, security, or curb appeal
