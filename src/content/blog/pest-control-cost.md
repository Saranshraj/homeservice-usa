Finding a cockroach in your kitchen or a mouse in the garage is enough to ruin your whole week. Here is the honest answer right up front: **pest control costs $150 to $500 for a one-time visit** in 2026, with most homeowners paying around $250. If you sign up for a recurring service plan, monthly visits run $40 to $70 per month, quarterly service runs $100 to $300 per treatment, and annual contracts range from $400 to $1,200 per year. Severe infestations involving rodents, cockroaches, or wildlife can push costs to $700 or more for a single service call. The good news: catching a problem early is dramatically cheaper than waiting until it spreads through your walls.

Before calling anyone, it helps to know what pest you have. The specific pest drives both the treatment method and the price more than almost any other factor. We will break that down in detail below.

## Pest Control Cost Overview

| Service Type | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| One-time visit (single pest) | $150 to $500 |
| One-time full-home inspection + treatment | $200 to $600 |
| Monthly service plan | $40 to $70 per month |
| Quarterly service plan | $100 to $300 per quarter |
| Annual pest control contract | $400 to $1,200 |
| Initial setup fee (recurring plans) | $100 to $300 |

Most pest control companies charge a higher "initial visit" fee to cover the full inspection and first treatment, then a lower ongoing fee for each follow-up. That initial visit is where they identify all entry points, treatment zones, and the size of the infestation.

## Pest Control Cost by Pest Type

This is where you will see the most variation. Some pests require one visit and simple chemical application; others need multiple treatments, structural exclusion work, or specialized equipment.

| Pest | Typical Cost Range | Treatment Visits |
|---|---|---|
| Cockroaches | $100 to $400 | 2 to 3 |
| Mice | $200 to $700 | 2 to 4 |
| Rats | $300 to $800 | 2 to 4 |
| Ants (general) | $150 to $400 | 1 to 2 |
| Wasps / hornets | $100 to $400 | 1 to 2 |
| Mosquitoes | $75 to $200 per treatment | Seasonal (5 to 8 treatments) |
| Fleas | $75 to $400 | 2 to 3 |
| Spiders | $100 to $300 | 1 to 2 |

| Squirrels (wildlife removal) | $200 to $600 | 1 to 2 |

| Bed bugs | $300 to $5,000 | 2 to 3 (or 1 for heat) |
| Termites | $500 to $3,500 | 1 (varies by method) |

**Cockroaches** are one of the most common calls pest control companies receive, especially in warmer states. A German cockroach infestation in a kitchen can require two or three visits with gel baits, insect growth regulators, and residual sprays. Expect $150 to $400 for a typical apartment or home.

**Mice and rats** cost more because effective treatment requires not just killing the existing rodents but sealing entry points so new ones cannot get in. This "exclusion work" often costs extra, anywhere from $100 to $500 on top of the basic treatment. If you skip exclusion work, the problem will almost certainly come back.

**Wasps and hornets** are often the cheapest and quickest fix: a technician locates the nest, applies a residual dust or spray, and the nest is dead within a day or two. Costs are low unless the nest is inside a wall cavity, which requires drilling and specialized application, pushing the price toward $400.

**Mosquito treatment** is typically sold as a seasonal program. Each yard treatment (usually every 3 to 4 weeks from late spring through fall) costs $75 to $200 per visit, and the season runs 5 to 8 treatments. The [EPA recommends](https://www.epa.gov/mosquitocontrol) integrated pest management approaches that combine habitat reduction with targeted treatment.

## Service Plan vs. One-Time Treatment: Which Makes Sense?

This is a decision a lot of homeowners struggle with. Here is a simple framework.

**A one-time treatment makes sense when:**
- You have a single, isolated pest problem (a wasp nest, a mouse that got in after you left a door open)
- Your home is relatively new or well-sealed and you have not had recurring pest issues
- You live in a dry climate where pest pressure is low

**A recurring service plan makes sense when:**
- You live in the South, Southeast, or Southwest where pest pressure is year-round
- Your home is older with gaps, cracks, and entry points that are difficult to fully seal
- You have had repeat infestations of cockroaches, ants, or rodents
- You want a warranty that covers re-treatment between visits at no extra charge

The math usually works out in favor of a plan if you need more than two one-time treatments per year. A quarterly plan at $125 per visit ($500 per year) can be cheaper than two one-time calls at $300 each. And the ongoing monitoring catches problems before they explode into full infestations.

## What Affects Pest Control Pricing?

### Home Size

Most pest control companies price by square footage for interior treatments. A 1,000 sq ft apartment costs less to treat than a 3,500 sq ft house. Exterior perimeter treatments are often priced per linear foot.

### Severity of Infestation

An early-stage mouse problem with one or two entry points costs far less than a multi-room rodent infestation with droppings throughout the walls. Getting an inspection at the first sign of a problem, not months later, is one of the best ways to control cost.

### Treatment Method

Chemical sprays are the cheapest option. Baiting systems cost more but can be more effective for certain pests. Heat treatments, cryonite, or fumigation are significantly more expensive but sometimes necessary for specific situations (see our [bed bug treatment guide](/blog/bed-bug-treatment-cost/) for a detailed breakdown of heat vs. chemical options).

### Location and Labor Rates

Pest control costs vary significantly by state and city. Urban areas in California, New York, and New England generally have higher labor rates. Southern states like Texas, Florida, and Georgia have high pest pressure but also more competitive markets with many local operators, which keeps prices in check.

### Seasonal Demand

Pest activity spikes in spring and summer when most insects are breeding. Calling a pest control company in the off-season (late fall or winter) may get you a lower price or quicker scheduling.

## DIY Pest Control vs. Hiring a Professional

For many pest problems, DIY is a reasonable first step. For others, it is a waste of money that delays effective treatment and allows the infestation to grow.

| Situation | DIY or Pro? |
|---|---|
| Single wasp nest in an accessible location | DIY is fine |
| Occasional ant trail near entry points | DIY products often work |
| Recurring cockroach problem in kitchen | Pro recommended |
| Any rodent infestation | Pro strongly recommended |
| Bed bugs | Pro required (DIY almost never works) |
| Termites | Pro required |
| Wildlife (raccoons, squirrels, bats) | Pro required |
| Mosquito yard treatment | Either works, pro is more consistent |

The [Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov) maintains guidelines on safe use of residential pesticides, which is worth reading before using any over-the-counter products.

**The honest truth about DIY:** Over-the-counter products from hardware stores use the same active ingredients as professional treatments but at lower concentrations. For simple problems, they work. For established infestations, they often drive pests deeper into walls without eliminating the colony, making the problem harder for a professional to fix later.

## How to Hire a Pest Control Company (and Avoid Scams)

The pest control industry has more than its share of door-to-door salespeople, high-pressure tactics, and shoddy operators. Here is how to protect yourself.

**Verify the license.** Every state requires pest control applicators to be licensed. The [National Pest Management Association (NPMA)](https://www.npmapestworld.org) maintains a directory of members, and your state's department of agriculture licenses pest control operators. For example, the Florida Department of Agriculture and Consumer Services regulates pest control companies at fdacs.gov, and the Texas Department of Agriculture does the same at squaremeals.org. Always ask for the technician's license number before work begins.

**Get at least three quotes.** Pricing varies enormously between companies, even for identical services. A second or third quote takes 20 minutes and can save you hundreds of dollars.

**Read the contract carefully.** Recurring service contracts typically require 30 to 90 days' notice to cancel without a penalty. Know what you are signing before you commit. Ask specifically: What pests are covered? What is the re-treatment policy if pests return between visits? Is there a cancellation fee?

**Watch for red flags:**
- Technicians who arrive unannounced claiming your neighbor just had a "serious pest problem"
- Extreme pressure to sign a contract on the same day
- Quotes significantly lower than everyone else without a clear explanation
- No written estimate before starting work
- Inability or unwillingness to provide a license number

**Ask what pesticides they use.** This matters especially if you have children, pets, or a family member with chemical sensitivities. The [EPA's pesticide registration database](https://www.epa.gov/pesticide-registration) lets you look up any registered product and read its safety data.

## Questions to Ask Before Signing a Pest Control Contract

1. What is included in the initial treatment, and what does each follow-up visit include?
2. How many days do I need to stay out of my home after treatment?
3. What is the re-treatment guarantee if the problem comes back between scheduled visits?
4. Is there a cancellation fee, and how much notice is required?
5. Are your products safe for pets and children, and what precautions should I take?
6. Can I see your state pest control applicator license?
7. What pest identification did you base this quote on?

## When to Call a Pest Control Company Immediately

Some pest problems can wait a week for a convenient appointment. Others need attention right away.

**Call the same day if you see:**
- A large number of rodent droppings, especially in a kitchen or food storage area
- Live cockroaches during daylight hours (cockroaches are nocturnal; seeing them during the day usually means a large infestation)
- A bat inside the living area of your home (bats carry rabies; contact your local health department as well)
- Signs of a bee or wasp nest inside a wall cavity (buzzing in the wall, staining on drywall)
- Any pest near a family member with a known allergy to stings

For raccoons or other wildlife inside the home, call a licensed wildlife removal specialist. Many states regulate the removal and relocation of wildlife, and attempting to trap and relocate without a permit can result in fines. The [Humane Society of the United States](https://www.humanesociety.org/resources/finding-pest-control-company) offers guidance on finding humane wildlife removal services.

## How to Reduce Pest Control Costs Long-Term

The most effective way to lower your pest control bill over time is to make your home less attractive to pests in the first place.

**Seal entry points.** Mice can fit through a hole the size of a dime. Caulk gaps around pipes, seal foundation cracks, and install door sweeps on exterior doors. This is cheaper than repeat rodent treatments.

**Eliminate moisture.** Many pests, including cockroaches, silverfish, and earwigs, are drawn to damp environments. Fixing leaky pipes, running a bathroom exhaust fan, and addressing any standing water reduces pest pressure significantly. See our [bathroom exhaust fan guide](/blog/bathroom-exhaust-fan-installation-cost/) and [sump pump guide](/blog/sump-pump-installation-cost/) for related home improvements.

**Store food properly.** Cockroaches and mice follow food. Storing dry goods in sealed containers, cleaning up crumbs promptly, and not leaving pet food out overnight removes the food source that attracts pests in the first place.

**Maintain the yard.** Overgrown shrubs, wood piles against the house, and leaf debris against the foundation are common pest harborage areas. Keeping landscaping trimmed and moving wood piles away from the house reduces the pest pressure on the building.
