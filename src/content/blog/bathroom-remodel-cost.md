If you are staring at cracked tile, a dripping faucet, or a vanity that belongs in a time capsule, you are probably wondering how much a bathroom remodel is going to run you. Here is the honest answer upfront: most US homeowners spend between **$8,000 and $18,000** for a full bathroom remodel in 2026, with a national average of around $11,500. A powder room refresh can come in under $3,000, while a luxury master bathroom overhaul can easily reach $40,000 or more. The biggest cost drivers are bathroom size, whether you move any plumbing or electrical, the materials you choose, and your local labor rates.

Take a breath. This guide breaks down every cost category so you know exactly what to expect, where you can save, and how to avoid being overcharged.

If you are planning your bathroom alongside a [kitchen remodel](/blog/kitchen-remodel-cost/) or adding [hardwood floors](/blog/hardwood-floor-installation-cost/) elsewhere in the home, bundling projects with the same contractor can save on labor. And since bathroom work always involves plumbing, our guide on [what a plumber actually costs](/blog/how-much-does-a-plumber-cost/) will help you sanity-check any quote. Before signing with anyone, read [how to find a good contractor](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/) to avoid the most common bathroom remodel scams.

## What Type of Bathroom Are You Remodeling?

The first thing that shapes your budget is which bathroom you are working with.

| Bathroom Type | Typical Size | Average Remodel Cost (2026) |
|---|---|---|
| Half bath (powder room) | 20-40 sq ft | $3,000-$8,000 |
| Full bath (guest bath) | 50-100 sq ft | $8,000-$20,000 |
| Master bathroom | 100-200+ sq ft | $15,000-$45,000 |

These ranges assume a mid-range renovation: updating existing fixtures, replacing tile, installing a new vanity, and refreshing the space without moving walls or plumbing lines. The moment you start relocating drains or adding circuits, costs climb fast.

## Cost Breakdown by Component

Here is where every dollar typically goes in a full bathroom remodel.

### Tile and Flooring: $1,500-$6,000

Tile is usually the largest material cost. Ceramic and porcelain floor tiles run $2-$8 per square foot for materials. Natural stone (marble, travertine, slate) can reach $15-$30 per square foot before you even count labor.

Tile installation labor runs $8-$20 per square foot, depending on tile size, pattern complexity, and your region. A basic 60-square-foot bathroom floor with simple ceramic tile might cost $400-$600 in materials and $500-$1,200 to install.

**Where to save**: Large-format tiles (12x24 inches or bigger) reduce labor time because there are fewer cuts and grout lines. Porcelain tiles that mimic marble or wood give you an upscale look at a fraction of the price.

### Vanity and Sink: $300-$3,500 Installed

A stock vanity from a big-box home improvement store costs $200-$800. Semi-custom options run $800-$2,000. Custom-built cabinetry pushes $3,000 or higher.

Add $150-$400 for a plumber to install the new vanity, hook up the drain, and connect supply lines. Countertop material matters too: cultured marble runs $50-$100 per linear foot, while quartz costs $80-$150 per linear foot.

### Toilet: $300-$900 Installed

A quality toilet costs $150-$600 for the unit itself. A plumber typically charges $150-$300 to remove the old one and install the new one. High-efficiency toilets (1.28 gallons per flush or less) qualify for rebates in many states and will noticeably reduce your water bill over time. The EPA's WaterSense program certifies toilets that use at least 20% less water than standard models. Check [epa.gov/watersense](https://www.epa.gov/watersense) for rebates available in your ZIP code.

### Shower and Tub: $1,500-$12,000

This is where budgets can swing the most. Here is a quick comparison:

| Option | Typical Installed Cost |
|---|---|
| Prefab shower stall (fiberglass or acrylic) | $1,500-$4,000 |
| Tile shower (basic, no niche) | $3,500-$6,000 |
| Custom tile shower with niche and bench | $6,000-$12,000+ |
| Bathtub replacement (alcove style) | $1,500-$3,500 |
| Tub-to-shower conversion | $2,500-$8,000 |
| Freestanding soaking tub | $3,000-$10,000 |

If you are converting a tub to a walk-in shower, factor in waterproofing, a new drain, and possible floor modification. A licensed plumber and a tile contractor both need to be involved.

### Plumbing: $500-$4,500

Replacing fixtures only (faucets, showerheads, shut-off valves) keeps plumbing costs modest, usually $500-$1,500. Moving a drain, relocating the toilet, or rerouting supply lines can add $2,000-$4,500 in plumbing labor alone.

Plumbers charge $75-$150 per hour in most US markets, with higher rates in cities like San Francisco, New York, Boston, and Seattle — see our [plumber cost guide](/blog/how-much-does-a-plumber-cost/) for a full breakdown by job type.

### Electrical: $300-$2,500

Modern bathrooms need GFCI outlets, proper ventilation fans, and updated lighting. The National Electrical Code requires GFCI protection for all bathroom outlets, and most municipalities require permits for any new circuits.

A new exhaust fan runs $50-$300 for the unit and $150-$400 to install. Adding a dedicated circuit for heated floors or an electric towel warmer adds $500-$1,200 in electrical work. Always hire a licensed electrician for bathroom electrical work. The combination of water and electricity is genuinely dangerous.

The [Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC)](https://www.cpsc.gov) reports that faulty bathroom wiring contributes significantly to residential electrical fires every year. This is not a place to cut corners.

### Paint and Finishes: $200-$800

Bathroom painting is one area where you can DIY to save money. A gallon of high-quality moisture-resistant paint costs $30-$60. Budget $200-$500 in labor if you hire a painter. Use a mold-resistant primer in high-humidity areas, especially around the shower.

### Permits: $100-$1,500

Most structural, plumbing, and electrical work requires a building permit. Costs vary by municipality. In many cities and counties, a bathroom remodel permit runs $150-$500. In high-cost metros like Los Angeles or Chicago, permit fees can reach $1,000-$1,500.

Never skip permits. Working without them can void your homeowners insurance, create serious problems when you sell the home, and expose you to code violation fines. It is also worth knowing [how homeowners insurance claims work](/blog/homeowners-insurance-claims/) in case water damage or unexpected issues surface after the project is complete. A reputable contractor will always pull permits.

## Labor Costs by Trade

Here is what to expect for skilled labor in 2026 across the US:

| Trade | Typical Hourly Rate |
|---|---|
| General contractor | $70-$130/hr (or 10-20% of total project cost) |
| Plumber | $75-$150/hr |
| Electrician | $80-$130/hr |
| Tile setter | $40-$80/hr |
| Painter | $30-$60/hr |
| Carpenter or cabinet installer | $50-$100/hr |

Labor typically makes up 40-60% of total bathroom remodel costs. In high-cost metros, expect labor rates 30-50% above the national averages listed above.

## Surprises That Can Raise Your Costs

Some discoveries during demolition can add thousands to your budget:

- **Water damage or mold behind walls**: If your old shower pan was leaking, you may find rotted subfloor or mold inside the walls. [Mold remediation](/blog/mold-remediation-cost/) can add $1,000-$4,000 to the project. The [EPA has thorough guidance on mold cleanup](https://www.epa.gov/mold) that is worth reading before any bathroom demo.
- **Outdated plumbing**: Older homes with galvanized steel pipes may need full pipe replacement, not just fixture swaps.
- **Asbestos**: Homes built before 1980 may have asbestos in floor tile, drywall compound, or pipe insulation. Testing costs $200-$600; remediation can add $1,500-$5,000 if asbestos is found.
- **Structural issues**: Moving load-bearing walls or addressing structural problems can add $3,000-$10,000 to your project.

A thorough contractor will inspect before finalizing a quote and will include contingency language in the contract for discoveries like these.

## DIY vs. Hiring a Pro

Some bathroom tasks are great DIY projects. Others require a licensed professional for safety and legal reasons.

| Task | DIY Friendly? | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Painting | Yes | Great DIY project, saves $200-$500 |
| Replacing a toilet | Yes, if handy | Shut off water, remove bolts, install new wax ring |
| Installing a vanity | Moderately | Plumbing hookup may need a licensed plumber |
| Setting tile | Yes, but tricky | Requires proper substrate prep and waterproofing |
| Moving a drain or pipes | No | Licensed plumber and permit required |
| New electrical circuits | No | Licensed electrician required by code |
| Shower waterproofing | No | Improper waterproofing is the top cause of costly water damage |

If you have the skills, handling painting and simple fixture swaps yourself can save $500-$1,500 on a mid-range remodel. But waterproofing and structural plumbing are areas where DIY mistakes cost far more than hiring a professional from the start.

## How to Vet Your Contractor (and Avoid Scams)

Bathroom remodels are a frequent target for contractor fraud. Knowing [how to find and vet a reliable contractor](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/) before you commit is your best protection. Here is how to protect yourself:

1. **Verify the license.** Every state requires plumbers and electricians to be licensed. Most states also license general contractors. Check your state contractor licensing board before signing anything. The [National Association of Home Builders (NAHB)](https://www.nahb.org) has a contractor locator tool worth using.

2. **Get three written quotes.** Each quote should be itemized: materials, labor, permit costs, and payment schedule all listed separately.

3. **Never pay more than 10-30% upfront.** A contractor demanding 50% or more before work begins is a serious red flag. Final payment should always come after the work passes inspection.

4. **Require a written contract.** The contract should include start and completion dates, a detailed scope of work, payment schedule, and a process for handling change orders.

5. **Confirm insurance.** Ask for proof of general liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage. If an uninsured worker is injured in your home, you could be held liable.

6. **Never pay in cash.** Pay by check or credit card so you have a documented paper trail.

## Smart Ways to Save Without Cutting Quality

- **Keep the layout the same.** Moving plumbing is expensive. Updating the bathroom without relocating the toilet, shower, or tub can save you $2,000-$5,000. The same principle applies if you're also planning a [kitchen remodel](/blog/kitchen-remodel-cost/) — keeping plumbing runs in place is the single biggest cost-saver in either project.
- **Reface rather than replace cabinets.** Cabinet refacing costs 40-50% less than full replacement and looks nearly identical.
- **Choose stock vanities over custom.** Stock options from home improvement stores deliver solid quality at a fraction of the price of custom cabinetry.
- **Buy your own materials.** Purchase tile, fixtures, and hardware yourself and let the contractor handle labor only. You control quality and can shop sales.
- **Time your project strategically.** Contractors are busiest in spring and early summer. Scheduling a remodel in fall or winter can sometimes yield better pricing.
- **Check for rebates.** The [Department of Energy's ENERGY STAR program](https://www.energystar.gov/products) offers rebates on bath fans, lighting, and water heaters in many states. The [IRS Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/energy-efficient-home-improvement-credit) may apply to certain upgrades as well.

## Financing Your Bathroom Remodel

If cash on hand is limited, several financing paths exist:

- **Home equity line of credit (HELOC)**: Often the lowest interest rate option if you have equity in your home. Interest may be tax-deductible for home improvements; check with a tax professional.
- **Personal loan**: Unsecured, no home equity required. Rates typically run 6-24% interest.
- **FHA 203(k) loan**: For larger renovations combined with a mortgage refinance. [HUD provides detailed guidance on 203(k) loans](https://www.hud.gov/program_offices/housing/sfh/203k).
- **Contractor financing**: Many contractors offer 0% or low-interest financing through third-party lenders, but read the fine print on deferred interest terms carefully.

## Common Questions

**How long does a bathroom remodel take?**
A full bathroom remodel typically takes 2-4 weeks if the contractor is on-site daily. Delays happen if materials are back-ordered, permits take longer than expected, or hidden damage turns up during demo.

**Do I need a permit for a bathroom remodel?**
Yes, for most structural, plumbing, and electrical work. A simple cosmetic update (new paint, fresh fixtures, vanity swap without moving plumbing) may not require a permit in many jurisdictions. Ask your local building department directly.

**Will a bathroom remodel increase my home's value?**
According to Remodeling Magazine's Cost vs. Value data, a mid-range bathroom remodel typically recoups 60-70% of costs in home value at resale. An upscale master bath remodel may recoup 50-60%. Your local market matters a great deal.

**How do I know if I need to replace the subfloor?**
Soft spots, squeaking underfoot, or visible water staining around the toilet base or shower are signs of subfloor damage. A good contractor will assess this during the initial walkthrough. Subfloor repair typically adds $500-$2,000 depending on extent.
