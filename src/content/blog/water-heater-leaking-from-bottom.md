If you're staring at a puddle under your water heater right now, here's what you need to know immediately: a small drip from the drain valve is a cheap fix (usually $10 to $30 in parts), but water pooling under the tank itself almost always means internal corrosion and a full replacement, which runs $900 to $2,500 installed. The key is figuring out exactly where the water is coming from before you panic or call anyone.

This guide walks you through the most common causes, what each one costs to fix, and the warning signs that tell you it's time to stop patching and just replace the unit.

## First Things First: Is It Actually Leaking?

Before anything else, dry up the puddle completely and place a few paper towels around the base of the unit. Wait 20 to 30 minutes, then check. If the towels are dry, you may be dealing with condensation, which is totally normal, especially on a cold tank in a humid basement.

Condensation happens when cold water first enters the tank or when humidity is high. It is not a problem and does not need a repair. If the towels are wet, you have a real leak and need to find the source.

**Safety note:** If you smell rotten eggs (gas) near the water heater, leave the house immediately and call your gas company. That is a gas leak, not a water issue, and it is an emergency.

## The 5 Most Common Causes of a Water Heater Leaking From the Bottom

### 1. A Leaking Drain Valve

This is the most common cause and the best-case scenario. The drain valve is a hose-bib-style valve near the very bottom of the tank. It is used for flushing sediment and is easy to bump loose or wear out over time.

**How to check:** Look for dripping or a slow drip directly from the valve itself, not from a crack in the tank below it.

**Fix:** Tighten the valve with a pair of pliers or a wrench (just snug, do not over-tighten). If it still drips, replace the valve. A replacement drain valve costs about $5 to $20 at any hardware store. A plumber will charge $75 to $150 for the visit, though this is a DIY-friendly job for most homeowners.

### 2. A Failing Temperature and Pressure Relief (T&P) Valve

The T&P valve is a safety device on the side of the tank, but it has a small discharge tube that runs down toward the floor. If that valve is weeping or discharging, water can collect at the base of the unit and look like a bottom leak.

**How to check:** Follow the discharge tube from the T&P valve. If it is damp or dripping from the end of the pipe near the floor, the valve is the culprit.

**Why this matters:** A T&P valve that constantly drips means your tank pressure or temperature is too high, which is a safety concern. Do not ignore this one.

**Fix:** A replacement T&P valve costs $15 to $30. A plumber charges $100 to $200 to replace it, including testing. Per [CPSC guidelines](https://www.cpsc.gov/), T&P valves should be tested annually and replaced every 3 to 5 years even if they are not visibly leaking.

### 3. A Corroded or Cracked Tank

This is the worst-case scenario and the most expensive. Water heater tanks are lined with a protective glass coating, but that lining degrades over time, especially when sediment builds up at the bottom. Once rust gets through the steel tank walls, water seeps out from the bottom seam or through pinholes.

**How to check:** Look for rust-colored water or a crust of minerals around the base. If the puddle forms directly under the tank body, not near a valve or fitting, corrosion is likely.

**Fix:** There is no repair for a corroded tank. This is a full replacement. The average cost to replace a 40- to 50-gallon traditional tank water heater is $900 to $1,500 installed. If you upgrade to a tankless unit, expect $2,000 to $4,500 installed.

### 4. Loose or Failing Inlet and Outlet Connections

Cold water enters and hot water leaves through fittings at the top of the tank, but over time these connections can corrode or loosen. When they drip, water runs down the side of the unit and pools at the bottom, making it look like a bottom leak.

**How to check:** Dry the tank completely, then look at the pipes entering and exiting from the top. If you see moisture running down the side of the tank from fittings at the top, you have found your source.

**Fix:** Tighten the fittings or replace a corroded nipple (the short pipe section connecting the fitting to the tank). Materials cost $5 to $25. A plumber visit runs $100 to $200.

### 5. Sediment Buildup Causing Internal Damage

Hard water leaves calcium and magnesium deposits that settle at the bottom of the tank. Over years, this layer of sediment overheats because the burner cannot transfer heat through it efficiently. That overheating accelerates corrosion and can crack the tank lining from the inside.

**Warning signs:** Popping, rumbling, or banging sounds from the water heater are almost always sediment. If you have heard those sounds for months and now see a leak, corrosion is very likely.

**Prevention:** The [Department of Energy recommends](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/do-it-yourself-savings-project-insulate-hot-water-pipes) flushing your water heater annually to remove sediment and extend tank life.

## How to Diagnose Your Leak: A Step-by-Step Process

1. **Dry everything** around the base and place paper towels to confirm there is actual water.
2. **Check the top first.** Are the inlet or outlet connections dripping? Water runs down.
3. **Check the T&P discharge pipe.** Is the floor near the end of that pipe wet?
4. **Inspect the drain valve.** Is it dripping from the threaded opening?
5. **Check the tank body itself.** If the wetness is coming from the tank seams or the bottom of the steel itself, that is an internal failure.

## Water Heater Repair vs. Replacement: When to Call It

| Situation | Repair or Replace? | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Dripping drain valve | Repair | $10 to $150 |
| Leaking T&P valve | Repair | $15 to $200 |
| Loose inlet/outlet fittings | Repair | $5 to $200 |
| Corroded tank body | Replace | $900 to $2,500 |
| Tank older than 10 years, any leak | Usually replace | $900 to $2,500 |
| Rusty hot water from taps | Replace | $900 to $2,500 |

**General rule:** If your water heater is more than 10 to 12 years old and is leaking, replacement almost always makes more financial sense than repair. A new unit will be more efficient, and you will avoid being caught with no hot water after paying for a band-aid fix.

## How Long Do Water Heaters Last?

| Water Heater Type | Average Lifespan |
|---|---|
| Traditional tank (gas) | 8 to 12 years |
| Traditional tank (electric) | 10 to 15 years |
| Tankless (gas or electric) | 20 to 25 years |
| Heat pump water heater | 10 to 15 years |

The [ENERGY STAR program](https://www.energystar.gov/products/water_heaters) notes that upgrading to a certified heat pump water heater or tankless model can cut water heating costs by 30 to 50 percent compared to a standard electric tank unit.

## What It Costs to Replace a Water Heater in 2026

Replacement costs vary significantly by region, unit type, and whether any code upgrades are required.

| Unit Type | Unit Cost | Installation | Total Range |

**Regional differences:** Installation costs tend to be higher in California, New York, and the Pacific Northwest due to labor costs and local code requirements. Some states also require earthquake strapping or expansion tanks, which add $50 to $200 to the job.

**Tax credit:** If you install a qualifying heat pump water heater, you may be eligible for a 30 percent federal tax credit (up to $600 per year) through the Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit. See [IRS Form 5695](https://www.irs.gov/forms-pubs/about-form-5695) for details.

## Emergency Steps If Your Water Heater Is Actively Leaking

If water is gushing or pooling fast, take these steps before calling a plumber:

1. **Turn off the cold water supply.** There is a shutoff valve on the cold water pipe entering the top of the tank. Turn it clockwise to close it. This stops new water from entering the tank.
2. **Turn off the power or gas.** For electric heaters, switch off the breaker. For gas heaters, turn the gas valve to the pilot position or off.
3. **Do not drain the tank yourself** unless you know what you are doing. Hot water under pressure can scald you seriously.
4. **Call a licensed plumber.** An emergency call will cost more (often $150 to $300 extra for after-hours service), but a flooding basement or utility room can cost thousands in water damage.

For water damage restoration after a tank failure, the [Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification (IICRC)](https://www.iicrc.org/) recommends professional drying within 24 to 48 hours to prevent mold growth.

## Can You Fix a Water Heater Leak Yourself?

For a drain valve replacement or tightening loose fittings, yes. These are low-risk tasks most homeowners can handle with basic tools. For anything involving the T&P valve, gas connections, or tank replacement, most states require a licensed plumber and a permit. Doing unpermitted water heater work can void your homeowner's insurance and create liability if something goes wrong later.

The [National Association of Home Builders](https://www.nahb.org/) recommends getting at least two quotes from licensed plumbers before agreeing to a full replacement. Prices on water heater installations can vary by $500 or more for the same job.

## How to Find a Reliable Plumber

When calling plumbers for a water heater issue, ask these questions:
- Are you licensed and insured in my state? (Check your state's contractor licensing board to verify.)
- Will you pull the required permit for a replacement?
- Do you warranty the installation, and for how long?
- Is the quote in writing, with parts and labor broken out?

Avoid any contractor who pushes you to skip the permit to save time or money. Permitted work is inspected, and the inspection protects you.

## Summary

A water heater leaking from the bottom is not always a catastrophe, but it needs attention today, not next week. Start by identifying the source: drain valve drips and T&P valve issues are cheap and easy fixes. A leaking tank body is a replacement job. If your unit is a decade old and showing any signs of rust or mineral buildup, use this leak as your signal to replace it before the tank fails completely and you end up with a flooded utility room.
