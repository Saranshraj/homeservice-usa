Professional dryer vent cleaning costs **$100 to $175** for most single-family homes, with the national average around **$130 to $150**. If your vent run is longer than 25 feet, has multiple bends, or hasn't been cleaned in years, expect to pay **$175 to $300**. That price buys you real peace of mind: clogged dryer vents cause an estimated **15,970 home fires every year**, according to the [U.S. Fire Administration](https://www.usfa.fema.gov/), making this one of the most cost-effective safety services you can schedule.

This guide breaks down exactly what drives the price, how often you need cleaning, the red flags that mean do not wait, and whether DIY is ever a smart option.

| Service | Typical Cost Range |
|---|---|
| Standard vent cleaning (up to 25 ft) | $100 to $175 |
| Extended vent run (25 to 50 ft) | $150 to $250 |

Most homeowners with a standard setup, a relatively short straight vent run to an exterior wall, will pay around **$125 to $150** and be done in 30 to 45 minutes.

## What Drives the Cost

### Vent Length and Route Complexity

The single biggest factor is how far the dryer exhaust has to travel to reach the outside. A dryer on an exterior wall with a 6-foot duct run is a quick job. A second-floor laundry room with a 35-foot run through the interior of the house, three 90-degree elbows, and a rooftop exhaust cap is a much harder clean. Technicians charge more for each additional duct section and each direction change because airflow restriction builds up faster and the equipment takes longer to run.

**Rule of thumb from the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/):** every 90-degree elbow in a dryer duct adds the equivalent of 5 feet to the effective duct length for airflow purposes. A code-compliant maximum run is typically 25 feet (adjusted for elbows).

### How Long Since the Last Cleaning

If your vents have not been cleaned in three to five years, there is likely significant lint compaction that takes more passes to clear. Some techs will charge a flat rate for this; others add a "heavy buildup" surcharge of $25 to $75.

### Obstructions

Birds, squirrels, and mice sometimes build nests inside dryer vent terminations, especially during spring. Removing a nest and clearing the blockage adds $50 to $150 to the job, depending on the size and how far back the obstruction goes.

## What the Service Actually Includes

A professional dryer vent cleaning typically covers:

- **Visual inspection** of the exterior termination cap and the duct connection behind the dryer
- **Brush and vacuum cleaning** using a rotary brush system and industrial vacuum
- **Airflow test** before and after to confirm the restriction is cleared
- **Lint trap housing cleaning** (the slot the lint screen slides into, which most homeowners never clean)
- **Reconnecting the duct** if it was disconnected to clean

A quality technician will also tell you if they spot crushed, kinked, or improperly terminated ductwork while they are in there. That inspection feedback alone is worth the service call.

## How Often Should You Clean Your Dryer Vent?

**For most households:** once per year is the standard recommendation. The [Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC)](https://www.cpsc.gov/) recommends annual cleaning for all dryer types.

You may need more frequent cleaning, every 6 months, if:

- You run more than 5 loads of laundry per week
- You frequently dry heavy items (towels, jeans, bedding)
- You have a long or complex duct run
- Your dryer is in a high-humidity area
- You have pets that shed heavily

Households with minimal laundry output and a short duct run can sometimes safely stretch to every 18 to 24 months, but annual is a safe default that most HVAC and appliance technicians recommend.

## Warning Signs Your Dryer Vent Is Clogged Right Now

Do not wait for the annual appointment if you notice any of these:

- **Clothes take two cycles to dry** or feel damp after a full cycle
- **The outside of the dryer is unusually hot** to the touch
- **A burning or musty smell** during the drying cycle
- **The laundry room feels humid or warm** while the dryer is running
- **The exterior vent flap barely opens** or stays shut while the dryer is on
- **More than a year has passed** since the last cleaning

That burning smell is the most urgent warning. It often means lint has already begun to overheat. Stop using the dryer and schedule a cleaning the same day if possible.

## The Real Fire Risk (and Why This Is Not Optional)

According to the [National Fire Protection Association (NFPA)](https://www.nfpa.org/), dryers and washing machines accounted for **1 in 22 home structure fires** between 2016 and 2020. Of dryer fires specifically, **34% were caused by a failure to clean** the vent or lint trap. That statistic holds steady year after year.

The math is simple: a $130 annual cleaning versus a dryer fire that averages **$10,600 in property damage** and, more importantly, puts your family at risk.

The risk goes up in certain situations:

- **Gas dryers** produce combustion gases in addition to heat. A clogged vent can cause carbon monoxide to back-draft into the home.
- **Multi-story homes** with rooftop terminations are harder to inspect visually and tend to accumulate more obstructions.
- **Condos and townhouses** sometimes share common duct chases, making proper termination a code compliance issue, not just a DIY preference.

If you have a gas dryer, the CPSC recommends also installing a [carbon monoxide detector](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Education-Centers/Carbon-Monoxide-Information-Center) near the laundry area.

## DIY vs. Professional Cleaning: When Each Makes Sense

You can buy a dryer vent brush kit for **$15 to $40** at most hardware stores. The DIY route makes sense if:

- Your duct run is short (under 15 feet) and straight
- You can physically access the full length of the duct
- You are comfortable disconnecting and reconnecting the duct at the wall

**DIY limitations to know:**

- A standard brush kit may not have enough reach for long runs
- Rotating brush kits (drill-powered) are more effective but still can't match the suction power of a professional-grade vacuum
- If you push lint inward instead of pulling it out, you can compact the clog further
- You will not catch kinked ductwork, disconnected joints, or bird nests behind walls

**When to always hire a professional:**

- The duct run is longer than 20 feet or has more than two elbows
- You've never had it professionally cleaned
- You suspect an obstruction (nest, crushed duct)
- Your home has a rooftop or high exterior termination you cannot safely access
- The dryer is gas-powered

For most homeowners, professional cleaning every year or two, combined with DIY lint trap housing cleaning every few months, is the right balance.

## How to Clean the Lint Trap Housing (What You're Probably Missing)

Most people clean the lint screen before every load. Very few clean the housing it slides into. Over time, a film of fabric softener and fine lint coats the inside of that slot, restricting airflow even when the screen itself looks clean.

Every 1 to 3 months:
1. Pull out the lint screen and set it aside.
2. Use a long, narrow brush (a "lint trap brush" costs $5 to $10) to reach into the housing slot.
3. Vacuum up what you loosen.
4. Wash the lint screen itself with dish soap and warm water, let it dry completely, and replace it.

This small habit meaningfully extends the time between full vent cleanings.

## What Duct Material Matters for Safety

If your home was built before 1990, your dryer vent may be made of flexible plastic or foil accordion duct. Both materials are no longer code-compliant for dryer exhaust in most jurisdictions because they sag, trap lint, and can melt or ignite.

**Current code-compliant standard (per [NFPA 211](https://www.nfpa.org/) and most local building codes):** smooth-wall rigid metal duct, typically 4 inches in diameter.

If a technician identifies plastic or flexible foil duct during a cleaning, ask for a quote to replace it with rigid metal. The upgrade costs **$150 to $400** depending on duct length and accessibility, and it is worth doing.

## Finding a Reputable Dryer Vent Cleaner

Dryer vent cleaning is a lightly regulated industry. Use these criteria to vet a contractor:

- **Certification:** Look for a [CSIA (Chimney Safety Institute of America)](https://www.csia.org/) certified technician or a company affiliated with the [Air Duct Cleaning Association (NADCA)](https://www.nadca.com/). Both require training and continuing education.
- **Before-and-after airflow test:** Any reputable tech will show you the airflow measurement has improved.
- **No high-pressure upsell:** A legitimate technician will point out problems they find. A scammy one will claim your entire duct system needs replacement without showing you evidence.
- **Get a written quote upfront:** The price should not more than double between the initial quote and the invoice unless they found a genuine obstruction.

Avoid any company that quotes $49 "whole-house duct cleaning" specials. Those deals almost always involve low-effort service with a hard upsell for additional services you may not need. Legitimate dryer vent cleaning alone costs $100 or more.

## Bundling to Save Money

If you are already scheduling an HVAC technician for your annual furnace tune-up or air duct cleaning, ask about bundling dryer vent cleaning. Many companies offer **$20 to $50 off** when you add dryer vent cleaning to an existing appointment, since the technician is already on site.

Similarly, if you have a gas dryer, ask your HVAC tech or gas appliance service company about checking the dryer exhaust connection at the appliance itself during your annual gas appliance checkup.
