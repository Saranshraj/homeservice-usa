Replacing an exterior door costs **$800 to $2,500 installed** for most US homeowners. A standard steel entry door runs $700 to $1,300 all-in; fiberglass doors land at $900 to $1,800; and solid wood or custom doors push from $2,000 to $5,000 or higher. Labor alone is typically $200 to $500 for a straightforward swap-out. If your old door drafts badly, sticks in summer, or looks worn down, this is one of the better home investments you can make: the [National Association of REALTORS](https://www.nar.realtor/) consistently ranks entry door replacement among the top projects for resale return.

---

## Why This Upgrade Actually Makes Sense

Your front door is the first thing visitors see and the first line of defense against weather, intruders, and high energy bills. An older door with worn weatherstripping or a damaged frame lets in drafts you can feel in November. According to the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/doors), air leaks around doors and windows account for up to 30 percent of a home's heating and cooling energy use. Replacing a leaky door pays you back over time.

Beyond energy savings, a new front door can genuinely change how your home feels from the street. You do not need a $4,000 custom wood door to get a great result. A solid fiberglass door in the right style can look beautiful, last 30 years, and save you money on heating.

## What You're Really Paying For: The Biggest Cost Drivers

### 1. Door Material

**Steel doors** are the most budget-friendly and the most durable. They resist dents better than you might expect, hold up against rust when properly maintained, and are excellent insulators when filled with polyurethane foam. Most steel exterior doors qualify for [ENERGY STAR certification](https://www.energystar.gov/products/building_products/residential_doors), meaning they meet strict energy performance standards.

**Fiberglass doors** cost more upfront but deliver a premium look. High-end fiberglass can be stained or painted to mimic real wood grain without the warping and swelling problems wood causes in humid climates. They are lighter than steel and very low maintenance. If you live somewhere with big humidity swings, like the Southeast or Pacific Northwest, fiberglass is often the smartest choice.

**Wood doors** are beautiful and traditional, but they require the most maintenance. Real wood swells in humidity, shrinks in cold, and needs repainting or restaining every few years. In a stable climate like Arizona or Southern California, wood holds up well. In the Southeast or Midwest, wood doors can frustrate you with seasonal sticking.

### 2. Frame Condition

If your door frame (the jamb and threshold) is rotted, you are looking at extra work. Frame repair or replacement adds $150 to $500 to the job. Many contractors will flag this during their quote visit. Do not skip this step. A beautiful new door on a rotted frame will not seal properly and will start failing within a few years.

### 3. Door Size and Configuration

Standard door widths are 32 or 36 inches. Anything non-standard costs more, both for the door itself and for labor. Sidelights (narrow glass panels on either side of the door) add $200 to $600. A transom window above the door adds another $150 to $500. French doors (a pair of doors) roughly double the door cost and add labor time.

### 4. Glass and Design Features

Plain panel doors cost less than doors with decorative glass or intricate woodwork. A basic steel door with no glass can be under $400 at home improvement stores. Add a privacy glass insert and you are looking at $100 to $300 more. Full-length glass panels or decorative wrought iron detailing push costs higher, sometimes significantly.

### 5. Hardware and Locks

Hardware is not always included. A basic handle-and-deadbolt set runs $50 to $200. Smart locks add $150 to $400. High-security multipoint locking systems (common on European-style doors) start around $300. If you want to match existing hardware finishes on your home, factor that into your budget.

## Labor Costs: What Contractors Actually Charge

Most door replacement projects take 2 to 4 hours for a skilled carpenter or handyman. Labor rates vary by region.

| Region | Typical Labor Rate | Basic Swap (2-3 hrs) |
|---|---|---|
| Midwest/South | $50 to $80/hr | $150 to $300 |
| Northeast | $75 to $120/hr | $225 to $480 |
| West Coast | $80 to $140/hr | $240 to $560 |
| Major metros (NYC, LA, SF) | $100 to $160/hr | $300 to $640 |

A basic door swap means removing the old door, installing a new pre-hung unit in the same opening, shimming for plumb and level, and installing new trim if needed. If the opening needs to be resized or structural work is involved, labor costs jump significantly.

## DIY vs. Hiring a Pro: The Honest Breakdown

This is one project where your skill level matters a lot. Here is the reality.

**DIY works well if:**
- You are replacing a door with a pre-hung unit in the same size opening
- The frame is in good condition
- You have basic carpentry tools and experience
- You can rent a door installation kit from a home improvement store

**Hire a pro if:**
- The frame is rotted or out of square
- You are changing the door opening size
- You are installing a heavy custom door or a complex multi-panel unit
- You want a warranty on the installation work

A poorly installed door that is not properly shimmed and sealed will leak air and water. The cost of fixing a bad DIY installation often exceeds what you would have paid a contractor in the first place. [The Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/weatherize/air-sealing-your-home) notes that proper sealing is critical to capturing energy savings, and that means precise installation.

## Signs You Need a New Exterior Door (Not Just Repairs)

Sometimes caulk, weatherstripping, and a fresh coat of paint are enough. But here are signs a full replacement makes more sense.

- **Visible daylight around the frame** when the door is closed, even after weatherstripping replacement
- **Sticking or dragging** that cannot be fixed by tightening hinges or planing the edge (can indicate a settling foundation)
- **Soft spots, rot, or discoloration** on wood doors that extend deep into the core
- **Significant denting or bowing** on steel or fiberglass that affects the seal
- **High energy bills** combined with a door that is more than 20 years old
- **Security concerns** after a break-in attempt or if the lock mortise is damaged
- **Major aesthetic mismatch** if you are preparing to sell

## Regional Price Differences Across the US

Costs vary meaningfully by state. A mid-grade fiberglass entry door replacement that costs $1,100 in the Midwest might run $1,600 in California or $1,800 in New York City. This difference reflects labor rates, permit requirements, and local contractor demand.

Some states require permits for structural changes to exterior openings, though a same-size door swap typically does not trigger this. Check with your local building department if you are changing opening sizes or converting a window to a door. Unpermitted work can complicate home sales later.

**Warm climates (FL, TX, AZ):** Fiberglass and steel both hold up well. Hurricane-rated doors in Florida can add $500 to $1,500 to total cost. Look for doors with impact resistance ratings if you are in a hurricane zone.

**Cold climates (MN, WI, MI, upstate NY):** Insulation value (U-factor) matters more. Aim for a door with a U-factor of 0.30 or lower. [ENERGY STAR certified doors](https://www.energystar.gov/products/building_products/residential_doors/key_product_criteria) meet this threshold and may qualify for a federal tax credit.

**High humidity (Southeast, Pacific Northwest):** Fiberglass outperforms wood significantly in humid conditions. Steel with proper painting holds up too.

## Federal Tax Credits and Local Incentives

Under the Inflation Reduction Act, homeowners may claim a tax credit of up to 30 percent of the cost of qualifying exterior doors, capped at $250 per door and $500 total per year. Doors must be ENERGY STAR certified. Check the [IRS guidance on energy efficiency credits](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/energy-efficient-home-improvement-credit) and save your receipts and the manufacturer's certification.

Many utilities also offer rebates for energy-efficient doors. The [ENERGY STAR rebate finder](https://www.energystar.gov/rebate-finder) lets you search by zip code. A $50 to $200 utility rebate is not unusual in states with aggressive efficiency programs.

## How to Get an Accurate Quote

Do not guess at costs. Get at least three quotes and make sure each contractor is giving you the same scope: same door product or equivalent, same labor, same hardware, and clarity on whether they will repair the frame if needed.

**Questions to ask your contractor:**
- Is the frame in good condition, or will there be additional repair costs?
- Does your quote include new interior trim?
- Is weatherstripping and threshold included?
- Do you handle permits if required?
- What is the warranty on your labor?

A reputable door installation contractor will measure the opening, check the frame, and give you a written quote. Be cautious of contractors who quote over the phone without seeing the job.

The [National Association of Home Builders](https://www.nahb.org/) and local homebuilder associations can help you find vetted contractors in your area.

## Thinking About Curb Appeal? Color Matters

Front door color has a measurable effect on first impressions, and a bold color on a quality door costs nothing extra. Deep navy, black, forest green, and classic red consistently perform well. If you are planning to sell within a few years, neutral and classic choices appeal to the widest range of buyers.

One practical tip: prime the door before painting, especially steel and fiberglass. Skipping primer causes paint to peel faster, especially in high-UV climates.
