US homeowners can claim **up to $3,200 in federal tax credits** on eligible energy-efficiency improvements every single year through at least 2032, and there is **no dollar cap** on the 30 percent credit for solar panels, battery storage, or geothermal systems. These are not deductions that reduce your taxable income. They are dollar-for-dollar reductions in the taxes you owe. If you owe $2,500 in federal income tax and you qualify for a $2,500 credit, your bill goes to zero. The credits were extended and expanded by the Inflation Reduction Act in 2022, and many homeowners are leaving thousands of dollars unclaimed simply because they do not know what qualifies.

This guide walks you through every major federal home improvement tax credit available to you in 2026, what each one covers, how much you can get back, and exactly how to claim it when you file. We also cover the state-level programs that can stack on top, because in some states the combined savings are genuinely jaw-dropping.

---

## The Two Main Federal Credits to Know

There are two separate federal tax credit programs that cover most home energy upgrades:

1. **The Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit** (IRS Section 25C): Up to $3,200 per year for insulation, windows, doors, heat pumps, and HVAC equipment.
2. **The Residential Clean Energy Credit** (IRS Section 25D): 30 percent of costs, no annual cap, for solar panels, solar water heaters, battery storage, and geothermal.

These are separate credits. You can claim both in the same tax year.

## Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (Section 25C)

This is the one that applies to most common home improvements. The credit is worth **30 percent of your costs**, but it comes with annual caps that limit how much you can claim in a single year. The structure is a little complicated, so here is the breakdown.

### The Annual Caps

The base annual limit is **$1,200 per year** across most categories. However, heat pumps and heat pump water heaters have their own separate annual limit of **$2,000**. That means in a year when you install a qualifying heat pump AND upgrade your windows, you could claim up to **$3,200 total**.

Important: the $1,200 and $2,000 limits reset every year. Unlike older versions of this credit, there is no lifetime cap. You can claim up to the annual maximum every year through 2032.

### What Qualifies Under the $1,200 Annual Cap

| Improvement | Credit Amount | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Exterior windows and skylights | 30% of cost, up to $600 | Must meet ENERGY STAR Most Efficient criteria |
| Exterior doors | 30% of cost, up to $500 total ($250 per door) | Must meet ENERGY STAR requirements |
| Insulation and air sealing | 30% of cost, up to $1,200 | Materials only, not labor costs |
| Home energy audits | 30% of cost, up to $150 | Must be performed by a certified auditor |
| Central AC, gas/oil/propane furnace | 30% of cost, up to $600 | Must meet efficiency thresholds |
| Electric panels and wiring (when required for efficiency upgrades) | 30% of cost, up to $600 | Only when required for another qualifying upgrade |

### The Separate $2,000 Cap for Heat Pumps

| Improvement | Credit Amount |
|---|---|
| Air-source heat pumps | 30% of cost, up to $2,000 |
| Heat pump water heaters | 30% of cost, up to $2,000 |
| Biomass stoves and boilers | 30% of cost, up to $2,000 |

A qualifying [heat pump installation](/blog/heat-pump-installation-cost/) that costs $6,500 would yield a $1,950 credit, right near the maximum. A [tankless water heater](/blog/tankless-water-heater-installation-cost/) that runs on a heat pump mechanism and costs $2,000 installed would give you a $600 credit.

### Efficiency Requirements Matter

Not every product qualifies. The IRS requires products to meet specific energy efficiency standards, which are generally tied to ENERGY STAR certification or higher performance tiers.

For windows and skylights, you need [ENERGY STAR Most Efficient](https://www.energystar.gov/products/windows_doors_skylights) products, which is a step above standard ENERGY STAR. For doors, standard ENERGY STAR certification is enough. For heat pumps and HVAC equipment, the IRS and DOE publish specific efficiency thresholds on the [Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit page at IRS.gov](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/energy-efficient-home-improvement-credit).

When you are shopping for any of these upgrades, ask your contractor for the manufacturer's certification statement. You will need this document to support your tax credit claim. Keep it with your tax records.

## Residential Clean Energy Credit (Section 25D)

This is the solar credit, and it is remarkably generous. There is no annual dollar cap. You get back **30 percent of the total cost**, including installation, for any qualifying clean energy system.

### What Qualifies

| System | Credit | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Solar electric panels (photovoltaic) | 30% of total cost | Includes installation labor |
| Solar water heaters | 30% of total cost | Must provide at least 50% of home's water heating |
| Small wind energy systems | 30% of total cost | Turbines installed on your property |
| Geothermal heat pumps | 30% of total cost | Must meet Energy Star requirements |
| Battery storage systems | 30% of total cost | 3 kWh minimum capacity, as of 2023 |
| Fuel cells | 30% of total cost | $500 max per half kilowatt of capacity |

The credit rate holds at 30 percent through 2032, drops to 26 percent in 2033, then 22 percent in 2034, and expires after that unless Congress extends it again.

### Solar Credit Example

A [solar panel installation](/blog/solar-panel-installation-cost/) that costs $22,000 fully installed would generate a **$6,600 federal tax credit**. If you do not have enough tax liability to use the full credit in one year, it carries forward to future tax years. It does not expire unused (within the credit's life).

Pairing solar with a qualifying battery system? The batteries are covered too, so a $22,000 solar plus $8,000 battery combination yields a **$9,000 credit** on a $30,000 total investment.

## Alternative Fuel Vehicle Refueling Credit (Section 30C)

If you installed an [EV charger at home](/blog/ev-charger-installation-cost/), you may qualify for a separate credit covering 30 percent of the cost, up to **$1,000 for personal vehicles**. This credit was extended through 2032 by the Inflation Reduction Act.

The charger must be located in a low-income census tract or a non-urban area to qualify after 2022 (a restriction added by the IRA). For most suburban and rural homeowners, the geographic requirement is likely satisfied. For urban homeowners in higher-income areas, check IRS eligibility guidance at [irs.gov](https://www.irs.gov/credits-deductions/alternative-fuel-vehicle-refueling-property-credit) or consult a tax professional.

A Level 2 home charger costing $1,500 to $2,500 installed could yield a **$450 to $750 credit** if you qualify.

## Stacking State Credits on Top

Federal credits are just the starting point. Many states offer their own programs that stack on top of federal savings.

### States with Strong Additional Programs

**California**: The SGIP (Self Generation Incentive Program) provides rebates on home battery storage, ranging from **$0.20 to $0.25 per watt-hour**. A 10 kWh battery that qualifies could add $2,000 to $2,500 in state rebates on top of the 30 percent federal credit.

**New York**: The NY-Sun program offers incentives up to **$1,000 per kilowatt** of solar installed for residential customers. Combined with the federal 30 percent credit and New York's 25 percent state credit (capped at $5,000), New Yorkers often see 50 percent or more of solar costs covered.

**Massachusetts**: The SMART program provides ongoing monthly incentive payments for solar production for 10 to 20 years, plus a state tax credit of 15 percent of costs up to $1,000.

**Illinois**: The Illinois Shines program offers solar renewable energy credits (SRECs) that homeowners can sell for ongoing income after installation.

**Colorado**: State income tax credit of 30 percent for geothermal heat pump systems, which stacks directly on the federal 30 percent credit for a combined 60 percent subsidy.

For a complete list of state programs in your area, the [Database of State Incentives for Renewables and Efficiency at DSIRE](https://www.dsireusa.org/) maintained by N.C. State University is the most comprehensive resource available.

### Utility Company Rebates

Your electric or gas utility may also offer rebates that apply before you get to tax credits. These rebates reduce your out-of-pocket cost, which reduces the base the tax credit is calculated on, but they often still produce a net benefit. Contact your utility company directly or check their website's energy efficiency programs page.

## The Inflation Reduction Act's Rebate Programs (HEEHRA and HOMES)

Separate from the tax credits, the IRA created two rebate programs that states are rolling out through 2026 and beyond:

**HOMES Rebates (Home Owner Managing Energy Savings)**: Up to **$8,000** in rebates based on measured energy savings from whole-home efficiency upgrades. Income-based limits apply, with lower-income households qualifying for higher rebates.

**HEEHRA (High Efficiency Electric Home Rebate Act)**: Up to **$14,000** in point-of-sale rebates for electrification upgrades including heat pump HVAC, heat pump water heaters, electric cooking, and EV chargers. Eligibility is income-based. Households below 80 percent of area median income qualify for the full rebate; households between 80 and 150 percent qualify for 50 percent.

These programs are administered by states and rollout timing varies. The [DOE's rebate program page](https://www.energy.gov/scep/home-energy-rebates-programs) lists which states have active programs and how to apply.

## How to Claim: Form 5695

You claim these credits using **IRS Form 5695** when you file your federal income tax return. The process is straightforward.

**Step 1**: Gather your receipts and invoices for all qualifying improvements made during the tax year.

**Step 2**: Collect the manufacturer's certification statements for each product (your contractor should provide these, or you can download them from the manufacturer's website).

**Step 3**: Complete Form 5695 and attach it to your Form 1040. Tax software like TurboTax, H&R Block, or FreeTaxUSA walks you through this automatically.

**Step 4**: The calculated credit flows to Line 5 of Schedule 3, which then reduces your total tax liability on Form 1040.

If the credit exceeds your tax liability for the year, the unused portion of the Residential Clean Energy Credit (25D) carries forward. The Energy Efficient Home Improvement Credit (25C) does not carry forward, so plan larger projects around years when you expect higher tax liability.

## Common Mistakes Homeowners Make

**Assuming renter improvements qualify**: These credits apply only to your **primary residence** for the 25C credit (principal residence). The 25D credit also applies to a second home in some cases, but not rental properties you do not personally use.

**Forgetting about the annual cap reset**: Many homeowners miss the fact that the $1,200 and $2,000 annual limits reset each year. If you replaced your windows in 2025, you can still claim the credit for insulation upgrades in 2026.

**Not keeping the manufacturer certification statement**: Without this document, the IRS has no way to verify the product meets efficiency requirements. Ask for it before installation and keep it in a dedicated tax folder.

**Skipping the energy audit before big projects**: A [home energy audit](/blog/home-energy-audit-cost/) that costs $200 to $600 qualifies for a $150 credit AND can identify which upgrades will save the most money, helping you sequence improvements for maximum tax benefit over multiple years.

**Confusing rebates and credits**: A utility rebate that reduces your purchase price also reduces the cost basis for the tax credit. But it is almost always still worth taking both: the math consistently favors combining them.
