A slab leak costs most homeowners between **$2,000 and $10,000** to fully fix, though simple repairs using epoxy lining can run as low as $500 to $1,500. If the leak has caused water damage to your floors, walls, or foundation, total project costs can climb to $15,000 or more. The wide range comes down to one thing: how you choose to repair the pipe, and how long the leak has been running.

This is one of those problems that gets worse the longer you wait. If your floors feel warm in spots, your water bill jumped without explanation, or you hear water running when every faucet in the house is off, you may already have a slab leak. Here is exactly what to expect when you pick up the phone to call a plumber.

## What Is a Slab Leak?

A slab leak happens when a water supply pipe or drain line develops a hole or crack beneath the concrete foundation slab of your home. Because the pipes are buried in or under concrete, even a small pinhole leak can go undetected for months while water slowly erodes your foundation, breeds mold under your floors, and runs up your water bill.

Slab leaks are especially common in:

- Homes built before 1990 with copper pipes (copper corrodes over time, especially in areas with hard, chlorinated, or high-pH water)
- States with expansive clay soils like Texas, Oklahoma, and Louisiana (soil movement stresses pipes)
- Florida, Arizona, and California homes where soil conditions and older housing stock create ideal conditions for pipe corrosion

## Slab Leak Detection Costs: $150 to $600

Before any repair can happen, a plumber needs to find the exact location of the leak. Guessing is not an option when you are dealing with concrete. Plumbers use specialized equipment including electronic amplification devices, infrared cameras, and helium tracing to pinpoint the leak without tearing up your floor unnecessarily.

| Detection Method | Average Cost |
|---|---|
| Electronic leak detection | $150 to $350 |
| Infrared/thermal imaging | $200 to $500 |
| Helium leak tracing | $300 to $600 |
| Full diagnostic with multiple methods | $300 to $600 |

Most reputable plumbing companies will apply the detection fee toward the repair if you hire them for the job. Always ask upfront.

## Slab Leak Repair Costs by Method

This is where costs vary the most. There are four main ways to repair a slab leak, and the right one depends on your pipe material, the leak's location, the age of your plumbing, and your budget.

### Method 1: Spot Repair (Jackhammer Access)

**Cost: $500 to $2,000**

The plumber jackhammers a section of your slab to expose the leaking pipe, fixes or replaces that section, then fills the concrete back in. This is the least expensive method for a single, accessible leak, but it is also the most disruptive. You will need to restore whatever flooring sits above the repair area, which adds $300 to $2,000 depending on tile, hardwood, or carpet.

Best for: A single small leak in an accessible location, newer plumbing that is otherwise in good condition.

### Method 2: Pipe Rerouting (Above-Slab)

**Cost: $2,000 to $5,000**

Instead of digging into your slab, the plumber runs brand-new copper or PEX pipe through your walls, attic, or crawl space, bypassing the damaged underground section entirely. The old pipe is simply abandoned in place. This avoids concrete work and is often the preferred choice for older homes where the pipes are likely to develop more leaks in the future.

Best for: Homes with aging pipe systems, multiple leaks, or when slab access is difficult due to tile or finished flooring you want to preserve.

### Method 3: Epoxy Pipe Lining (Trenchless)

**Cost: $1,500 to $4,000**

A flexible epoxy-coated liner is threaded into the existing pipe and cured in place, essentially creating a new pipe inside the old one. This is the least invasive method and does not require any demolition, but it only works in certain pipe sizes and configurations. Not every plumbing company offers this service.

Best for: Drain lines and certain supply lines where the pipe interior is accessible from each end, and the pipe is not too corroded or collapsed.

### Method 4: Tunneling (Under-Slab Excavation)

**Cost: $4,000 to $12,000**

Plumbers hand-dig a tunnel beneath your slab from outside or through the garage, then work on the pipe from below without cutting through your floors at all. It preserves your interior floors completely, which makes it the premium option for homeowners with expensive tile, marble, or hardwood.

Best for: High-end finished floors you cannot afford to disturb, or when the leak is under a finished area of the home.

## Signs You Have a Slab Leak

Knowing these warning signs can save you thousands of dollars because early detection means less water damage to remediate.

**Strong indicators:**
- Your water meter is spinning even when every faucet, toilet, and appliance is off
- One section of your floor, usually tile or hardwood, is noticeably warm to your bare feet (hot water line leak)
- Your water bill jumped $50 to $200 more than usual with no change in usage
- You can hear the sound of running or trickling water through your floors or walls when nothing is on
- Wet spots, damp patches, or soft areas appearing in your flooring
- Mold or a musty smell coming from your floors or baseboards
- Cracks forming in your walls, especially near the floor

**What to do right now:** Turn off every water fixture in your home, then go outside and look at your water meter. If the dial is moving, water is escaping somewhere. This is the fastest way to confirm you have a leak before spending anything on a plumber visit.

## Does Homeowners Insurance Cover Slab Leaks?

This is the question every homeowner wants answered, and the truth is complicated. Homeowners insurance policies vary widely in how they handle slab leaks.

**What is usually covered:**
- Water damage to your floors, walls, drywall, and belongings caused by the leak (the resulting damage)
- Mold remediation in some policies

**What is usually NOT covered:**
- The cost of actually repairing or replacing the damaged pipe (the cause of the damage)
- Foundation repair if the leak has compromised the slab

**Service line coverage:** Some insurers offer a "service line" or "water backup" endorsement that can cover underground pipe repairs. These add-ons typically cost $5 to $15 per month and are absolutely worth considering if you live in a slab home with older plumbing. Ask your insurance agent specifically about service line coverage before you ever need it.

Always document everything before repairs begin: take photos and video, keep all receipts, and file your claim as soon as possible. Delays can complicate coverage.

The Insurance Information Institute (iii.org) has clear guidance on what standard policies cover and what riders are available. Check your policy's declarations page for the words "sudden and accidental" water damage, which is the typical language used for covered pipe leaks.

## Why You Should Never Ignore a Slab Leak

Plumbers will tell you that the homeowners who end up paying the highest bills are the ones who suspected something was wrong and waited. Here is what an ignored slab leak does over weeks and months:

1. **Foundation erosion.** Water continuously washing under your slab erodes the soil support beneath it. Over time, this causes settlement, cracking, and tilting that can cost $10,000 to $30,000 or more to correct.

2. **Mold growth.** The [Environmental Protection Agency](https://www.epa.gov/mold) warns that mold can begin growing within 24 to 48 hours of water intrusion. Once mold gets into your subfloor and wall cavities, remediation costs escalate fast.

3. **Flooring destruction.** Hardwood buckles. Tile grout dissolves. Carpet grows mold. The longer you wait, the more flooring you replace.

4. **Higher water bills.** A moderate slab leak can add $50 to $200 per month to your bill every month it goes undetected.

## Choosing a Plumber for Slab Leak Repair

Slab leaks require specialized skills and equipment that not every plumber has. Here is how to vet contractors before you hire:

- **Ask specifically about slab leak experience.** A plumber who handles slab leaks regularly will have leak detection equipment on their truck or readily available.
- **Get at least three quotes.** For a job this size, the difference between contractors can be $2,000 to $4,000.
- **Verify licensing and insurance.** Every state has a licensing board for plumbers. In Texas, check the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. In California, look up contractors at the [California Contractors State License Board](https://www.cslb.ca.gov). In Florida, check the [Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation](https://www.myfloridalicense.com).
- **Check if they warranty their work.** Reputable plumbers typically offer a one-year warranty on labor. The pipe materials they use (copper or PEX) carry manufacturer warranties of 25 years or more.
- **Ask if they will coordinate with your insurance adjuster.** Good contractors document everything for your claim.

## Can You DIY a Slab Leak Repair?

No, and trying will likely make things worse. Slab leak repair requires jackhammers or tunnel-digging equipment, concrete patching expertise, and licensed plumbing skills. Permits are almost always required, and unpermitted work can void your homeowners insurance coverage and create issues when you sell the home.

The only DIY move worth making is turning off your main water shutoff valve (usually near the street or at the meter) to stop active water damage while you wait for a plumber. The [U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov) recommends every homeowner know the location of their main shutoff before an emergency happens.

## Preventing Future Slab Leaks

If your home had one slab leak, it is worth asking your plumber about the condition of the rest of your pipes. A whole-home repiping in copper or PEX costs $4,000 to $15,000 but eliminates the risk of future slab leaks. Many homeowners choose repiping after a second or third leak rather than continue paying for spot repairs.

Other preventive steps:
- Install a whole-home water softener if you have hard water: hard water accelerates copper pipe corrosion
- Monitor your water bill monthly for unexplained increases
- Ask your plumber to pressure-test your pipes during any major plumbing visit

The [Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov) recommends periodic inspection of your water heating and distribution systems as part of regular home maintenance.
