**The short answer:** Installing a home security system costs **$200 to $1,500** when a professional sets it up, including equipment and labor. Ongoing professional monitoring adds **$10 to $60 per month**. DIY wireless systems, where you handle the setup yourself, cost **$100 to $400** for a starter kit, with optional self-monitoring or professional monitoring on top. Most homeowners who go the professionally installed route spend around **$500 to $800** total in year one, including equipment, installation, and the first few months of monitoring. The good news: a properly installed system can reduce your homeowners insurance premium by **5 to 20 percent**, which offsets a meaningful chunk of the ongoing cost.

If you're reading this, you're probably weighing your options after a break-in on your street, a door left unlocked too long, or the nagging feeling that your family deserves better than a deadbolt and a hope. All valid reasons. This guide gives you the real numbers, a clear breakdown of what you actually get at each price point, and a plain-English comparison of every major system type and brand so you can make a confident decision.

## What a Home Security System Actually Costs

The cost range is wide because "home security system" means very different things. A single door sensor and a basic siren is not the same product as a full wired camera network with 24/7 professional monitoring and smart locks. Here is how the major cost categories break down:

| Cost Category | Typical Range |
|---|---|
| DIY wireless starter kit (3 to 5 pieces) | $100 to $350 |
| Professional installation (labor only) | $100 to $400 |
| Equipment from a pro company (included or bundled) | $0 to $800 |
| Self-monitoring (app only, no monthly fee) | $0 |
| Professional monitoring, basic | $10 to $25/month |
| Professional monitoring, full-featured | $30 to $60/month |
| **Professional install, year one total** | **$400 to $1,500** |
| **DIY install, year one total (with monitoring)** | **$220 to $750** |

## DIY vs. Professional Installation: The Core Decision

This is the first fork in the road, and it matters more than brand choice.

### DIY Systems

DIY security systems, like [Ring Alarm](https://ring.com), [SimpliSafe](https://simplisafe.com), and [Google Nest Secure](https://store.google.com/us/category/security), ship to your door and use your home's existing Wi-Fi. Setup takes 30 to 90 minutes and requires no drilling skills beyond mounting a sensor on a door frame. The sensors use adhesive strips or a single screw.

**Best for:** Renters, apartment dwellers, homeowners who move frequently, anyone on a tight budget, or tech-comfortable homeowners who want flexibility.

**Limitations:** Relies on your Wi-Fi and power grid. If the router goes down or a burglar cuts your power, a basic DIY system may not function. Better systems include cellular backup to address this.

### Professional Installation

Companies like ADT, Brinks, Vivint, and Frontpoint send a technician to install sensors, cameras, keypads, and motion detectors. They handle placement decisions and test every component before they leave.

**Best for:** Homeowners who want it done right the first time, older homes with complex layouts, large properties, or anyone who simply does not want to think about it.

**Limitation:** The upfront cost is higher, and many professional companies require a multi-year monitoring contract. Read the fine print before you sign.

| Factor | DIY | Professional |
|---|---|---|
| Equipment cost | $100 to $350 | $0 to $800 (often bundled) |
| Installation labor | $0 | $100 to $400 |
| Contract required | No | Often 1 to 3 years |
| Cellular backup included | Sometimes (paid tier) | Usually included |
| Smart home integration | Excellent | Good to excellent |
| Equipment ownership | You own it | Sometimes leased |

## Cost Breakdown by System Type

Not all security systems work the same way. The three main architectures each come with different costs and trade-offs.

### Wireless (Most Popular)

Wireless systems use battery-powered sensors and communicate over your Wi-Fi or a proprietary radio frequency (Z-Wave, Zigbee). They are the easiest to install and the most common choice today.

- **Starter kit (5 to 8 pieces):** $150 to $300
- **Additional sensors:** $15 to $50 each
- **Indoor cameras:** $60 to $150 each
- **Outdoor cameras:** $80 to $200 each
- **Video doorbell:** $100 to $250
- **Smart door locks:** $100 to $300 each

A complete wireless setup covering a 1,500 sq ft home typically runs **$300 to $600 in equipment**, plus monitoring.

### Wired (Hardwired Systems)

Wired systems run low-voltage cable from each sensor back to a central control panel. They are more reliable than wireless (no batteries, no Wi-Fi dependency) but require professional installation.

- **Labor:** $250 to $600 for a basic install
- **Full wired system (equipment plus install):** $600 to $1,500
- **Best for:** New construction or major renovations where walls are open

### Hybrid Systems

Many modern professional installs use a hybrid approach: a hardwired main panel and keypad, with wireless sensors and cameras. This gives you the reliability of wired with the flexibility of wireless. Expect to pay **$400 to $1,200** for a professional hybrid install.

## What Drives the Final Cost

### 1. Home Size and Number of Entry Points

A basic system covers front door, back door, and one motion sensor. A thorough setup covers every door, every accessible window, garage entry, and the main interior hallways. Here is a rough guide:

| Home Size | Entry Points to Cover | Estimated Equipment Cost |
|---|---|---|
| Apartment / small home | 3 to 5 | $100 to $250 |
| Medium home (1,500 sq ft) | 6 to 10 | $250 to $500 |
| Large home (2,500 sq ft+) | 10 to 16 | $450 to $900 |
| Large home with outbuildings | 16+ | $700 to $1,500+ |

### 2. Cameras

Cameras are optional on every system but add significant value and cost. An indoor camera runs **$50 to $150**. A weatherproof outdoor camera runs **$80 to $250**. A floodlight camera, which combines a motion-activated floodlight with a wide-angle camera, costs **$150 to $300** per unit.

### 3. Professional Monitoring vs. Self-Monitoring

Self-monitoring means the app alerts your phone when a sensor trips. You decide whether to call 911. There is no monthly fee.

Professional monitoring means a live call center is notified within seconds of an alarm. They attempt to reach you first, then dispatch police, fire, or EMS. This costs **$10 to $60 per month** depending on the plan.

For most homeowners with families and busy schedules, professional monitoring is worth the cost. If you travel frequently or work long hours, the peace of mind it provides is real.

### 4. Cellular Backup

The biggest weakness of any home security system is its dependence on power and internet. A thief who knows what they are doing can cut your Wi-Fi router or trip your circuit breaker before triggering a sensor. A system with cellular backup communicates via a mobile network even when your power and internet are down.

Cellular backup is either included in professional monitoring plans or available as an add-on for **$5 to $10 per month** more. It is worth it.

### 5. Smart Home Integration

If you already have a smart home ecosystem (Amazon Alexa, Google Home, Apple HomeKit), you want a security system that integrates natively. Most modern wireless systems do this well. Paid professional systems vary: ADT and Vivint have proprietary ecosystems that may or may not talk to your existing devices. Check compatibility before you commit.

## State-by-State Considerations

Home security costs are largely consistent across the US, but a few regional factors affect your decision:

**Higher-crime urban areas** (parts of Los Angeles, Chicago, Baltimore, Detroit): Comprehensive systems with cameras are a stronger investment. Consider professional monitoring with cellular backup.

**Hurricane and tornado zones** (Florida, Texas, Gulf Coast, Tornado Alley): Look for systems that include environmental sensors: smoke, CO, flood, and freeze detectors. These add **$20 to $100** to your kit but can prevent catastrophic losses.

**Rural areas with slow response times:** Professional monitoring is more valuable here because police response times may exceed 30 minutes. A system that can also sound a loud local siren (105 dB or higher) deters opportunistic thieves even before police arrive.

**High-wildfire-risk states** (California, Colorado, Oregon, Washington): Smoke and heat sensors are not optional. Look for a system with environmental monitoring built in.

## The Insurance Discount: Real Savings

This is the number most homeowners overlook. According to the [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org), a professionally monitored alarm system can reduce your homeowners insurance premium by **5 to 20 percent**.

On a national average homeowners insurance premium of roughly **$1,400 per year**, that discount is worth **$70 to $280 annually**. A monitoring fee of $20/month ($240/year) can be partially or fully offset by the insurance savings, especially for homeowners in high-risk areas who pay above-average premiums.

To get the discount: after your system is installed, call your insurance company and ask. They will typically want either a certificate from a professional monitoring company or proof that the system is UL (Underwriters Laboratories) listed. Get this documentation when your system is installed.

## DIY Installation: Step-by-Step Overview

A wireless DIY system installation typically goes like this:

1. **Place the base station** (the brain of the system) near your router and a power outlet, ideally on the first floor.
2. **Install door and window sensors.** These come in two pieces: one mounts on the door or window frame, the other on the moving door or window itself. Most use adhesive strips; no drilling required.
3. **Place motion detectors** in the corners of main living areas, about 6 to 7 feet high with a clear line of sight across the room.
4. **Mount the keypad** near your main entry door, about chest height.
5. **Download the app** and connect everything to your Wi-Fi.
6. **Test every sensor** before activating the system.

The [Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC)](https://www.cpsc.gov/safety-education/safety-education-centers/home-safety) and most alarm manufacturers recommend testing your system monthly and replacing sensor batteries every one to two years.

## Red Flags to Watch for When Hiring a Security Company

Scams and high-pressure sales tactics are unfortunately common in the home security industry. Watch out for:

- **Door-to-door salespeople demanding same-day decisions.** Legitimate companies do not pressure you to sign a contract on the spot.
- **Contracts buried with auto-renewal clauses.** Read for automatic renewal language; some contracts renew for a full year if you do not cancel 30 to 60 days before the end date.
- **"Free" equipment that is actually leased.** If the company owns the equipment, you cannot take it with you when you move.
- **Relocation policies.** Ask specifically: if I move in two years, what happens to my contract and my equipment?
- **Vague cancellation terms.** If a salesperson cannot clearly explain the cancellation process and early termination fee, that is a red flag.

The [Federal Trade Commission (FTC)](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/home-security-companies) has published guidance on home security sales tactics and your rights when canceling contracts.
