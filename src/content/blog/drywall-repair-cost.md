Drywall repair typically costs **$75 to $350 for small to medium holes**, and **$350 to $1,500 or more for large sections or water-damaged walls**. If you hire a drywall contractor, expect to pay **$50 to $100 per hour for labor**. A simple nail-pop fix might cost you $75; repairing a fist-sized hole left by a doorknob usually runs $150 to $250; and replacing a full damaged wall section in a living room can easily reach $800 to $1,200 once you factor in materials, labor, texturing, and painting to match.

Drywall damage happens to every homeowner eventually. Whether it is a doorknob that swung too hard, a water leak that quietly ruined a wall, or a renovation that left gaps behind, knowing what repairs actually cost helps you budget honestly and avoid getting overcharged. Let us walk through every scenario so you know exactly what to expect.

## Why Drywall Repair Costs Vary So Much

Before you get a quote, understand what drives the price:

- **Size of the damage:** Contractors often price by the square foot or by hole size. A 1-inch nail hole takes 10 minutes; a 2-foot section takes hours.
- **Location in the home:** Ceiling repairs cost more than wall repairs because they require scaffolding or ladders and are physically harder to finish smoothly.
- **Texture matching:** Flat walls are easiest. Knockdown, orange peel, and especially popcorn textures take extra skill and time to match invisibly.
- **Paint matching:** Most quotes do not include repainting. If your wall color is a custom match, that adds $50 to $200.
- **Water or mold damage:** Wet drywall must be completely removed, dried, treated for mold, and replaced. This adds $300 to $1,000 to any repair job.
- **Your region:** Labor rates in New York City or San Francisco run 40 to 60 percent higher than rural Midwest rates.

## What You Are Actually Paying For

When a drywall contractor quotes you a job, here is what that price covers:

**Labor:** The biggest chunk. Skilled drywall finishers charge $50 to $100 per hour nationally, with some specialists in high-demand markets charging $120 or more. A small patch might take 45 minutes; a large section repair including drying time between coats can stretch across two days.

**Materials:** Joint compound ("mud"), mesh tape, drywall screws, and new drywall panels. A 4x8 sheet of standard 1/2-inch drywall costs $15 to $25 at home improvement stores. A 5-gallon bucket of joint compound runs $20 to $35. These materials are cheap; the skill to use them invisibly is what you are paying for.

**Texture application:** Most finishers charge an additional $50 to $150 to texture a repaired area so it blends with the surrounding wall. This is not optional if you want the repair to be invisible.

**Primer coat:** Often included in drywall quotes, or billed at $30 to $60. Final paint color matching is almost always extra.

## Drywall Repair Cost by Type of Damage

### Water Damage Repair: $300 to $1,500

This is where homeowners most often get surprised. A small stain on a ceiling from a roof leak might look like a $100 fix, but if the drywall is soft and crumbling, the entire wet section must come out. The contractor needs to:

1. Remove and dispose of wet drywall
2. Allow the framing to fully dry (sometimes requiring fans for 24 to 72 hours)
3. Test for and treat any mold (adds $200 to $600 on its own)
4. Install new drywall, tape, and finish
5. Texture and prime to match

A 4x4-foot section of water-damaged ceiling commonly runs $400 to $900 after all those steps. For larger leaks, costs can exceed $2,000.

Always fix the source of the water before repairing drywall. If you patch over an active leak, you are throwing money away.

### Doorknob Hole Repair: $150 to $300

One of the most requested drywall repairs. A typical doorknob hole is 3 to 5 inches in diameter. The contractor will use a mesh patch or California patch technique, apply three coats of joint compound (each requiring drying time), sand, texture, and prime. This job often takes two visits because of drying time.

### Popped Nails and Settling Cracks: $75 to $200

As homes settle, nails can pop through drywall surfaces and diagonal cracks can appear at corners of windows and doors. These are cosmetic fixes most of the time. A contractor can address multiple nail pops or cracks in one visit for a flat fee of $100 to $200.

### Corner Bead Damage: $100 to $250

Metal or vinyl corner bead on wall edges gets dinged by furniture and foot traffic. Replacing a section requires removing the damaged bead, cutting and installing a new piece, and feathering joint compound out several inches. Budget $100 to $250 per corner depending on length.

### Ceiling Drywall Repair: $200 to $600 (premium over wall repairs)

Ceiling work is always more expensive. Contractors need to work overhead, often requiring scaffolding on larger jobs. Finishing a ceiling smoothly without visible joints or texture mismatches requires more skill. Add 25 to 50 percent over comparable wall repair prices for any ceiling work.

## DIY Drywall Repair: When It Makes Sense (And When It Does Not)

### DIY is reasonable for:
- Nail holes and small dings (under 1 inch): A $10 to $15 spackle tube from the hardware store handles these in 20 minutes. Sand when dry, touch up with paint.
- Small to medium holes (1 to 6 inches): Peel-and-stick mesh patches with joint compound work well for patient, detail-oriented homeowners. Materials cost $20 to $40.

### Hire a pro for:
- Anything involving water damage or potential mold
- Ceiling repairs (safety and finish quality)
- Repairs requiring texture matching, especially knockdown or skip trowel
- Holes larger than 6 inches where a backer board is needed
- Any repair you want to be truly invisible

The mistake most DIYers make is applying too little joint compound too thickly, then sanding too aggressively and burning through into the drywall paper. Three thin coats, thoroughly dried, is always better than one thick coat.

**DIY material cost breakdown:**
- Spackle (small holes): $8 to $15
- Peel-and-stick patch kit (medium holes): $15 to $35
- Joint compound, 5-gallon bucket: $20 to $35
- Mesh tape roll: $8 to $12
- Sandpaper assortment: $10 to $20
- Drywall panel (4x8 sheet): $15 to $25
- Primer spray: $10 to $15

Total DIY material cost for a medium hole repair: **$50 to $100**. Compare that to $150 to $300 for a professional, and you can see the savings, but only if the finished result is acceptable to you.

## How to Hire a Drywall Contractor Without Getting Burned

Drywall work is one of the most frequently DIY-attempted repairs that ends up being redone professionally, because poor finishing is obvious under paint. Here is how to hire right:

**Get at least three quotes.** Pricing on drywall can vary 40 percent or more between contractors in the same zip code. Small jobs sometimes carry minimum trip fees of $100 to $150 regardless of the actual work, so compare total cost, not hourly rate.

**Ask to see finished work.** Drywall finishing is a craft. Ask the contractor for photos of recent repairs, or references from homeowners who can show you the work in person.

**Confirm they handle texture matching.** Not every handyperson knows how to match knockdown, orange peel, or swirl textures. Ask specifically whether texture matching is included in their quote.

**Ask about painting separately.** Most drywall contractors prime but do not paint. Clarify upfront whether the quote includes a finish coat or just primer.

**Check licensing.** Most states require contractor licensing for work above certain thresholds. Check your state's contractor licensing board to verify. The [National Association of Home Builders (NAHB)](https://www.nahb.org) maintains links to state licensing resources.

**Watch for red flags:** Cash-only demands before work begins, no written estimate, pressure to decide immediately, or a quote far below all others (often signals inexperience or unlicensed work).

## Drywall and Home Insurance: What Is Covered

Drywall repair caused by a sudden, accidental covered event, like a burst pipe, a wind-damaged roof letting in rain, or a fire, is typically covered under your standard homeowners insurance policy, minus your deductible.

What is usually NOT covered:
- Gradual water damage from a slow drip you should have caught
- Mold resulting from neglect
- Normal settling cracks
- Repairs after flooding (requires separate flood insurance through the [National Flood Insurance Program](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance))

If you file a claim, your insurer may require you to document the damage with photos before any cleanup, and may send an adjuster before authorizing repairs. Always check with your insurer before starting work on a potentially covered repair.

## Red Flags That Drywall Damage Is More Than Cosmetic

Sometimes a crack or stain is a symptom of a bigger problem. Before spending money on drywall repair, check for:

- **Diagonal cracks from window or door corners:** Could indicate foundation settlement. If cracks are widening over time, have a structural engineer evaluate before patching.
- **Horizontal cracks in basement walls:** Potentially a sign of lateral soil pressure. [FEMA's flood and foundation resources](https://www.fema.gov) can help you understand risk levels.
- **Stains that keep reappearing after you paint over them:** The moisture source is still active. Repair the plumbing or roofing issue first.
- **Soft, crumbling drywall behind a wall that looks dry:** Hidden moisture, possibly from a slow leak or condensation issue in an exterior wall.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does drywall repair take?**
A small patch: one to two hours plus drying time. A large section requiring multiple coats: one to two days spread across visits. The compound must dry completely between coats; rushing this step is the most common reason repairs look bad after painting.

**Can I paint over repaired drywall the same day?**
No. Joint compound must cure fully, which takes 24 to 48 hours for each coat depending on temperature and humidity. Painting over wet or uncured mud will cause bubbling and cracking.

**How do I find a stud before hanging something heavy?**
Use a stud finder tool ($15 to $40 at any hardware store), or knock along the wall and listen for the hollow to solid transition. Studs in US homes are typically 16 inches apart, center to center, as specified by standard residential framing practices.

**Will my repair be noticeable under paint?**
If done by an experienced finisher, no. If done by a beginner or a rushed contractor, it will often show under raking light (light hitting the wall at a low angle). This is why texture matching and feathering the compound far out from the repair matters.
