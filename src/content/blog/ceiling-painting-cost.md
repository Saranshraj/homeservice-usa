Painting a ceiling typically costs **$150 to $700 per room** when you hire a professional, or about **$0.50 to $3.00 per square foot** including labor and materials. A standard 12x12 living room ceiling runs $175 to $350, while a two-story foyer or vaulted great room can cost $600 to $1,500 or more. If you paint it yourself, you'll spend $40 to $150 on supplies and paint. That wide range comes down to ceiling type, height, condition, and where you live.

We know how much it stings to get a quote that feels way higher than you expected. Let us break down exactly what drives the price so you can budget with confidence.

## What Affects the Cost of Ceiling Painting

### 1. Ceiling Height and Accessibility

Standard 8-foot ceilings are the least expensive because painters can reach them with a standard roller extension and a 6-foot ladder. As ceilings get taller, scaffolding or specialty equipment adds real cost.

| Ceiling Height | Extra Cost vs. Standard |
|---|---|
| 8 feet (standard) | Baseline |
| 9 to 10 feet | +$50 to $100 per room |
| 11 to 12 feet | +$100 to $200 per room |
| 14 to 16 feet (vaulted) | +$150 to $400 per room |
| 18 feet or higher (great room) | +$300 to $800 per room |

### 2. Ceiling Type and Texture

Flat, smooth ceilings are quickest to paint. Textured ceilings like popcorn, orange peel, or skip trowel require more paint, slower application, and extra care to avoid damaging the texture.

| Ceiling Type | Cost Per Square Foot |
|---|---|
| Smooth/flat drywall | $0.50 to $1.50 |
| Light texture (orange peel) | $0.75 to $2.00 |
| Heavy texture (popcorn, skip trowel) | $1.00 to $3.00 |
| Coffered or tray ceiling | $2.00 to $5.00 |
| Beadboard or tongue and groove | $1.50 to $4.00 |

**A note on popcorn ceilings:** If your home was built before 1978, popcorn ceilings may contain asbestos. Painting over an intact asbestos ceiling is generally considered safe, but disturbing it (sanding, scraping) is not. The [EPA recommends](https://www.epa.gov/asbestos/asbestos-your-home) testing before any work on older textured ceilings. Testing kits cost $30 to $50, or you can hire a certified inspector for $250 to $500.

### 3. Surface Condition and Prep Work

A ceiling in good condition needs only a light cleaning and a coat or two of paint. Problem areas drive up the price fast.

| Prep Work Needed | Added Cost |
|---|---|
| Standard cleaning/prime | Included in most quotes |
| Water stain blocking (per stain) | $20 to $75 |
| Skim coating/smoothing | $1.00 to $3.00/sq ft extra |
| Crack or hole repair (small) | $50 to $150 per area |
| Mold treatment and prime | $100 to $300+ |

If you see water stains, fix the leak first. Painting over active moisture damage is a short-term patch that will fail within months.

### 4. Paint Quality and Number of Coats

Most ceilings need two coats for a solid, even finish, especially over a color change or water stain. Budget one to two extra hours of labor for a second coat.

**Paint types for ceilings:**

- **Flat/matte ceiling paint:** The standard choice. It hides surface imperfections and reflects less light, so scuffs and uneven texture are less visible. Cost: $25 to $50 per gallon.
- **Eggshell ceiling paint:** Slightly more sheen, easier to wipe clean. Better for kitchens and bathrooms. Cost: $30 to $60 per gallon.
- **Semi-gloss:** Used in high-humidity areas like showers. Stands up to moisture and mildew. Cost: $35 to $65 per gallon.

One gallon covers roughly 350 to 400 square feet with one coat.

### 5. Labor Rates and Your Location

Painter labor rates vary widely by region. Expect to pay more in major metro areas and coastal cities.

| Region | Typical Labor Rate Per Hour |
|---|---|
| Northeast (NYC, Boston, DC) | $60 to $100 per hour |
| Pacific Coast (LA, SF, Seattle) | $55 to $90 per hour |
| Midwest (Chicago, Columbus) | $40 to $70 per hour |
| South (Atlanta, Dallas, Houston) | $35 to $65 per hour |
| Mountain West (Denver, Phoenix) | $40 to $75 per hour |

Most professional painters charge per project rather than per hour once they assess the job. Always get at least three quotes for any painting project over $300.

## The Full House: What Does It Cost to Paint All Ceilings?

If you want every ceiling in a 2,000-square-foot home done at once, you can expect to pay **$1,200 to $3,500**. Hiring one contractor for the whole job usually gets you a better rate per room than scheduling rooms individually because the painter mobilizes once and moves efficiently through the house.

Some contractors bundle ceiling painting with a full interior paint job (walls and trim). Bundling can save 10 to 25 percent versus separate quotes.

## DIY Ceiling Painting: Is It Worth It?

You can absolutely paint a ceiling yourself, and the cost drops dramatically. Here is what you'll need:

**Supplies checklist:**
- Ceiling paint (1 gallon per 350 to 400 sq ft): $25 to $50
- Roller frame and extension pole: $15 to $30
- High-nap roller covers (3/4 inch for textured ceilings): $8 to $15
- Painter's tape: $6 to $12
- Drop cloths: $10 to $25
- Primer (if needed): $25 to $45

**Total DIY cost for one standard room: $50 to $150**

The savings are real, but so are the trade-offs. Ceiling painting is physically demanding (you're looking straight up the entire time), and drips, streaks, and missed spots are much more noticeable than on walls. Vaulted or high ceilings add genuine safety risk without the right ladder or scaffolding setup.

The [Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov) reports that ladders are involved in over 164,000 emergency room visits each year. If your ceiling is over 10 feet, renting scaffolding planks for $40 to $80 per day is a worthwhile investment over balancing on a ladder.

**When to DIY:**
- Flat 8-foot ceilings in good condition
- You're comfortable on a ladder
- You have a full weekend and the right supplies
- You don't mind a slow pace and multiple coats

**When to hire a pro:**
- Vaulted, coffered, or multi-story ceilings
- Existing water stains, mold, or damage
- You want it done in one day with minimal disruption
- The room has high-end trim or finishes you want protected

## How to Get the Best Price on Ceiling Painting

You can lower your out-of-pocket cost without sacrificing quality by following a few practical steps.

**Move furniture yourself.** Most painters charge $25 to $75 per room to move and protect furniture. Clear the room before they arrive and you eliminate that add-on.

**Bundle rooms.** As mentioned, doing multiple rooms or the whole house at once gives you negotiating power and saves the contractor time, which usually translates to a lower per-room rate.

**Ask about the paint.** Some contractors mark up paint by 20 to 40 percent. Ask them to quote labor separately, and buy the paint at your local hardware store yourself. This only makes sense if you can get the same or better quality at a better price.

**Schedule during the off-season.** Painters are busiest in spring and early fall. Scheduling interior work during winter months (January to February) can sometimes get you a 10 to 15 percent discount.

**Get everything in writing.** A solid quote should specify the number of coats, brand of paint, prep work included, and cleanup. Misunderstandings about scope are the most common source of disputes with contractors. The [National Association of Home Builders](https://www.nahb.org) recommends always getting at least three written bids before choosing a contractor for any home project.

## Red Flags When Hiring a Painter

Not every contractor who shows up with a brush does quality work. Watch for these warning signs:

- Requesting more than 30 percent upfront (a deposit of 10 to 25 percent is standard)
- No written contract or itemized quote
- Pressure to start immediately without time to compare bids
- Cash-only payment with no receipt
- No proof of liability insurance or workers' compensation coverage
- Online reviews that mention unfinished work or payment disputes

Always ask to see a contractor's license in states that require it. The [Painting and Decorating Contractors of America](https://www.pdca.org) has a contractor locator that can help you find certified professionals in your area.

## What About Lead Paint?

If your home was built before 1978, your ceiling may have layers of lead paint underneath. Painting over intact lead paint is not inherently dangerous, but sanding or scraping it creates hazardous dust.

Under the [EPA's Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) Rule](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program), contractors working on pre-1978 homes must be EPA-certified and follow lead-safe work practices. Ask any contractor working on an older home to confirm their lead-safe certification.

If you are pregnant, have young children at home, or simply want peace of mind, hire a certified inspector to test your ceilings before any work begins. Testing costs $250 to $500 and gives you a clear picture of what you're dealing with.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**How long does it take to paint a ceiling?**
A professional can paint a standard bedroom ceiling in 1 to 2 hours. A full house with multiple rooms takes 1 to 2 days. DIY typically takes longer because of setup, drying time between coats, and the learning curve.

**Do I need to prime a ceiling before painting?**
Not always. If you're painting over a similar color with good coverage, a self-priming ceiling paint may be enough. If there are stains, a new drywall surface, or a drastic color change, a dedicated primer saves paint and improves adhesion.

**Can I paint a bathroom ceiling?**
Yes, but use a paint formulated for high-humidity areas. Look for paint labeled "bathroom," "kitchen and bath," or "mold-resistant." These formulations contain mildewcides that help prevent mold growth in humid environments.

**What sheen is best for a bedroom ceiling?**
Flat or matte is the standard recommendation for most ceilings because it hides imperfections and reduces glare. Eggshell is a reasonable choice if you want something slightly easier to clean.

**How often should ceilings be repainted?**
In good condition, ceilings typically need repainting every 7 to 10 years. Kitchens and bathrooms with more humidity and grease may need attention every 3 to 5 years.
