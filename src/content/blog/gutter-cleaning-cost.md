Gutter cleaning costs **$120 to $400** for most single- and two-story homes, with a national average around **$200**. If you have a larger home, steep roof pitch, or significant debris buildup from overhanging trees, expect to pay toward the higher end or beyond it. The good news: getting this done twice a year is one of the most cost-effective maintenance investments you can make, because ignoring clogged gutters leads to repairs that easily run into the thousands.

If you have been putting off that gutter cleaning this fall, you are definitely not alone. It is one of those tasks that feels optional right up until water starts pouring over the sides and you notice water stains creeping down your foundation. This guide breaks down exactly what you will pay, what affects the price, and how to get the best deal.

## What Drives the Price Up or Down

### 1. Number of Stories

This is the biggest cost variable. Working one story off the ground is straightforward ladder work. Two stories requires taller equipment, more time to reposition, and carries more liability for the crew. Three stories often means specialized ladder setups or even a bucket truck for steep or complex rooflines.

Expect to pay roughly **30 to 60 percent more** for a two-story home than a comparable single-story home, and double or more for three stories.

### 2. Linear Footage of Gutters

Most cleaners price by the linear foot (typically $0.70 to $2.00 per foot) or by the job. A ranch-style home with a simple roofline might have 100 to 150 linear feet of gutters. A two-story colonial with valleys and dormers could easily have 250 to 350 feet or more.

Before getting quotes, do a rough walk around your home and estimate how much gutter you actually have. This helps you spot-check whether a quote seems reasonable.

### 3. Debris Level and Tree Cover

A home with oak, pine, or maple trees directly overhanging the gutters will have significantly heavier debris loads than a home on an open lot. Some companies charge an extra **$25 to $75** for heavy debris cleanouts, particularly after a wet fall when leaves mat together and add weight.

If you have not had your gutters cleaned in two or more years, you should expect the higher end of any range, and potentially a surcharge.

### 4. Roof Pitch

A steep-pitch roof (anything above a 6:12 pitch) is harder and more dangerous to work on. Contractors often add a **10 to 25 percent steep-pitch surcharge**. If you live in a region with heavy snowfall, you likely have a steeper pitch designed to shed snow, so this applies to a lot of homeowners in the Northeast and Mountain West.

### 5. Gutter Guards or Covers

If you have gutter guards installed, cleaning takes more time because the guards have to be lifted, the troughs cleaned, and the guards replaced. This typically adds **$1.50 to $3.00 per linear foot** to the job. Some guards actually trap debris on top rather than preventing clogs, which creates a maintenance problem of its own.

### 6. Add-On Services

Many gutter cleaning companies offer optional services you can add to the visit:

- **Downspout flushing:** Usually included, but confirm before booking. Some charge $10 to $25 extra per downspout.
- **Minor gutter repairs** (resealing joints, re-securing loose sections): $75 to $200 depending on scope.
- **Gutter brightening** (cleaning the exterior face): $50 to $150.
- **Roof debris blowoff:** $50 to $150 to clear leaves from the roof surface before they wash into the gutters.

## How Often Should You Clean Your Gutters?

The standard recommendation from the National Association of Home Builders ([nahb.org](https://www.nahb.org)) is **twice per year**: once in late spring after trees finish shedding seeds and blooms, and once in late fall after the leaves have dropped.

If you have pine trees nearby, you may need three cleanings per year because pine needles fall year-round and are particularly good at clogging downspouts.

FEMA recommends regular gutter and downspout maintenance as part of flood-loss prevention, since clogged gutters can redirect water toward your foundation during heavy rain events. See their home maintenance guidance at [fema.gov/flood-insurance](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance).

## Signs Your Gutters Need Cleaning Now

You do not always have to wait for the calendar to tell you when it is time. Watch for these signs:

- **Gutters are visibly sagging or pulling away from the fascia.** This usually means water-logged debris is adding weight.
- **Water spills over the front of the gutter during rain.** This is the classic sign of a clog.
- **Plants or moss are growing out of the gutters.** At this point you have soil accumulation, which means the clog has been there a while.
- **Staining on the siding below the gutters.** Dark streaks often indicate overflow that has been happening for some time.
- **Water pooling near your foundation.** If your downspouts are blocked or disconnected, water has nowhere to go but toward your house.
- **Birds or pests are nesting.** Clogged gutters create an appealing habitat for birds, mice, and mosquitoes.

## What Happens If You Skip Gutter Cleaning

This is the part that usually motivates homeowners more than the cleaning cost itself. Clogged gutters can cause:

- **Foundation damage:** When gutters overflow toward the house, water saturates the soil around your foundation. This can cause settlement, cracking, or basement flooding. Foundation repair can cost $5,000 to $30,000 or more, according to data from the American Society of Home Inspectors.
- **Fascia and soffit rot:** Constant moisture contact with the wood behind the gutter causes rot that spreads. Fascia and soffit replacement typically runs $600 to $2,500 depending on scope.
- **Roof damage:** Ice dams in cold climates form when backed-up gutters trap water that freezes and refreezes. This can cause water to back up under shingles. The Consumer Product Safety Commission ([cpsc.gov](https://www.cpsc.gov)) lists ice dam prevention as an important winter home safety measure.
- **Mold growth:** Water infiltrating into walls or attic spaces from overflow can create mold conditions. Mold remediation typically costs $1,500 to $9,000 depending on extent, per EPA guidelines at [epa.gov/mold](https://www.epa.gov/mold).
- **Landscape erosion:** Concentrated water pouring over the gutter front can erode mulch beds and damage plantings.

Spending $150 to $200 twice a year is a straightforward way to avoid these much larger bills.

## DIY Gutter Cleaning: Is It Worth It?

You can absolutely clean your own gutters. Here is a realistic picture of what it involves and costs.

**What you need:**
- Extension ladder (rent for $30 to $50/day, or purchase for $150 to $400)
- Gutter scoop or trowel ($5 to $15)
- Garden hose with spray nozzle or pressure washer
- Bucket and tarp
- Safety glasses, work gloves, and non-slip footwear

**Time required:** 1 to 3 hours for a single-story home; 2 to 5 hours for a two-story home.

**When DIY makes sense:** You are physically comfortable on a ladder, your home is single-story, your roof pitch is moderate, and you have basic tools. Regular twice-yearly maintenance is easier than dealing with years of buildup.

**When to hire out:** Two-story or higher homes, steep roofs, significant physical limitations, or if your gutters have not been touched in several years. Falls from ladders are a leading cause of home injury fatalities. The Centers for Disease Control and Prevention (CDC) reports that more than 500,000 people are treated for ladder-related injuries each year. For many homeowners, the $150 to $200 cost of a professional visit is simply good risk management.

## How to Hire a Gutter Cleaner Without Getting Ripped Off

### Get Three Quotes

For a job in the $150 to $300 range, it is worth getting two or three quotes. Many companies will give you a quote over the phone or via photo if you send them a picture of your home and describe the number of stories.

### Confirm What Is Included

Before hiring, ask:
1. Does the price include downspout flushing?
2. Will debris be bagged and removed, or blown into the yard?
3. Is there a fee if the gutters are especially clogged?
4. Do you carry liability insurance and workers comp?

### Check Licensing

Gutter cleaning generally does not require a contractor's license in most states, but companies doing any repairs typically do. For any repairs involving ladder work and your home's exterior, check your state contractor licensing board to verify credentials. The National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies maintains links to each state board at [nascla.org](https://www.nascla.org).

### Look for Bundled Deals

Many companies will discount gutter cleaning when bundled with roof debris blowoff, pressure washing, or window cleaning. If you need multiple exterior services this fall, ask about package pricing.

### Schedule Ahead

Late fall is peak season for gutter cleaning. Trying to book in mid-November often means waiting two to three weeks or paying rush rates. If you can schedule in October or early November, you typically get better availability and sometimes better pricing.

## Gutter Cleaning vs. Gutter Guards: A Cost Comparison

A common question homeowners ask is whether installing gutter guards eliminates cleaning altogether. The honest answer is: it reduces the need, but rarely eliminates it.

| Option | Upfront Cost | Annual Maintenance | 10-Year Cost Estimate |
|---|---|---|---|
| Regular cleaning (2x/year) | $0 | $300 to $600/year | $3,000 to $6,000 |
| Basic screen guards + cleaning (1x/year) | $300 to $800 | $150 to $300/year | $1,800 to $3,800 |
| Premium micro-mesh guards + cleaning (1x/year) | $1,500 to $4,000 | $100 to $200/year | $2,500 to $6,000 |

Premium micro-mesh guards (such as LeafFilter or MasterShield) can genuinely reduce cleaning frequency for many homes. But they still require periodic flushing, and they do not work well in all debris conditions. For most average homes, regular cleaning remains the most cost-effective path.

## A Quick Word on Timing for Tax Purposes

Gutter cleaning on your primary residence is not a deductible home improvement expense. However, if you use a portion of your home for a home office or if the property is a rental, the cleaning cost may be deductible as a maintenance expense. The IRS provides guidance on home-related deductions at [irs.gov/publications](https://www.irs.gov/publications). Always confirm with your tax professional.
