Skipping fall home maintenance costs the average US homeowner $3,000 to $15,000 in preventable winter damage each year, according to estimates from the National Association of Home Builders. The good news: a single weekend of work, plus a few hundred dollars in pro services, can protect your home from burst pipes, ice dams, furnace failures, and structural water damage before temperatures drop. This checklist covers all 25 critical tasks, with real cost estimates for everything you should hire out.

Fall is the best time to catch problems because contractors are available (and cheaper than in the dead of winter), materials seal properly in cool but not frozen temperatures, and you have weeks of lead time before the first hard freeze. Get started by late September if you live in the northern tier of the US, or by mid-October in the South.

---

## Why Fall Maintenance Pays Off

Home insurance covers sudden, accidental damage, but carriers routinely deny claims when they can show "neglect." A frozen pipe that bursts is covered. A pipe that burst because you never drained it in October is a gray area that often results in a denied claim. The [Insurance Information Institute](https://www.iii.org/) consistently finds that frozen pipes are among the most common reasons for claim disputes.

Beyond insurance, deferred maintenance compounds. A $200 caulking job around a window frame prevents a $1,800 mold remediation job two winters later. A $150 furnace tune-up catches a cracked heat exchanger before it fails on the coldest night of January.

## Exterior: Start Here

### 1. Inspect and Clean Gutters and Downspouts

**When to do it:** After most leaves have fallen, typically October in the North, November in the South.

Clogged gutters cause ice dams, which can peel off shingles, rot fascia boards, and push water behind your siding. Clean gutters yourself for $0 if you have a ladder, or hire a pro for $100 to $250 for a typical single-story home. Two-story homes average $175 to $350.

While up there, check that downspouts discharge at least 4 feet from your foundation. Extensions cost $5 to $20 each at any hardware store.

### 2. Check the Roof for Damaged or Missing Shingles

**Cost to hire a pro:** $150 to $400 for an inspection; $200 to $800 for minor repairs.

Don't wait for a leak to tell you there's a problem. After any wind event, walk around your home and look for shingles on the ground. From the ground with binoculars, look for curling, cracked, or missing shingles. Any granule accumulation in your gutters signals shingles near the end of their life.

The [National Roofing Contractors Association](https://www.nrca.net/) recommends a professional roof inspection every three years, or after any storm with winds over 60 mph.

### 3. Seal Gaps Around Windows and Doors

**DIY cost:** $5 to $25 per window for caulk and weatherstripping.

On a cold day, hold your hand around every window and exterior door frame. Feel for cold air infiltration. According to the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/weatherize/air-sealing-your-home), drafts around windows and doors account for up to 30% of heating costs in older homes.

Silicone caulk ($5 to $8 per tube) handles the gap between the frame and siding. Foam weatherstripping ($4 to $12 per roll) seals the door itself. Both are easy DIY jobs that take under an hour per door.

### 4. Shut Down Irrigation Systems and Drain Outdoor Faucets

**DIY cost:** $0 to $15 for a hose bib insulation cover.
**Pro sprinkler blowout:** $50 to $150.

This is the single most important fall plumbing task. A burst pipe from a forgotten outdoor faucet or irrigation line causes $2,500 to $5,000 in water damage on average. Turn off the interior shutoff valve for each exterior faucet, open the exterior faucet to drain residual water, then install a foam hose bib cover ($3 to $6 at hardware stores).

For in-ground sprinkler systems, hire an irrigation company to blow out the lines with compressed air. Skipping this step voids most irrigation warranties and routinely causes $400 to $1,200 in line repairs.

### 5. Check the Foundation for Cracks

Walk the perimeter and look for new cracks in your foundation. Horizontal cracks in block foundations or cracks wider than 1/4 inch warrant a structural engineer inspection ($300 to $700). Hairline vertical cracks in poured concrete are typically normal settling; fill them with polyurethane caulk ($8 to $15) to prevent water infiltration.

### 6. Store or Cover Outdoor Furniture and Equipment

Leaving cast iron furniture and grills uncovered through a wet winter shortens their life by years. Cover or store everything before the first freeze. Gas grills: disconnect and cap the propane line, or roll the whole unit inside the garage.

### 7. Grade Soil Away from the Foundation

Check that soil and mulch grade away from your foundation at a slope of at least 1 inch per foot for the first 6 feet. Water pooling against your foundation is the leading cause of basement water intrusion. Adding or regrading topsoil costs $200 to $600 for a typical home if you hire a landscaper.

## HVAC: Your Most Expensive System

### 8. Schedule a Furnace or Heat Pump Tune-Up

**Professional cost:** $80 to $150 for a furnace tune-up; $100 to $200 for a heat pump inspection.

HVAC companies are slammed when furnaces fail in January. Scheduling in September or October means shorter wait times and often lower prices. A tune-up includes cleaning the burners, checking the heat exchanger for cracks (a cracked heat exchanger leaks carbon monoxide), lubricating moving parts, and checking refrigerant levels on heat pumps.

The [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/heat-and-cool/service-and-maintenance) states that annual HVAC maintenance reduces energy costs by up to 15% and prevents most emergency breakdowns.

### 9. Replace Air Filters

**Cost:** $8 to $30 per filter.

A dirty filter forces your furnace to work harder, increasing energy bills and wear. Replace it at the start of heating season regardless of when you last changed it. Homes with pets or allergies should use MERV 11-13 rated filters. Change filters every 60 to 90 days through the heating season.

### 10. Test the Thermostat

Switch to heating mode and verify the system kicks on. If you still have an old programmable thermostat, consider upgrading to a smart thermostat ($150 to $350 installed). Most US homes save $50 to $180 per year on heating bills after the switch, per [ENERGY STAR](https://www.energystar.gov/).

### 11. Bleed Radiators (If You Have Hot Water Heat)

Hot water heating systems trap air in radiators over summer, leading to uneven heating and gurgling noises. Use a radiator key ($4 to $8) to open the bleed valve on each radiator until water flows steadily. This is a 15-minute DIY job.

### 12. Inspect and Seal Ductwork

Leaky ducts waste 20 to 30% of heated air, per the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/duct-sealing). Check accessible ducts in your attic and basement for gaps at joints and connections. Seal with mastic sealant ($8 per quart) or metal tape, not ordinary duct tape, which fails quickly.

## Plumbing: Prevent Frozen Pipes

### 13. Locate Your Main Water Shutoff

This sounds obvious, but in a water emergency, every second counts. Find your main shutoff and confirm it turns easily. If it's stuck (common in older homes with gate valves), replace it with a ball valve while you have time ($150 to $350 hired out). Know where it is before you need it.

### 14. Insulate Exposed Pipes in Unheated Spaces

**Cost:** $0.50 to $1.00 per linear foot of foam pipe insulation.

Pipes in unheated garages, crawl spaces, and exterior walls are the first to freeze. Foam pipe insulation sleeves cut to length with scissors and slip over pipes in minutes. The [American Red Cross](https://www.redcross.org/get-help/how-to-prepare-for-emergencies/types-of-emergencies/winter-storm/frozen-pipes.html) recommends insulating any pipe within 6 inches of an exterior wall.

### 15. Drain and Flush the Water Heater

Sediment accumulates at the bottom of tank water heaters and cuts efficiency by up to 20%, per the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/maintain-your-water-heater). Attach a garden hose to the drain valve and flush 2 to 3 gallons to remove sediment. Water heaters over 10 years old that haven't been flushed regularly may not recover full efficiency.

## Safety Systems: Don't Skip These

### 16. Test Smoke Detectors and Carbon Monoxide Detectors

**Cost:** $0 to $30 each for replacement units.

Test every smoke alarm and CO detector by pressing the test button. Replace batteries in all units, even if they seem to work. The [U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov/) recommends replacing smoke detectors older than 10 years and CO detectors older than 7 years. Carbon monoxide poisoning peaks in winter when furnaces run constantly, and CO detectors are your only defense.

### 17. Check and Clean the Dryer Vent

**DIY cost:** $20 to $50 for a vent cleaning brush kit.
**Professional cost:** $90 to $150.

Clogged dryer vents cause approximately 2,900 house fires each year, according to the [National Fire Protection Association](https://www.nfpa.org/). Disconnect the vent hose and clean it from both the dryer side and the exterior vent cap. The exterior cap should open freely when the dryer runs.

### 18. Inspect and Clean the Chimney

**Professional chimney sweep:** $100 to $350.

If you use a wood-burning fireplace or stove even occasionally, schedule a chimney inspection and cleaning before winter. Creosote buildup inside the flue is a leading cause of chimney fires. The [Chimney Safety Institute of America](https://www.csia.org/) recommends annual inspections for regularly used fireplaces.

### 19. Check the Fireplace Damper

Open and close the damper to confirm it operates freely and seals properly when closed. An open damper in an unused fireplace is like leaving a window open all winter, letting warm air escape and cold air in.

## Interior: The Easy Wins

### 20. Reverse Ceiling Fans

Ceiling fans have a reverse switch (usually on the motor housing) that makes them spin clockwise in winter, pushing warm air down from the ceiling. This simple step can reduce heating costs by 10 to 15%, per the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/fans-cooling).

### 21. Check Window Locks and Storm Windows

Loose or unlocked windows allow cold air infiltration. Close all storm windows and check that they seal. Single-pane windows benefit from interior window insulation kits ($3 to $8 per window), which create a dead air layer that dramatically cuts heat loss.

### 22. Inspect the Attic for Ventilation and Insulation

Attic insulation keeps heat in. Attic ventilation prevents ice dams by keeping the roof deck cold. Both must work together. Attic insulation should be R-38 to R-60 in most US climate zones, per the [U.S. Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov/energysaver/insulation). If your attic insulation is below the top of the joists, adding more will typically pay back the cost within 2 to 4 years.

## The True Cost of Skipping Fall Maintenance

Common winter damage costs for homeowners who skip fall prep:

- **Burst pipe:** $1,500 to $5,000 for repair plus water damage
- **Ice dam damage:** $1,000 to $8,000 for roofing and interior repairs
- **Furnace emergency call:** $150 to $400 service call plus parts
- **Foundation water intrusion:** $3,000 to $10,000 for waterproofing
- **Chimney fire:** $2,000 to $20,000 for repairs

Contrast that with the realistic cost of doing everything on this list: $300 to $900 in professional services (primarily furnace tune-up, gutter cleaning, and chimney sweep), plus $50 to $150 in materials for DIY tasks. The return on investment is substantial.

---

## Prioritizing When Your Budget Is Tight

If you can only do a few things, focus in this order:

1. **Drain outdoor faucets and irrigation** (free to $15): highest risk, easiest win
2. **Replace furnace filter and test thermostat** ($10 to $30): prevents a January breakdown
3. **Test smoke and CO detectors** ($0 to $60 for batteries and replacements): life safety
4. **Seal windows and doors** ($20 to $80): immediate energy savings
5. **Clean gutters** ($0 to $350): prevents expensive ice dams and foundation damage

The rest can be phased in over the season, but these five items address the most common and costly winter surprises.
