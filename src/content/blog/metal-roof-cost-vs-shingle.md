**The short answer:** Metal roofing costs **$8 to $14 per square foot installed** ($16,000 to $28,000 for a typical 2,000 sq ft roof), roughly 60 to 100 percent more upfront than architectural asphalt shingles at **$5.25 to $8 per square foot** ($10,500 to $16,000). But metal lasts 40 to 70 years versus 25 to 30 years for shingles, so over a 40-year ownership window, metal roofing is often the cheaper option once you account for a shingle roof needing one full replacement in that same timeframe. Whether metal wins for you depends on how long you plan to stay in the home, your climate, and your upfront budget.

This is one of the most common roofing decisions US homeowners face, and it is genuinely close in a lot of cases. Neither option is objectively "better," they solve for different priorities. This guide breaks down the real numbers so you can decide based on your situation, not a contractor's sales pitch for whichever material they happen to install most.

If you are trying to decide between repairing your current roof or replacing it entirely first, start with our [roof repair vs. replace guide](/blog/roof-repair-vs-replace/). This comparison assumes you have already decided on full replacement.

## Upfront Cost Comparison

| Material | Cost per Sq Ft | Total Installed (2,000 sq ft roof) | Expected Lifespan |
|---|---|---|---|
| 3-tab asphalt shingles | $4.25-$6.00 | $8,500-$12,000 | 15-20 years |
| Architectural asphalt shingles | $5.25-$8.00 | $10,500-$16,000 | 25-30 years |
| Exposed-fastener metal (corrugated) | $6.00-$9.00 | $12,000-$18,000 | 30-50 years |
| Standing seam metal | $8.00-$14.00 | $16,000-$28,000 | 40-70 years |

For a full breakdown of what drives roofing costs by state and roof complexity, see our [roof replacement cost guide](/blog/roof-replacement-cost/). This article focuses specifically on the metal-versus-shingle decision.

## The 40-Year Cost Comparison

Upfront price only tells part of the story. Here is what each option actually costs over a 40-year ownership window, the point at which most standing seam metal roofs are just starting to need attention.

### Asphalt Shingles Over 40 Years

- Initial installation (architectural shingles): $13,000
- Replacement at year 25-27: $16,000 (accounting for material cost inflation)
- Routine maintenance and minor repairs over 40 years: $2,000-$4,000
- **Total: approximately $31,000-$33,000**

### Standing Seam Metal Over 40 Years

- Initial installation: $22,000
- No full replacement needed within 40 years
- Routine maintenance (recoating, fastener checks if exposed-fastener style): $1,500-$3,000
- **Total: approximately $23,500-$25,000**

Over a 40-year horizon, metal roofing often works out to be the lower total cost, even though it costs more upfront. The math flips the other direction if you sell your home within 10 to 15 years, since you would be paying the metal premium without living long enough to recoup it through avoided replacement.

## When Shingles Are the Better Choice

**You are not staying long-term.** If you plan to sell within 10 years, shingle roofing's lower upfront cost usually wins. You are unlikely to fully recoup a metal roof's premium in resale value alone.

**Budget is the primary constraint.** Shingles let you get a fully functional, code-compliant roof for significantly less cash today. This matters if you are also budgeting for other major repairs.

**Your home's architecture suits it better.** Traditional colonial, ranch, and craftsman-style homes in many US neighborhoods are visually built around an asphalt shingle look. Metal can work aesthetically, but it is a bigger visual departure in some markets and may affect resale appeal in shingle-dominant neighborhoods.

**You want more contractor options.** Nearly every roofing contractor in the US installs asphalt shingles. Metal roofing, particularly standing seam, requires specialized installers, and in some markets there are meaningfully fewer qualified metal roofing crews, which can mean higher labor costs and longer wait times.

---

## When Metal Roofing Is the Better Choice

**You are staying 20+ years.** This is where metal's math works best. You install once and are largely done for the remainder of your time in the home.

**You live in a wildfire-prone area.** Metal roofing is noncombustible and offers the best fire resistance of any common residential roofing material. Many insurance companies in wildfire-risk states (California, Colorado, parts of the Mountain West) offer premium discounts for metal roofs.

**You live in a severe hail or high-wind zone.** Quality standing seam metal roofing handles hail and wind significantly better than asphalt shingles, though it can still dent from very large hail. Insurance discounts are common here too, check with your provider before assuming the cost difference is fully out of pocket.

**You want lower long-term energy costs.** Reflective metal roofing (especially with a light-colored or specially coated finish) can reduce cooling costs by reflecting more solar heat than dark asphalt shingles. [ENERGY STAR](https://www.energystar.gov/products/building_products/roof_products) certifies cool roofing products, including many metal options, and some qualify for utility rebates.

**You want to avoid repeat tear-offs.** Every roof replacement generates a significant amount of landfill waste (old shingles) and disruption. If avoiding a second full replacement matters to you, metal's longer lifespan reduces that entirely.

## Insurance and Financing Considerations

Many homeowners insurance companies offer premium discounts for metal roofs, sometimes 5 to 20 percent, particularly in hail-prone and wildfire-prone states. Ask your insurer directly, this can meaningfully offset metal's upfront premium over time. Our [homeowners insurance claims guide](/blog/homeowners-insurance-claims/) covers how roofing material affects claims and coverage more broadly.

Metal roofing installation typically qualifies for standard home improvement financing the same way shingle roofing does. If you are weighing whether to finance a metal roof premium versus paying cash for shingles now, run the numbers against your specific loan terms rather than assuming either option is automatically better.

## Noise, Weight, and Other Practical Differences

**Noise during rain:** This is a common concern, but modern metal roofing installed over solid decking with proper underlayment is not significantly louder than shingles during rain. The "loud tin roof" reputation comes mostly from older barn-style installations without insulation or solid decking underneath.

**Roof weight:** Metal roofing is actually lighter than asphalt shingles per square foot in most cases, which is rarely a limiting factor for either choice on a standard residential structure.

**Walking on the roof:** Standing seam metal can be more slippery and requires more care when walking on it for maintenance (gutter cleaning, chimney work) compared to shingles. Some homeowners find this a minor inconvenience for future service calls.

**DIY potential:** Asphalt shingle installation is more commonly attempted as a DIY project, though we generally do not recommend it. Metal roofing, especially standing seam, essentially requires professional installation due to the specialized tools and seaming equipment involved.

## Making the Decision

Ask yourself these questions in order:

1. **How long do you plan to stay in this home?** Under 10 years leans shingles. Over 20 years leans metal.
2. **What is your upfront budget?** If the metal premium is not available without financing, and financing costs would erode the long-term savings, shingles may be the more practical choice regardless of ownership timeline.
3. **What is your climate and disaster risk?** Wildfire, hail, or high-wind exposure shifts the math toward metal, especially once insurance discounts are factored in.
4. **Does your neighborhood and home style support metal aesthetically?** This is a resale consideration worth discussing with a local real estate agent if you are unsure.

Before hiring a contractor for either option, read our guide on [how to find a good contractor](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/), and get quotes for both materials from at least one contractor who installs both, so you are not just getting a shingle-biased or metal-biased opinion.
