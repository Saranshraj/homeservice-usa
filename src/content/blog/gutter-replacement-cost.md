**Most homeowners pay between $900 and $2,100 to replace gutters on an average-sized house, with a national average around $1,400.** That works out to roughly $4 to $12 per linear foot installed, depending on the gutter material and your region. A small ranch-style home with 120 linear feet of gutters might cost as little as $600 for vinyl, while a two-story colonial with 200 feet of copper gutters can run $5,000 or more. Knowing these numbers before you call a contractor is the difference between paying a fair price and getting taken advantage of.

## Why Gutters Matter More Than You Think

Gutters are your home's first line of defense against water damage. When they fail, rainwater pours directly against your foundation, seeps into your [basement](/blog/basement-waterproofing-cost/), erodes landscaping, and soaks the fascia boards behind the gutters. The [Federal Emergency Management Agency (FEMA)](https://www.fema.gov) estimates that water damage is one of the most common and costly home disasters in the US. Replacing failing gutters is almost always cheaper than repairing the damage they would cause if left in place, and water damage from failed gutters may be worth reviewing against your [homeowners insurance coverage](/blog/homeowners-insurance-claims/).

The [National Association of Home Builders (NAHB)](https://www.nahb.org) notes that gutters have an expected lifespan of 20 years for aluminum, 30 years for steel, and 50 or more years for copper. If your gutters are pulling away from the fascia, have visible cracks, leak at seams, or show rust staining on your [siding](/blog/siding-replacement-cost/), replacement is likely overdue.

## Gutter Replacement Cost by Material

Material choice is the biggest driver of your total cost. Here is how the most common options compare:

| Material | Cost Per Linear Foot (Installed) | Lifespan | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Vinyl | $3-$6 | 10-20 years | Tight budgets, mild climates |
| Aluminum | $5-$10 | 20-30 years | Most homes, best value |
| Steel (galvanized) | $8-$13 | 20-30 years | Heavy snowfall regions |
| Zinc | $12-$20 | 40-50 years | Long-term investment |
| Copper | $15-$30 | 50+ years | Historic homes, premium curb appeal |

### Vinyl Gutters: $3-$6 per linear foot

Vinyl is the least expensive option and installs easily, which is why it is popular for DIY projects. The downside is that vinyl becomes brittle in freezing temperatures and fades in intense UV sunlight. If you live in the South or have a mild climate, vinyl can be a perfectly reasonable choice. In Minnesota, Wisconsin, or upstate New York, vinyl tends to crack within 10 years. A typical 150-foot vinyl installation runs $450 to $900.

### Aluminum Gutters: $5-$10 per linear foot

Aluminum is the most popular material for a reason: it does not rust, holds paint well, comes in dozens of colors, and lasts two to three decades. Seamless aluminum gutters (which run in one continuous piece from corner to corner with no joints to leak) are by far the most common professional installation. Expect to pay $750 to $1,500 for a typical home.

### Steel Gutters: $8-$13 per linear foot

Galvanized steel holds up better than aluminum in heavy snow and ice, making it popular in the Great Lakes region and New England. The trade-off is weight: steel gutters require more brackets and eventually rust if the galvanized coating is scratched. Half-round steel gutters are common on older homes. Typical cost for 150 feet: $1,200 to $1,950.

### Copper Gutters: $15-$30 per linear foot

Copper is a luxury product with a price tag to match, but it develops a beautiful patina over time, essentially never needs painting, and will outlast the roof and possibly the house itself. Copper gutters are typical on historic homes, high-end renovations, and custom builds. A full copper gutter installation on a large home can cost $4,000 to $8,000 or more.

## Seamless vs. Sectional Gutters

Most contractors today install seamless gutters, which are fabricated on-site from a single roll of metal and cut to the exact length of each run. Sectional gutters come in 10-foot or 20-foot pieces that are joined with connectors.

| Type | Cost Premium | Leak Risk | DIY Friendly? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sectional | Baseline price | Higher (joints leak) | Yes |
| Seamless | Add $1-$2 per linear foot | Much lower | No (requires machine) |

For most homeowners, seamless gutters are worth the small upcharge. Fewer joints mean fewer places for leaves to clog, fewer places to leak, and less maintenance over the gutter's lifetime.

## Labor Costs: What You Are Really Paying For

Labor typically runs $1 to $4 per linear foot on top of material costs. On a 160-foot project, that adds $160 to $640. Several factors push labor costs up:

**Two-story homes:** Working from tall ladders or scaffolding on a 20-foot wall costs more than ground-level work. Expect a 20 to 40 percent premium on labor.

**Steep or complex rooflines:** Homes with many corners, valleys, or hips require more cuts, mitered corners, and downspout placements. Each inside or outside corner adds $5 to $25.

**Downspout count and placement:** Downspouts typically run $20 to $60 each, and most homes need one for every 35 to 40 feet of gutter run.

**Fascia board replacement:** If your old gutters caused rot in the fascia boards behind them, those boards must be replaced before new gutters are installed. Fascia replacement adds $1 to $3 per linear foot for the boards plus labor.

**Old gutter removal:** Most contractors charge $0.50 to $1.50 per linear foot to remove and haul away your old gutters. Some include this in the total price, so ask.

## Regional Cost Differences

Where you live significantly affects what you pay, mostly because of labor market differences and regional demand.

| Region | Typical Range (150 LF, Aluminum) |
|---|---|
| Southeast (FL, GA, SC) | $700-$1,400 |
| Midwest (OH, IN, IL) | $750-$1,500 |
| South Central (TX, OK, AR) | $750-$1,450 |
| Mountain West (CO, UT, ID) | $900-$1,800 |
| Pacific Northwest (WA, OR) | $1,000-$2,000 |
| Northeast (NY, MA, CT) | $1,100-$2,200 |
| California (Bay Area, LA) | $1,200-$2,400 |

Cities with high construction costs, strong unions, or limited contractor supply push prices toward the top of those ranges. Rural areas in the South and Midwest tend to be the most affordable.

## Gutter Add-Ons and Their Costs

### Gutter Guards: $500-$2,500+ for a typical home

Gutter guards are screens, filters, or reverse-curve systems that keep leaves and debris from clogging your gutters. The [Consumer Product Safety Commission (CPSC)](https://www.cpsc.gov) reports that falls from ladders cause more than 160,000 emergency room visits per year. If gutter guards mean you are never cleaning gutters from a ladder again, that is a real safety benefit in addition to the maintenance savings.

Guard quality and cost vary enormously:

| Guard Type | Cost Per Linear Foot | Maintenance Level |
|---|---|---|
| Plastic mesh screen | $0.40-$1.00 | High (clogs in pine needles) |
| Aluminum micro-mesh | $1.50-$4.00 | Low |
| Foam inserts | $2-$4 | Medium |
| Surface tension (reverse curve) | $3-$6 | Low |
| Professional micro-mesh | $6-$12 | Very low |

Premium brands like LeafFilter, LeafGuard, and MasterShield charge $8 to $12 per linear foot, which works out to $1,200 to $1,800 for a typical home on top of gutter installation. For that price you also get a warranty. Cheaper aluminum micro-mesh screens at $1.50 to $4 per linear foot perform nearly as well for most homeowners.

### Downspout Extensions: $5-$20 each

Standard downspouts typically terminate 4 to 6 inches from your foundation. That is often not enough. Extensions, splash blocks, and underground drainage pipes move water further from your home. Underground drain pipes from downspouts to a pop-up emitter in the yard run $200 to $600 per downspout including installation.

### Gutter Heating Cables: $300-$1,000 installed

Homeowners in cold climates sometimes add electric heat cables to prevent ice dams. These are typically $3 to $8 per linear foot installed and use a thermostat to activate when temperatures drop. The [Department of Energy](https://www.energy.gov) notes that proper attic insulation and ventilation address the root cause of ice dams more effectively and at lower long-term cost, but heat cables are a helpful stopgap.

## Signs You Need Replacement vs. Repair

Not every gutter problem requires a full replacement. Repairs run $75 to $400 and make sense when:

- Only one or two sections are leaking at a seam (reseal with gutter caulk)
- A downspout is clogged or disconnected (often a free DIY fix)
- A gutter has pulled away from the fascia in one spot (re-anchor with new spikes or screws)
- A single section is bent or damaged after a storm

Full replacement makes more sense when:

- Gutters are sagging along multiple runs (means the fascia behind them has rotted)
- Rust or cracks appear in multiple places
- The gutters are more than 20 years old and have had repeated problems
- You are [replacing the roof](/blog/roof-replacement-cost/) (gutters are removed anyway; replace them now at a discount)

## Can You DIY Gutter Installation?

Sectional gutters in vinyl or aluminum are technically DIY-friendly and the materials cost $100 to $300 for a small home at a home improvement store. The project requires a ladder, a saw, gutter sealant, and a few hours of work.

However, most homeowners hire out gutter replacement for good reasons:

- Seamless gutters cannot be done DIY (you need the rolling machine)
- Working at height on a two-story home is genuinely dangerous
- A contractor carries liability insurance; you do not
- Improperly pitched gutters do not drain and create new problems

If you are comfortable on a ladder and your home is single-story, DIY sectional gutters are a viable option. For two-story homes or anything requiring seamless gutters, hire a pro.

## How to Get a Fair Quote

**Get at least three quotes.** Gutter installation is a competitive market, and bids can vary by 30 to 50 percent for the same scope. Always compare apples to apples: material type, seamless vs. sectional, whether fascia replacement and hauling are included.

**Ask about [contractor licensing](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/).** Most states require contractors to be licensed for work above a certain dollar amount. You can verify licenses through your state's contractor licensing board (search "[your state] contractor license verification"). An unlicensed contractor who causes property damage may leave you with no recourse.

**Check the pitch.** Gutters must slope slightly toward the downspouts (about 1/4 inch per 10 feet) to drain properly. Ask your contractor how they ensure proper pitch, or watch them use a level during installation.

**Understand the warranty.** Manufacturer warranties on aluminum gutters typically run 20 years on the material. Labor warranties from reputable contractors run 1 to 5 years. Get both in writing.

**Avoid paying the full amount upfront.** A reasonable deposit is 25 to 33 percent. Pay the remainder on completion after you have walked around and inspected the work.

## How Many Linear Feet Does Your Home Have?

A rough estimate is easy: walk the perimeter of your home and measure the total length of all exterior walls that have a roof edge above them. For most ranch-style homes, that is 120 to 150 linear feet. For a two-story colonial or home with a complex roofline, it could be 175 to 250 feet. Your contractor will measure precisely before quoting.

## Sample Project Costs

To make this concrete, here are sample totals for three common scenarios:

**Small ranch home (120 linear feet, aluminum seamless, single-story, Midwest)**
- Material: 120 ft x $6 = $720
- Labor: 120 ft x $2.50 = $300
- 3 downspouts x $35 = $105
- Removal of old gutters: 120 ft x $1 = $120
- **Total estimate: $1,245**

**Two-story colonial (190 linear feet, aluminum seamless, Northeast)**
- Material: 190 ft x $7 = $1,330
- Labor: 190 ft x $3.50 = $665 (two-story premium)
- 5 downspouts x $45 = $225
- 2 inside corners x $20 = $40
- Removal: 190 ft x $1.25 = $238
- **Total estimate: $2,498**

**Historic home (160 linear feet, copper half-round, Southeast)**
- Material: 160 ft x $22 = $3,520
- Specialty labor: 160 ft x $6 = $960
- 4 downspouts x $150 = $600
- **Total estimate: $5,080**
