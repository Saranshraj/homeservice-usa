Replacing a garbage disposal costs **$150 to $650 installed**, with most homeowners landing around **$300 to $400** when a licensed plumber does the work. If you already own the unit and feel comfortable under the sink, a DIY swap runs just **$80 to $250** for the disposal itself. The price swings that much because motor horsepower, brand, and whether you need any electrical or plumbing work all factor in.

This guide breaks down every cost so you can walk into that plumber conversation knowing exactly what you should pay.

---

## What Drives the Price of Garbage Disposal Replacement

Before getting into numbers, it helps to understand what you are actually paying for. Most quotes bundle three things: the unit itself, the labor to swap it out, and any extras your kitchen needs (new drain basket, outlet installation, etc.).

### The Disposal Unit: $50 to $400

The disposal is the biggest cost variable. Units are sold primarily by motor size, measured in horsepower (HP). More power means fewer jams, quieter operation, and a longer lifespan, but also a higher sticker price.

| Motor Size | Best For | Price Range |
|---|---|---|
| 1/3 HP | Occasional single-person use, soft foods only | $50 to $100 |
| 1/2 HP | Couples, light daily use | $80 to $150 |
| 3/4 HP | Average family of 4, regular cooking | $120 to $200 |
| 1 HP | Large families, frequent cooking, harder foods | $180 to $400 |
| 1+ HP (commercial-grade) | Heavy use, fibrous vegetables, occasional bones | $250 to $500+ |

For most households, a **3/4 HP unit is the sweet spot.** It handles everyday scraps without the premium price of a 1 HP model. Going too cheap with a 1/3 HP unit often means replacing it again in three to four years rather than the seven to twelve years a quality disposal can last.

### Labor Cost: $100 to $250

A licensed plumber typically charges **$100 to $250 in labor** for a straightforward swap-out. The job takes 30 to 90 minutes. Rates vary considerably by region:

- **Northeast (NY, MA, CT):** $175 to $300
- **Pacific Coast (CA, WA, OR):** $150 to $275
- **Midwest (IL, OH, MI):** $100 to $200
- **South (TX, FL, GA):** $90 to $175
- **Mountain West (CO, AZ, UT):** $110 to $220

Some plumbers charge a flat fee for a disposal swap; others bill by the hour at $75 to $175 per hour. Ask upfront which pricing model they use.

## Brand Comparison: Which Disposal Is Worth the Money?

Three brands dominate the US market, each with a loyal following. Here is an honest breakdown:

### InSinkErator (Emerson)
The most recognized name in garbage disposals. The Evolution series is quiet and powerful; the Badger series is budget-friendly but louder. Expect a **1 to 2 year warranty on Badger models** and up to **8 years on Evolution** units. Prices run $70 to $450 depending on model.

**Best for:** Homeowners who want wide parts availability and strong dealer support.

### Waste King (Legend Series)
Often recommended by budget-conscious plumbers as a reliable alternative. Waste King's permanent magnet motors run faster (2,700 RPM vs. InSinkErator's 1,725 RPM), which reduces jams. Prices range from $60 to $200, and they offer **lifetime in-home warranty** on higher-end models.

**Best for:** Value-focused buyers who cook frequently and want jam-resistant performance.

### Moen
A relative newcomer to garbage disposals but brings strong brand quality. Moen units are quiet and come with solid warranty coverage (up to 10 years on the GX Pro series). Prices run $100 to $300.

**Best for:** Homeowners who already love Moen faucets and want a cohesive kitchen ecosystem.

## Should You Repair or Replace Your Garbage Disposal?

This is the question most homeowners wrestle with. As a general rule: if the repair costs more than 50 percent of a new unit's price, replace it. Here is a quick decision guide:

**Repair it if:**
- The disposal hums but does not spin (usually a jammed flywheel; fix with the hex key that came with the unit, or $0 to $50 with a plumber)
- It trips the reset button repeatedly (can indicate an overload; press the red reset button on the bottom first)
- It leaks from the top at the sink flange (a $20 to $50 repair)

**Replace it if:**
- The motor makes grinding or rattling noises that do not go away after clearing a jam
- It leaks from the bottom or sides (internal seal failure)
- It is older than 10 to 12 years
- The reset button no longer holds
- Repairs cost more than $100 on a unit over seven years old

## Factors That Can Raise Your Total Cost

A standard disposal swap is inexpensive, but a few situations can push the price up:

### No Existing Electrical Outlet
Disposals need a dedicated 120-volt circuit under the sink. Most kitchens already have one, but if yours does not, an electrician needs to install it. Budget **$150 to $350** for this additional work. This is common in older homes built before disposals were standard, and in some rentals converted to owner-occupied homes.

### Corroded or Damaged Drain Basket
The drain basket (the metal ring at the sink drain opening) often corrodes and needs replacing when you swap disposals. Replacing it adds **$50 to $100** in parts and labor. A good plumber will check this during the job.

### Hard-to-Access Under-Sink Area
If your under-sink cabinet is cramped, has a lot of plumbing for a dishwasher, or has cabinets that are difficult to work around, some plumbers charge more. This is rarely a huge add-on, usually **$25 to $75**, but worth asking about.

### Septic System Considerations
If your home uses a septic system, you should know that the [EPA cautions](https://www.epa.gov/septic/how-care-your-septic-system) that garbage disposals add significant organic waste to your tank and can reduce pump intervals. If you have a septic system, look for a disposal specifically rated for septic use (like InSinkErator's Evolution Septic Assist) and budget for more frequent pumping. These specialized units cost **$200 to $350**.

## DIY Garbage Disposal Replacement: Is It Worth Trying?

If you are comfortable kneeling under the sink, have basic tools, and can follow directions, a garbage disposal swap is one of the more approachable DIY plumbing projects. The process generally takes 45 to 90 minutes.

**You will need:**
- A screwdriver (flathead and Phillips)
- Plumber's putty or silicone sealant
- Bucket and towels
- The disposal unit

**When to call a pro instead:**
- You need to run new electrical wiring
- Your existing plumbing looks corroded or damaged
- You are not confident disconnecting and reconnecting drain lines
- Your home is older with galvanized or cast iron pipes that crack easily

Most disposal manufacturers include detailed installation videos. InSinkErator's and Waste King's YouTube channels both have step-by-step guides tailored to their mounting systems (and mounting systems differ, so the old unit's brand matters).

## Signs Your Garbage Disposal Needs Replacing Now

You already know disposals do not last forever, but here are the clearest signals that replacement is the right call rather than another repair attempt:

**Frequent resets.** Pressing the reset button once or twice a year is normal. If you are pressing it every week, the motor is wearing out.

**Persistent odors.** If cleaning with ice, salt, and citrus does not fix a sour smell, the grinding chamber may have cracks or buildup that cannot be cleaned out.

**Slow draining.** This often points to the disposal rather than a clog downstream. If a plumber clears the drain and it slows again within weeks, the unit is the culprit.

**Visible rust or leaks.** Any moisture dripping from the body of the disposal means internal corrosion. That is not fixable; it is replaceable.

**Age.** The [Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov) does not set a mandatory lifespan for disposals, but most manufacturers and plumbers agree on a practical service life of **8 to 12 years** for quality units and **5 to 7 years** for budget models.

## Questions to Ask Your Plumber Before You Commit

Getting a fair price means asking the right questions before any work begins:

1. "Does your quote include parts and labor, or just labor?"
2. "What brand and model are you recommending, and why?"
3. "Is there an extra charge if the drain basket needs replacing?"
4. "Do you haul away the old unit?"
5. "What warranty do you offer on your labor?"
6. "Are you licensed in this state?"

A reputable plumber should answer all of these without hesitation. If they resist itemizing the quote, that is a red flag.

## How to Save Money on Garbage Disposal Replacement

You do not have to pay premium rates. A few strategies work well:

**Buy your own unit.** Plumbers mark up parts significantly, often 20 to 50 percent above retail. Buy your preferred disposal at a home improvement store or online, then ask the plumber to install it (many will; some charge slightly more for "customer-supplied materials," but you still often come out ahead).

**Get three quotes.** Disposal replacement is competitive. Three quotes from licensed plumbers in your area will quickly reveal a fair local rate.

**Avoid emergency calls.** If the disposal can wait, schedule during regular business hours. Emergency rates add $50 to $150 or more.

**Use manufacturer rebates.** Both InSinkErator and Waste King run seasonal rebates, often in spring and fall. Rebates of $20 to $50 are common and easy to miss if you do not check the manufacturer's website before buying.
