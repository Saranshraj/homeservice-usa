**Most garage door repairs cost $100 to $600**, with the national average around **$200 to $300** for common fixes like broken springs, snapped cables, or a malfunctioning opener. Simple adjustments (track realignment, sensor cleaning, lubrication) can run as little as $75 to $100 for a service call. On the higher end, replacing multiple springs on a heavy commercial-grade door or installing a brand-new smart opener can push the bill past $600. If repair quotes exceed $800 for an older door, replacement is often the smarter financial move. Read on for a complete breakdown by repair type, including what drives costs up in your state.

## Why Garage Door Repair Costs Vary So Much

Before diving into specific numbers, it helps to understand what moves the needle on price:

- **Type of repair:** A sensor realignment takes 15 minutes; a broken spring replacement takes 45 to 90 minutes and requires specialized tools.
- **Single vs. double door:** Double doors are heavier and use heavier-duty hardware. Spring replacements on double doors typically cost 30 to 50 percent more.
- **Door age and brand:** Parts for older or discontinued brands are harder to source, which adds cost.
- **Your location:** Labor rates in California, New York, and Massachusetts run 20 to 40 percent above the national average. Rural Midwest and South regions tend to be cheapest.
- **Emergency vs. scheduled service:** Emergency weekend or after-hours calls add $50 to $150 to any repair.

## Garage Door Repair Cost by Problem Type

### Broken Spring Replacement: $150 to $350

Broken springs are the single most common garage door repair. Springs do the heavy lifting when your door opens and closes, and most are rated for 10,000 cycles (about 7 to 10 years of typical use before they fail).

There are two spring types:

| Spring Type | Typical Cost | Location on Door |
|---|---|---|
| Torsion spring (1 spring) | $150 to $250 | Above the door, horizontal |
| Torsion spring (2 springs) | $200 to $350 | Above the door, horizontal |
| Extension spring (per pair) | $100 to $200 | Along the side tracks |

**Safety note from the Consumer Product Safety Commission ([cpsc.gov](https://www.cpsc.gov)):** Garage door springs are under extreme tension and are responsible for injuries and deaths every year. This is one repair where the cost of hiring a pro is clearly worth it. Never attempt spring replacement without professional training.

Most reputable companies replace springs in pairs even if only one broke. This avoids a second service call when the older companion spring fails shortly after.

### Garage Door Opener Repair or Replacement: $100 to $500

Opener problems range from minor to major:

| Issue | Typical Repair Cost |
|---|---|
| Remote reprogramming | $50 to $75 (often free with service call) |
| Replace wall button | $75 to $125 |
| Replace safety sensors | $85 to $150 |
| Repair motor/circuit board | $100 to $200 |
| Replace entire opener unit | $250 to $500 installed |

If your opener is more than 10 to 15 years old and needs a major repair, replacement often makes more sense. A new belt-drive smart opener from brands like Chamberlain, LiftMaster, or Genie costs $150 to $350 in materials plus $100 to $150 for installation.

Smart openers with Wi-Fi connectivity and smartphone control run $200 to $450 installed and are increasingly worth the small premium for the convenience and added home security.

### Broken Cable Replacement: $100 to $200

Cables run along the sides of your door and work with the springs to lift it smoothly. When a cable snaps or frays, the door may hang crooked or become impossible to open.

Most cable replacements take under an hour and cost:
- **Single door:** $100 to $175 per cable (cables usually come in pairs)
- **Double door:** $150 to $200 per pair

Like springs, cables are under significant tension and are not a safe DIY project.

### Track Repair or Realignment: $75 to $200

Bent or misaligned tracks cause grinding, sticking, and uneven movement. A technician can often realign a slightly bent track without replacing it, which keeps costs low.

- **Track realignment (both sides):** $75 to $150
- **Track section replacement:** $125 to $200 per section, including labor

If debris, ice, or an accidental vehicle bump bent the track severely, full replacement of one side typically costs $125 to $200.

### Panel Replacement: $150 to $700

Dented or damaged door panels are often cosmetic, but deep dents can compromise insulation and structural integrity. Panel replacement costs depend heavily on door material and whether matching panels are still available:

| Door Material | Single Panel Replacement |
|---|---|
| Steel (standard) | $150 to $300 |
| Wood | $250 to $500 |
| Aluminum/glass | $300 to $700 |
| Insulated steel | $200 to $400 |

Important caveat: if your door is more than 10 years old or a discontinued model, matching replacement panels may not be available. In that case, full door replacement becomes the only aesthetically clean option.

### Weatherstripping Replacement: $75 to $200

The rubber seal along the bottom of your door wears out every 3 to 5 years. When it cracks or pulls away, cold air, water, and pests enter your garage.

- **Bottom weatherstrip only:** $75 to $150 (often a DIY-friendly job)
- **Full perimeter weatherstrip (all four sides):** $150 to $250

This is one of the few garage door repairs where an experienced DIYer can save the full labor cost. Replacement bottom seals cost $15 to $40 at home improvement stores, and installation requires only a screwdriver and about 30 minutes.

### Roller and Hinge Replacement: $100 to $175

Rollers and hinges wear down over time and cause noisy, jerky operation. Replacing all rollers and hinges on a standard door typically costs $100 to $175 in labor plus $20 to $60 in parts.

Nylon rollers (quieter, longer lasting) cost slightly more than steel but are worth the upgrade if you have a bedroom above the garage.

### Garage Door Keypad or Smart Lock: $75 to $200

Adding or replacing a keypad entry system:
- **Basic keypad installation:** $75 to $125
- **Smart lock with app control:** $150 to $200 installed

## Repair vs. Replace: When to Call It

This is the question most homeowners struggle with. Here is a simple framework:

**Repair makes sense when:**
- The door is less than 10 years old
- The repair cost is under 50 percent of replacement cost
- The door has no structural or panel damage
- Springs or opener are the only problem

**Replacement makes sense when:**
- The repair quote exceeds $600 to $800
- The door is 15 to 20 years old and showing multiple problems
- The door lacks insulation and you are losing energy through the garage
- Replacement panels are unavailable for cosmetic damage
- You want to upgrade to a smart opener and better insulation at the same time

According to Remodeling Magazine's Cost vs. Value report, new garage doors return about 103 percent of cost at resale, making replacement one of the few home improvements that actually pays for itself.

## How to Save Money on Garage Door Repairs

**Get three quotes.** Garage door repair is a competitive market in most cities. Prices for the same spring replacement can vary $100 or more between companies.

**Ask about parts brands.** Some companies upsell premium parts you do not need. Standard torsion springs from reputable brands like Ideal or Clopay perform well for most residential doors.

**Bundle small repairs.** If you need a new spring and your rollers are noisy, have the technician handle both while they are already on-site. The marginal labor cost for small add-ons is low.

**Check for manufacturer warranties.** New garage doors often come with 1 to 10 year warranties on parts. Check your documentation before paying for a repair that might be covered.

**Join a home warranty.** Home warranties from companies like American Home Shield or Choice Home Warranty cover garage door openers and sometimes springs, capping your out-of-pocket cost at $75 to $125 per claim. Worth considering if your door and opener are aging.

**Avoid after-hours calls when possible.** If the door is stuck open and a security concern, you may have no choice. But if the door is stuck closed and your car is not trapped, scheduling a next-day appointment saves $50 to $150 over emergency rates.

## What the Service Call Covers

Most garage door companies charge a **$50 to $85 service/diagnostic fee** just to come out and inspect the problem. This fee is usually waived or applied toward the repair if you proceed with the work.

Always ask upfront: "Is your service fee separate from the repair cost, or is it applied?" This single question prevents an unpleasant surprise on the final bill.

## Warning Signs You Need a Repair Soon

Do not wait until the door will not open to call for service. Address these warning signs early to prevent a small repair from becoming an expensive emergency:

- **Squealing or grinding noise:** Often worn rollers or lack of lubrication. A $10 can of garage door lubricant (not WD-40) may fix this yourself.
- **Door opens or closes unevenly:** Usually a cable or spring showing wear.
- **Door reverses before closing fully:** Most likely a sensor alignment issue, often fixable in minutes.
- **Slow movement:** Could be motor wear in the opener, friction in the tracks, or spring tension loss.
- **Door feels heavy when manually lifting:** Springs are losing tension and working overtime. This puts stress on the opener motor too.
- **Visible rust or cracks on springs:** Replace before they snap, not after.

The International Door Association recommends annual lubrication and visual inspection of springs, cables, rollers, and tracks. A $10 tube of silicone-based lubricant once a year is far cheaper than an emergency repair.

## Is This a Safe DIY Project?

For most garage door repairs, the honest answer is no, except for the very minor tasks listed below.

**Safe for most homeowners:**
- Lubricating rollers, hinges, and springs with appropriate lubricant
- Cleaning and realigning safety sensors (wipe with a dry cloth, adjust angle)
- Replacing weatherstripping at the bottom of the door
- Reprogramming a remote or replacing the remote battery

**Leave to the pros:**
- Spring replacement (serious injury risk from stored tension)
- Cable replacement (same risk)
- Opener motor or circuit board work
- Track replacement
- Any repair involving the torsion bar assembly

The U.S. Consumer Product Safety Commission ([cpsc.gov](https://www.cpsc.gov)) has documented injuries and deaths from garage door spring failures during DIY repairs. The $100 to $200 you save is not worth the risk.

## How to Find a Reputable Garage Door Repair Company

Garage door repair has a higher-than-average rate of scam operations, particularly companies that advertise unrealistically low prices and then upsell unnecessary parts on-site.

**Red flags to watch for:**
- Quotes for spring replacement under $50 (real parts and labor do not come that cheap)
- High-pressure same-day upsells for full door replacement when you called about a minor issue
- Companies that cannot provide a written quote before starting work
- Cash-only payment requirements
- No physical business address or license number

**Green flags to look for:**
- Licensed and insured in your state (most states require contractor licensing for door installation)
- Reviews on Google, Yelp, and the Better Business Bureau ([bbb.org](https://www.bbb.org)) from the past 6 months
- Upfront written estimate before work begins
- Clear explanation of parts being used (brand and model)
- Warranty on parts and labor (look for at least 90 days; good companies offer 1 year)

For finding licensed contractors in your state, check your state contractor licensing board or the National Association of Home Builders ([nahb.org](https://www.nahb.org)) contractor locator.
