Wallpaper removal costs **$1 to $4 per square foot** when you hire a professional, which works out to **$200 to $850 for a standard 12x12 bedroom**. Larger rooms with multiple layers of old wallpaper, or surfaces that need significant wall repair afterward, can run $1,500 to $3,000 or more. If you tackle it yourself, your out-of-pocket cost drops to $30 to $80 for tools and supplies, though the labor is genuinely hard work and can take a full weekend.

If you just discovered wallpaper under a fresh coat of paint in your new home, or you are finally ready to update that dated floral pattern from 1992, you are not alone. Wallpaper removal is one of the top renovation projects US homeowners undertake each year. The good news: understanding what drives the cost helps you budget accurately and avoid the surprises that catch so many people off guard.

## What Affects Wallpaper Removal Cost

### 1. Number of Layers

This is the single biggest cost driver. Many older homes, especially those built before 1980, have two, three, or even four layers of wallpaper stacked on top of each other. Each additional layer multiplies the labor time and can easily double or triple your bill. If you are buying an older home, ask the inspector specifically whether wallpaper has been painted over, which makes removal significantly harder.

### 2. Type of Wallpaper

Modern vinyl-coated wallpaper is the easiest to remove. The vinyl layer peels off in large sheets, leaving behind a paper backing that soaks off with water and fabric softener. The harder types include:

- **Fabric-backed or woven wallpaper**: requires scoring and extended soaking, adding 50 to 100 percent more labor
- **Grasscloth or natural fiber wallpaper**: extremely delicate to remove without gouging the drywall beneath
- **Pre-pasted vintage paper**: often absorbs into drywall paper facing and tears walls when removed
- **Wallpaper glued directly to unprimed drywall**: the worst scenario; removing it almost always damages the drywall face, requiring skim coating

### 3. Wall Surface Underneath

Plaster walls (common in homes built before 1950) are actually more forgiving than drywall when it comes to wallpaper removal. Plaster is dense and hard; moisture does not damage it the way it can damage drywall. Modern drywall, on the other hand, is vulnerable. If the original installer did not apply a primer sealer before hanging the wallpaper, the removal process can tear off the paper facing of the drywall. Fixing that damage with skim coat adds $0.50 to $2 per square foot on top of removal costs.

### 4. Room Size and Ceiling Height

Standard 8-foot ceilings are priced into most estimates. Rooms with 9 or 10-foot ceilings require ladder work, which slows everything down. Vaulted ceilings or stairwells with high walls can add 25 to 50 percent to the base price, since scaffolding or specialty ladders may be required.

### 5. Prep and Cleanup

A reputable contractor protects floors, furniture, and outlets before starting. Expect 30 to 60 minutes of prep time for a bedroom-size room. Some companies charge separately for cleanup and disposal; others include it. Always confirm this in your written estimate.

## DIY vs. Hiring a Professional

Removing wallpaper yourself is completely doable, but it is one of those projects that takes about three times longer than you expect. Here is an honest comparison.

| Factor | DIY | Professional |
|---|---|---|
| Labor cost | Free | $1 to $4 per sq ft |
| Supplies | $30 to $80 | Included |
| Time (bedroom) | 8 to 20 hours | 3 to 6 hours |
| Risk of wall damage | Moderate to high | Low to moderate |
| Wall repair if needed | Additional cost | May be included |
| Quality of result | Variable | Consistent |

**When DIY makes sense:** You have a modern home with single-layer vinyl wallpaper on properly primed drywall, you have the time and patience, and the room has straightforward geometry with no vaulted ceilings or intricate trim work.

**When to hire a pro:** You have an older home, multiple wallpaper layers, grasscloth or natural fiber wallpaper, unprimed drywall you suspect was never sealed, or you are on a tight timeline before painting begins.

### DIY Supplies You Will Need

- Wallpaper scorer ($10 to $20): perforates the surface so water can penetrate
- Spray bottle or garden sprayer ($10 to $25): applies warm water or removal solution
- Wide plastic scraper or drywall knife ($5 to $15): removes soaked paper without gouging
- Drop cloths ($10 to $30): protects floors from water and paper debris
- DIF wallpaper removal solution ($15 to $25 for a concentrate): more effective than plain water
- Sponges and buckets ($5 to $15)

Always score lightly. Pressing too hard gouges drywall beneath the paper, creating repair work that costs far more than the tool rental.

## Lead Paint Warning for Homes Built Before 1978

If your home was built before 1978, there is a real chance the wall surfaces behind or beneath the wallpaper contain lead-based paint. When wallpaper is removed and the walls are sanded or scraped, lead dust can become airborne.

The [EPA's Lead Renovation, Repair and Painting Rule (RRP)](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program) requires contractors working in pre-1978 homes to be certified in lead-safe work practices. When hiring a contractor, ask specifically whether they are EPA RRP certified. You can verify certification at the EPA's contractor lookup tool at epa.gov. If you are going DIY, the [CPSC recommends](https://www.cpsc.gov/Safety-Education/Safety-Guides/Home-Tips/Lead) using an EPA-approved lead test kit before you start work.

## What Happens After the Wallpaper Is Off?

Removing the wallpaper is usually only the first step. Here is what typically follows.

### Wall Repair and Skim Coating

Almost every wallpaper removal job leaves some damage: torn drywall paper, residual glue, uneven texture, nail holes, or small gouges. Light sanding and a coat of oil-based primer often handles minor imperfections. For moderate damage, a skim coat (a thin layer of drywall compound spread over the entire wall) creates a smooth surface. Expect to pay $1 to $2 per square foot for skim coating, plus $0.50 to $1 per square foot for priming.

### Painting

After repairs and priming, walls are ready for paint. Interior painting typically costs $2 to $4 per square foot including labor and materials, or $400 to $900 for a standard bedroom. Many contractors will give you a bundled discount if you combine wallpaper removal, wall repair, and painting in a single project.

## How to Find a Good Wallpaper Removal Contractor

### Check Licensing and Insurance

Wallpaper removal and painting contractors should be licensed in your state and carry both general liability insurance and workers' compensation coverage. Licensing requirements vary by state; check your state contractor licensing board to verify credentials. For example:
- California: [CSLB (cslb.ca.gov)](https://www.cslb.ca.gov)
- Texas: [TDLR (tdlr.texas.gov)](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov)
- Florida: [DBPR (myfloridalicense.com)](https://www.myfloridalicense.com)

### Get Three Written Estimates

Do not accept a verbal quote. A written estimate should specify the number of layers being removed, what wall repair is included, whether primer is included, the total square footage, and payment terms. Any estimate that asks for more than 25 percent upfront before work begins is a red flag.

### Ask About Post-Removal Wall Assessment

A trustworthy contractor will assess the wall condition after removal before painting begins, and will tell you if additional repair work is needed and what it will cost. If a contractor promises a fixed final price before they can see the wall condition, be cautious.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can wallpaper be painted over instead of removed?**
Technically yes, but it is almost never recommended. Paint-over jobs tend to bubble, peel, and show seams within a year or two, especially in humid rooms like kitchens and bathrooms. The [National Association of Home Builders (NAHB)](https://www.nahb.org) recommends removing wallpaper before painting for any finished result you expect to last more than a few years.

**How long does wallpaper removal take?**
A professional crew typically removes wallpaper from a standard bedroom in 3 to 6 hours. A single-person DIY effort on the same room can take 8 to 20 hours depending on the number of layers and wall condition.

**Does removing wallpaper always damage drywall?**
Not always, but often. Plaster walls, and drywall that was properly primed before the wallpaper was applied, usually come through in good shape. Unprimed drywall is the highest risk situation. If you are buying an older home, budget for some wall repair as part of any wallpaper removal project.

**Is it cheaper to remove the drywall and replace it?**
In extreme cases, like four layers of old wallpaper on unprimed drywall, it can actually be cheaper to remove and replace the drywall than to strip and repair it. A contractor can assess this for you. New drywall costs $1.50 to $3.50 per square foot installed, which can compete with heavy removal and skim coating costs.

## Budgeting Tips

1. Add a 15 to 20 percent contingency to whatever estimate you receive; hidden layers and wall damage are common surprises.
2. If you are painting afterward anyway, bundle the work for a potential 10 to 20 percent discount on the total project.
3. Schedule the project before any other finish work (flooring, trim painting) to avoid overspray and damage.
4. Avoid scheduling during high-humidity seasons if possible; moisture from the removal process takes longer to dry in humid weather, which can delay painting.
