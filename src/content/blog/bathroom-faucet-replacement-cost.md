Replacing a bathroom faucet in the US typically costs **$150 to $400**, with most homeowners paying around $250 when you include both the faucet itself and a plumber's labor. The faucet hardware alone runs $30 to $250 depending on the style and brand, while a licensed plumber charges $100 to $200 for the swap. If you are comfortable with basic plumbing, this is one of the more approachable DIY projects that can cut costs nearly in half.

That dripping faucet or outdated fixture that has been annoying you for months is worth addressing sooner rather than later. A slow drip can waste more than 3,000 gallons of water per year according to the EPA, running up your water bill and adding wear to your pipes. Let's walk through exactly what you will pay, what affects the price, and how to decide whether this is a weekend project or a job for a pro.

## Average Bathroom Faucet Replacement Cost in 2026

| Scenario | Estimated Cost |
|---|---|

| Mid-range faucet + DIY install | $80 to $180 |
| Budget faucet + plumber | $130 to $230 |

## What You Are Actually Paying For

### The Faucet Itself

Walk into any home improvement store and you will find faucets ranging from $25 to well over $500. The price gap is real, and it reflects more than just aesthetics.

**Budget faucets ($25 to $75):** Brands like American Standard's entry-level line and Moen's most basic models fall here. They work, but valves and finishes wear faster. Fine for a guest bathroom that rarely sees heavy use.

**Mid-range faucets ($75 to $200):** This is the sweet spot for most homeowners. Brands like Moen, Delta, and Kohler offer solid cartridge-based valves, lifetime warranties on some models, and finishes (brushed nickel, matte black, polished chrome) that hold up to daily use. Delta's WaterSense-certified models can save a family of four around $350 per year on water and energy costs according to the EPA.

**Premium faucets ($200 to $500+):** Brizo, Grohe, and high-end Kohler fixtures offer designer aesthetics, touch-activated or touchless operation, and very durable ceramic disc valves rated for millions of cycles. Worth it for a primary bathroom you use every day and plan to keep for 20 years.

### Labor Costs

A licensed plumber typically charges **$50 to $150 per hour** for bathroom faucet work, and a standard replacement takes one to two hours. Most plumbers charge a trip or service fee of $50 to $100 even if the job is quick, so expect to pay $100 to $200 for a straightforward single-hole or three-hole faucet swap.

If anything unexpected comes up, such as corroded supply lines, a leaking shutoff valve, or water damage under the sink, costs rise. Adding new braided stainless supply lines (which you should do anyway, as rubber lines crack over time) adds $10 to $20 in parts.

## Faucet Types and Their Cost Differences

The style of faucet you choose affects both the product price and the installation complexity.

### Single-Hole Faucets

These mount through one hole in the sink or countertop and often include a side-mounted lever handle or a single centered handle. Installation is the simplest type. Expect to pay **$50 to $300** for the faucet plus $100 to $150 labor.

### Centerset Faucets (3-Hole, 4-Inch Spread)

Two handles and a spout all mounted on a 4-inch base plate that fits a standard 3-hole sink. Very common in builder-grade bathrooms. Similar cost to single-hole: **$40 to $250** for the faucet.

### Widespread Faucets (3-Hole, 8-Inch Spread)

Handles mount separately from the spout, typically 6 to 16 inches apart. More upscale look, common in higher-end bathrooms. Installation is more complex, so budget $150 to $250 for labor. The faucets themselves run **$80 to $500+**.

### Wall-Mount Faucets

These come out of the wall rather than through the sink or countertop. Beautiful in modern and farmhouse bathrooms, but installing one for the first time requires cutting into the wall and rough-in plumbing work. That can push total costs to **$500 to $1,500** for a new installation, though swapping one wall-mount for another is less involved.

### Touchless and Smart Faucets

Motion-activated faucets use battery power (usually 6 AA batteries) or hardwired electricity. They reduce germ spread and water waste. Kohler's Sensate and Moen's MotionSense lines run **$150 to $400**, and installation is similar to standard faucets except for the sensor connection. These typically earn an EPA WaterSense certification.

## Signs It Is Time to Replace, Not Repair

Before you spend money, it is worth knowing whether a faucet needs replacing or just a simple fix. A cartridge or O-ring replacement (often a $5 to $15 DIY repair) can fix a dripping faucet without buying a new unit.

**Replace when:**
- The faucet is more than 15 to 20 years old and shows visible corrosion or mineral scale buildup that cleaning cannot remove.
- Handles are cracked, the finish is peeling, or the spout wobbles even after tightening.
- Cartridge replacements no longer stop the drip (worn valve seat or body damage).
- You are renovating the bathroom and the faucet no longer matches your vision.
- Calcium and mineral deposits have closed off the aerator holes beyond what a vinegar soak can clear.

**Repair when:**
- The faucet drips at the spout, which usually means a worn cartridge or washer.
- The handle leaks at the base, which typically means a worn O-ring.
- Low water pressure from one faucet, which may just be a clogged aerator (unscrew the tip, rinse it, done).

## DIY Bathroom Faucet Replacement: Is It Worth It?

Replacing a bathroom faucet is one of the friendliest DIY plumbing jobs a homeowner can tackle. You do not need to shut off water to the whole house, just the individual shutoff valves under the sink. Most modern faucets come with clear instructions, and the only tools you typically need are an adjustable wrench, a basin wrench (for hard-to-reach nuts), and plumber's tape.

**DIY saves:** $100 to $200 in labor costs on a typical job.

**DIY is appropriate when:**
- The shutoff valves under the sink work properly (turn them to confirm before you start).
- You are swapping a like-for-like faucet with the same number of holes and similar plumbing connections.
- You are comfortable working in a tight space under the sink.

**Hire a plumber when:**
- The shutoff valves are corroded and will not turn, or they leak when you try to close them.
- You want to change from a 3-hole centerset to a single-hole faucet, which may require plugging holes.
- There is any sign of water damage, mold, or soft wood under the cabinet.
- You are upgrading to a wall-mount faucet (requires rough-in plumbing work).
- You simply do not want the hassle, and that is a completely valid reason.

## Hidden Costs to Watch For

A simple faucet swap can reveal problems that were hiding behind the fixture. Ask your plumber to look at these while they are under the sink.

**Corroded shutoff valves:** If your home was built before 1990, the angle stop valves (the little knobs under the sink) may be original compression-type valves that can crack when turned for the first time in years. Replacing a pair of shutoff valves adds $80 to $150 to the job, but it is worth it for peace of mind.

**Old braided supply lines:** Rubber or corroded metal supply lines are a common source of under-sink water damage. Braided stainless steel lines cost $8 to $15 each and should be replaced with the faucet as a matter of routine.

**Sink drain and p-trap damage:** While your plumber is under the sink, have them check the drain assembly and p-trap. Replacing a leaking p-trap adds $50 to $100 but prevents much larger water damage repairs down the road.

**Countertop hole sizing:** If you are switching from a 3-hole faucet to a single-hole model, the extra holes in your countertop will need cover plates (usually included with widespread faucets) or will need to be patched. Patching granite or marble adds $100 to $300 depending on the material.

## How to Save Money on Bathroom Faucet Replacement

**Buy the faucet yourself:** Plumbers mark up fixtures, sometimes by 30 to 50 percent. Buy directly from a home improvement store or online retailer and provide the faucet to the plumber as supplied materials. Just confirm they are comfortable installing customer-supplied parts (most are, but some have policies about warranty).

**Group small plumbing jobs:** If you have other minor plumbing tasks, such as a running toilet, a slow drain, or a leaky shutoff valve, knock them all out in one visit to avoid paying multiple service call fees.

**Check for rebates:** Many water utilities offer rebates for replacing older faucets with WaterSense-certified models. Check your local utility's website. The EPA's WaterSense program maintains a directory of qualifying products at epa.gov/watersense.

**Time it right:** Plumbers are busiest in the dead of winter (pipe freezes and burst pipes) and early spring (post-freeze repairs). Scheduling non-emergency work in late summer or early fall often means faster scheduling and sometimes lower rates.

## Choosing a Faucet Finish That Lasts

Finish selection affects both aesthetics and long-term durability, especially in bathrooms where the fixture encounters water, toothpaste, and daily handling.

| Finish | Durability | Maintenance | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Polished Chrome | High | Easy (shows fingerprints) | Classic, affordable, highly durable |
| Brushed Nickel | High | Easy (hides fingerprints) | Most popular finish; works in most bath styles |
| Matte Black | Medium | Moderate | Trending; water spots visible; look for PVD coating |
| Oil-Rubbed Bronze | Medium | Moderate | Living finish: may develop patina over time |
| Polished Nickel | High | Moderate | Warm tone; more expensive than brushed nickel |
| Brushed Gold/Champagne | Medium-High | Moderate | Trendy; ensure it is a PVD-coated finish, not just painted |

PVD (physical vapor deposition) coatings last much longer than traditional lacquer finishes and are worth seeking out for matte black or gold faucets.

## What Plumbers Are Licensed to Do (and Why It Matters)

All states require plumbers to be licensed, though the specific requirements vary. The National Inspection Testing and Certification Corporation (NITC) and the International Association of Plumbing and Mechanical Officials (IAPMO) both provide certification programs that many state licensing boards build on.

A licensed plumber carries liability insurance and a bond, meaning that if a faucet they installed leaks and damages your vanity, you have recourse. A handyman or unlicensed worker typically does not carry the same coverage, and some homeowner insurance policies require licensed contractors for plumbing work. Always ask to see a plumber's license number before hiring and verify it through your state's contractor licensing board.
