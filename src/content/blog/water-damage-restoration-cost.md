Water damage restoration costs **$1,300 to $5,600** for most US homeowners in 2026, with the national average around **$3,200** for a moderate incident like a burst pipe or appliance overflow in a single room. Minor damage caught within 24 hours can run as little as **$300 to $800**. Severe flooding, long-term leaks with structural damage, or sewage backup situations can push costs to **$10,000 to $50,000 or more**. The number that matters most is not the average. It is how quickly you call for help, because every 24 to 48 hours you wait can double your final bill as water soaks deeper into walls, floors, and insulation.

If you just walked into standing water in your basement or found a soaking wet ceiling, take a breath. This guide breaks down exactly what you will pay, what your insurance likely covers, and how to avoid the most common water damage scams.

## Water Damage Cost at a Glance

| Damage Level | Typical Cost | What It Usually Means |
|---|---|---|
| Minor (under 10 sq ft) | $300 to $800 | Small leak caught fast, one surface affected |
| Moderate (10 to 100 sq ft) | $1,300 to $5,600 | Burst pipe, appliance flood, one room |
| Significant (100 to 300 sq ft) | $5,000 to $15,000 | Multiple rooms, subfloor or drywall damage |
| Severe (300+ sq ft or structural) | $15,000 to $50,000+ | Flooding, roof failure, sewage backup |

## The Single Biggest Factor: Water Category

Restoration companies use a three-category system established by the Institute of Inspection, Cleaning and Restoration Certification (IICRC) to classify water damage. The category of water determines the safety protocols required, the disposal method for affected materials, and ultimately your bill.

**Category 1: Clean Water** costs **$3 to $4 per square foot** to remediate. This is water from a supply line: a burst pipe, a leaking water heater, an overflowing sink or tub. It poses no immediate health risk and can often be dried in place if caught quickly. This is the least expensive category.

**Category 2: Gray Water** costs **$4 to $6.50 per square foot**. This includes water from dishwashers, washing machines, toilet overflow (urine only), or sump pump failure. It contains contaminants that can cause illness if you come in contact with it, so affected porous materials like carpet and drywall usually need to come out.

**Category 3: Black Water** costs **$7 to $7.50 per square foot** at minimum. This covers sewage backup, flooding from rivers or storm drains, and water that has been sitting for more than 48 hours (which allows bacteria to multiply). Black water jobs require full protective equipment and aggressive removal of all porous materials. This is never a DIY situation.

## What You Are Actually Paying For

A restoration invoice is not one charge. It is a collection of services, and understanding each one helps you spot inflated estimates.

**Water extraction** runs **$1,000 to $2,500** for most homes. Technicians use truck-mounted or portable extraction units to pull standing water out as fast as possible. The faster the water is out, the less it spreads.

**Structural drying** costs **$1,000 to $4,500** depending on the affected area. Industrial air movers and dehumidifiers run continuously, sometimes for three to five days, to bring moisture levels back to normal. Restoration companies typically charge a daily equipment rental rate in the range of $50 to $100 per piece of equipment, and a medium-sized job might require eight to twelve machines.

**Mold prevention treatment** adds **$500 to $2,000** to the total. An antimicrobial spray applied during drying helps prevent mold growth. If mold has already started, that is a separate remediation project. See our [mold remediation cost guide](/blog/mold-remediation-cost/) for full pricing on that scenario.

**Drywall removal and replacement** runs **$1.50 to $3 per square foot** for removal and **$2 to $5 per square foot** for replacement. Water wicks up drywall and turns it into a mold factory, so most contractors remove a one- to two-foot "flood cut" above the water line to ensure everything behind the wall is dry before closing it back up.

**Flooring replacement** varies widely. Saturated carpet typically gets pulled and replaced at **$3 to $11 per square foot** installed. Hardwood floors can sometimes be dried and saved, but warped or buckled boards need replacement at **$8 to $15 per square foot**. Tile usually survives unless the subfloor underneath is damaged.

**Subfloor replacement** is **$3 to $10 per square foot** and often surprises homeowners. The subfloor is the structural wood layer under your finished flooring, and it soaks up water like a sponge. If you smell something musty after a flood repair, the subfloor is usually the culprit.

## Cost by Water Source

Some sources of water damage are more expensive than others because of where they are located and how fast the damage spreads.

| Water Source | Typical Restoration Cost | Why It Varies |
|---|---|---|
| Burst pipe (supply line) | $1,500 to $6,000 | Depends on how long it ran |
| Appliance leak (washer, dishwasher, fridge) | $1,000 to $4,500 | Usually caught faster |
| Roof leak (attic damage) | $2,000 to $8,000 | Often not discovered until mold forms |
| Sewage backup | $3,000 to $15,000 | Black water protocols required |
| Basement flooding (groundwater) | $5,000 to $30,000+ | Structural and waterproofing repairs needed |
| HVAC condensate line overflow | $500 to $3,000 | Often minor if caught early |
| Sump pump failure | $2,500 to $10,000 | Volume depends on storm size |

## State and Regional Price Differences

Labor and material costs vary meaningfully across the US. Coastal states with high humidity and flood risk tend to have more restoration companies competing for work, which can moderate prices, but demand spikes after major storms can push costs up 20 to 40 percent.

States with the highest average restoration costs: California, New York, Hawaii, Massachusetts, and Connecticut, where labor rates and cost of living drive up the invoice.

States with higher disaster frequency but more competitive markets: Florida, Texas, Louisiana, Mississippi, and Alabama. You will find plenty of contractors after a major storm, but demand surges cause price spikes. Always get three quotes.

States with typically lower average costs: Midwest and Mountain West states like Kansas, Iowa, Nebraska, and Wyoming tend to have lower labor rates, though rural locations can add mobilization charges if you are far from a major city.

## What Homeowners Insurance Covers

This is the part that trips up most homeowners, and it is important to get it right before you pay anything.

**Standard homeowners insurance (HO-3 policy) covers** sudden and accidental water damage, including a burst pipe, an appliance malfunction, or an overflow from a sink or tub. If the damage happened quickly and you had no way to know it was coming, your insurance almost certainly covers it.

**Standard homeowners insurance does NOT cover** flooding from outside your home. If a river overflows, storm drain water backs up into your yard, or heavy rain puddles against your foundation and seeps in, that is a flood and requires a separate flood insurance policy. The National Flood Insurance Program (NFIP), administered by FEMA, is the primary source of flood insurance in the US. See [FEMA's flood insurance information](https://www.fema.gov/flood-insurance) for details on coverage and how to purchase a policy.

**Standard homeowners insurance also typically does not cover** damage from a slow or gradual leak that you should have caught over time. If your toilet has been slowly seeping at the base for six months and you never noticed, the insurer may deny the claim as a maintenance issue.

Average homeowners insurance payout for water damage claims runs about **$11,000** according to the Insurance Information Institute, though payouts vary dramatically based on your deductible, policy limits, and the cause of the damage. If you have had a claim denied, read our [homeowners insurance claims guide](/blog/homeowners-insurance-claims/) for how to fight back.

**What to do immediately when you discover water damage:**
1. Stop the source if you safely can (shut off the main water supply if it is a plumbing failure).
2. Document everything with photos and video before touching anything.
3. Call your insurance company to open a claim before hiring a restoration company.
4. Ask your insurer if they have preferred vendors, but you are not required to use them.

## DIY vs. Hiring a Professional

For minor damage involving small areas of clean water caught within 24 hours, some homeowners can handle the drying themselves with rented equipment. Consumer-grade dehumidifiers and fans are available at hardware stores for $50 to $100 per day.

But there are clear situations where you must call a professional. If the affected area is larger than 10 square feet, if the water has been sitting more than 24 hours, if you suspect mold, if any gray or black water is involved, or if structural materials are affected, professional restoration is not optional. It is also required by most insurance policies before a claim can be paid.

A licensed water damage restoration company will use moisture meters to verify that walls, floors, and ceilings are fully dry before closing up. Without those readings, you may seal in moisture that causes mold months later.

## How to Protect Yourself From Water Damage Scams

Major storms and flooding bring out legitimate restoration companies and predatory contractors in equal measure. Here is how to protect yourself.

Never sign an Assignment of Benefits (AOB) agreement that hands over your insurance claim rights to the contractor. This practice is common in Florida and is a major source of fraudulent claim inflation. You want to remain in control of your own insurance claim.

Get at least three written estimates. A legitimate restoration company will provide a line-item estimate using industry-standard pricing software like Xactimate. Vague estimates with large round numbers are a red flag.

Verify licensing and insurance. Most states require water damage contractors to hold a contractor's license. Check your state licensing board and confirm they carry liability insurance and workers' compensation before work begins. Our [guide to finding a good contractor](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/) walks through the full vetting process.

Avoid paying large amounts upfront. A reasonable deposit of 10 to 30 percent is normal. Companies demanding 50 percent or more before work begins are often a red flag.

## What to Expect on the Timeline

Water damage restoration is not a one-day job. Here is a realistic timeline for a moderate incident:

Day 1 to 2: Water extraction and initial assessment. Equipment is placed throughout the affected area.

Day 2 to 5: Active drying. Technicians return daily to check moisture readings and adjust equipment. This phase cannot be rushed, and anyone who promises same-day drying is not being honest.

Day 5 to 7: Final moisture verification. Once all readings are at normal levels, equipment is removed and the space is cleared for reconstruction.

Day 7 to 30+: Reconstruction. Replacing drywall, flooring, cabinets, and other materials takes additional time and may involve separate contractors.
