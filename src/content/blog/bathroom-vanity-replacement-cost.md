**The short answer:** Replacing a bathroom vanity costs between **$300 and $3,800** for most US homeowners in 2026, including the vanity cabinet, sink top, faucet, and professional installation. A standard 36-inch single vanity with a prefab top and new faucet runs **$700 to $1,500** installed. Larger double vanities land at **$1,200 to $3,500** or more. A full custom built-in with stone countertop and premium hardware can push **$4,000 to $8,000** depending on your market.

If your vanity is peeling, water-damaged, wobbling, or just so outdated it makes you cringe every morning, you are in the right place. This is one of those projects that feels intimidating but is actually one of the most rewarding bathroom upgrades you can do. It transforms the entire feel of the room, often for less than you expect. Let us walk through every cost factor so you can go into your project with your eyes open and your budget protected.

## What Drives the Cost Up (or Down)

### 1. Vanity Size

This is the biggest single factor. Every extra inch of cabinet width costs you money in materials and sometimes labor. A 24-inch vanity is fairly cheap to source and easy for one person to install. A 72-inch double vanity is heavy, requires careful leveling, and often needs a second set of hands. Plumbers and handymen factor in that extra time.

- **24 to 30 inches:** Powder rooms and small guest baths. Budget-friendly, wide selection.
- **36 to 48 inches:** The sweet spot for a single-sink primary bath. Best selection at most price points.
- **60 to 72 inches:** Double sink setups, primary suites. Material costs jump substantially.

### 2. Vanity Style and Construction

**Freestanding (stock) vanities** from Home Depot, Lowe's, or IKEA offer the widest range of affordable options. A solid wood-frame vanity with dovetail drawers is more expensive than a particleboard box with a painted finish, and it will also last significantly longer in a humid bathroom.

**Semi-custom vanities** come in standard sizes but with more finish, hardware, and configuration choices. Think of these as the middle ground: better quality than big-box stock, but not as expensive as going fully custom.

**Custom built-in vanities** are designed and built specifically for your bathroom. Cabinetmakers or finish carpenters frame them in place. This is the most expensive option but gives you perfect fit, maximum storage, and a look that stock products cannot match.

### 3. Countertop and Sink Material

The sink top alone can swing your budget considerably:

| Countertop/Sink Type | Typical Cost (Installed) |
|---|---|
| Prefab cultured marble (included in most stock units) | $0-$150 extra |
| Ceramic or porcelain undermount sink + tile top | $200-$600 |
| Quartz countertop, custom cut | $400-$1,200 |
| Granite or marble countertop | $500-$1,500 |
| Vessel sink (above counter, glass or ceramic) | $100-$500 for the sink, plus faucet |
| Integrated concrete top | $600-$2,000 |

If you want a vessel sink, keep in mind you also need a taller-than-standard faucet and your plumber may need to adjust the drain height.

### 4. Faucet

Most stock vanities do not include a faucet. Budget $50 to $500 for the fixture itself, and factor in 30 to 60 minutes of plumber time to connect it. A basic single-handle faucet from Moen or Delta runs $80 to $200 and will give you years of trouble-free use. A designer brushed brass or matte black faucet from a brand like Grohe or Brizo can cost $300 to $700 or more.

### 5. Labor and What the Pro Actually Does

Vanity replacement involves more than just swapping out a cabinet. A licensed plumber or experienced handyman will:

- Shut off the water supply lines and disconnect the existing drain
- Remove the old vanity, top, and mirror (if included in scope)
- Check for water damage or mold behind the old unit
- Set and level the new vanity cabinet
- Connect the new supply lines (usually replaced at the same time, around $50 to $100 in parts)
- Install the drain assembly and P-trap
- Reconnect or install the faucet
- Caulk the backsplash and base

Labor typically runs **$150 to $600** for a straightforward swap. Complications that push labor costs higher include: moving a drain location, correcting old plumbing that does not meet code, fixing water damage discovered during the project, or tiling a new backsplash.

Expect to pay more in high-cost-of-living cities. A San Francisco or New York plumber may charge $150 to $200 per hour; a plumber in the Midwest or South might charge $75 to $100 per hour.

## Signs Your Vanity Needs Replacing (Not Just Repairing)

You do not always have to replace the whole thing. But these signs usually mean replacement is smarter than repair:

- **Soft or spongy cabinet bottom:** This signals water damage that has compromised the structural integrity of the cabinet box. Repair is rarely cost-effective.
- **Visible mold inside or behind the cabinet:** A small patch of surface mold can sometimes be cleaned. Black mold penetrating into the wood or drywall behind the vanity is a bigger problem that warrants a full removal and inspection.
- **Peeling laminate or delaminated veneer:** This is cosmetic but also allows moisture to get into the substrate, accelerating damage over time.
- **Cabinet doors that do not close properly:** If the box has racked or warped, realignment is rarely a permanent fix.
- **Cracks in the sink or countertop:** These allow water to seep in and can cause serious damage below over time.
- **It is just outdated:** Honey oak cabinets from 1997 with a matching brown toilet and gold faucets are not doing your home's value any favors. A fresh vanity is one of the highest-ROI bathroom updates you can make.

## DIY vs. Hiring a Pro: The Honest Breakdown

This is a project where confident DIYers can save real money, but it does involve plumbing, and a small mistake can cause a lot of water damage.

**DIY is reasonable if:**
- You are replacing a vanity with the same or smaller footprint (no moving drain lines)
- You are comfortable with basic plumbing: shutting off water, disconnecting P-traps, reconnecting supply lines
- The vanity fits through your bathroom door (measure twice before ordering)
- You have a helper for a large, heavy unit

**Hire a pro if:**
- The drain or supply line location is changing
- You discover water damage or mold during removal
- You are adding a new countertop that requires a separate measurement and fabrication process
- You want the faucet, drain, and everything perfectly aligned on the first try

Even if you do the cabinet installation yourself, it is worth paying a plumber for an hour of their time to make the final water connections if you are not confident in your plumbing skills. A slow leak behind a vanity can cause thousands of dollars in water damage that you will not notice until it is too late.

## How to Save Money on Your Vanity Replacement

**1. Buy the vanity yourself:** Plumbers and contractors often mark up materials. Buying your own vanity from Home Depot, Lowe's, IKEA, or online retailers like Wayfair lets you control that cost. Just confirm that the contractor is comfortable installing a customer-supplied unit (most are, though some charge a small fee for it).

**2. Keep the same footprint:** Moving a drain line even a few inches involves cutting into your subfloor and can add $300 to $800 or more to the job. Staying in the same spot keeps costs down dramatically.

**3. Skip the vessel sink if it's just for looks:** Vessel sinks are stylish, but they are trickier to install, require custom faucets, and the basin height can be awkward. A quality undermount or drop-in sink on a standard vanity is a more practical choice for most bathrooms.

**4. Reuse your mirror and light fixture:** If they are still in good shape, there is no reason to replace them during a vanity swap. You can always update them later as a separate, smaller project.

**5. Get quotes from handymen, not just plumbers:** For a straightforward vanity swap with no drain relocation, a licensed handyman can often do the job for less than a licensed plumber. Just verify they are experienced with plumbing connections in your state.

**6. Shop sales and overstock:** Big box stores run sales on bathroom fixtures regularly, and overstock sites often have name-brand vanities at steep discounts. Be patient and buy before you are in crisis mode.

## What to Ask a Contractor Before Hiring

Before you hand over any money, make sure you get clear answers to these questions:

- Are you licensed and insured for plumbing work in this state?
- Does your quote include hauling away the old vanity?
- What happens to the price if you find water damage behind the existing vanity?
- Do you pull permits? (Some jurisdictions require them for vanity replacements that involve plumbing modifications.)
- Can I supply my own vanity, or do you require using yours?
- What is your warranty on your labor?

Licensing requirements vary by state. The [National Association of State Contractors Licensing Agencies (NASCLA)](https://www.nascla.org) provides a directory of state licensing boards where you can verify credentials. Many states publish license lookup tools online.

## The ROI: Does a New Vanity Add Home Value?

Bathroom remodels consistently rank among the highest-ROI home improvements. According to the [National Association of Realtors](https://www.nar.realtor/), a midrange bathroom renovation recoups approximately 66 cents per dollar spent at resale, and a cosmetic-only bathroom update (like a vanity swap) tends to perform even better in terms of ROI per dollar because the labor is lower.

A new vanity with a stone top and updated hardware can make an entire bathroom feel fresh and modern to a buyer, even if the tile and tub are unchanged. In a competitive real estate market, that impression matters.

## Permits: When Do You Need One?

Most straight vanity replacements (same location, no drain or supply line changes) do not require a permit. However, if you are moving the drain, adding a new rough-in location, or upgrading the plumbing as part of a larger bathroom remodel, your local building department may require a permit and inspection.

Always ask your contractor if a permit is required. A licensed contractor should know local code. The [International Code Council (ICC)](https://www.iccsafe.org) sets the model plumbing codes that most states adopt, with state-specific amendments. You can check your local municipality's building department website for their specific requirements.

Skipping a required permit is not worth the risk. It can complicate a future home sale and, in the event of water damage, give your insurance company grounds to deny a claim.
