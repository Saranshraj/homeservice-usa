Painting the outside of your home costs **$1,800 to $13,000** for most American homeowners in 2026, with the national average landing around **$3,200 for a 1,500 square foot single-story home**. Multi-story or larger homes run $5,000 to $13,000 or more. If you are getting quotes right now and feeling sticker shock, you are not alone. Labor accounts for 70 to 85 percent of that total, and material and labor costs have climbed steadily since 2021. This guide breaks down exactly what you are paying for, where you can trim costs without ruining your home's protection, and how to spot contractors who will take your money and leave you with a peeling mess in two years.

## Why Your Neighbor Paid Half What You Were Quoted

Before you panic about a high quote, understand that exterior painting prices vary enormously based on factors that have nothing to do with profit margin. Two identical houses on the same street can produce quotes $3,000 apart based on their condition, the paint specified, and the prep work required. Here are the big variables:

**Home Size and Stories**
The square footage of paintable surface area drives the base cost. A 1,200 sq ft ranch-style home has far less surface than a 2,400 sq ft two-story colonial, even though the footprints look similar from the street. Getting up a ladder for second and third-story work adds scaffolding costs and time, which is why multi-story homes cost significantly more per square foot.

**Surface Condition**
If your home has peeling, chalking, or flaking paint, the prep work doubles or triples labor hours. Wood rot must be repaired before painting. Old caulking must be stripped and replaced. Fascia boards, soffits, and trim in poor condition add cost. Skipping prep is where cheap painters save money, and it is also why their paint jobs fail in three years instead of ten.

**Lead Paint**
Homes built before 1978 may contain lead-based paint. Federal EPA regulations under the Renovation, Repair and Painting (RRP) Rule require contractors to use lead-safe work practices, which adds $100 to $400 to the project cost. This is not optional; contractors who skip it are violating federal law and putting your family at risk. You can learn more at [EPA's lead paint page](https://www.epa.gov/lead/renovation-repair-and-painting-program).

**Paint Quality**
Cheap exterior paints cost $25 to $40 per gallon. Premium paints from brands like Sherwin-Williams Emerald or Benjamin Moore Aura run $70 to $100 per gallon. A good exterior paint job on a typical home uses 15 to 20 gallons. That $800 difference in materials is real, but premium paint genuinely lasts longer and looks better, especially in harsh climates with UV exposure, humidity, or freeze-thaw cycles.

**Number of Colors and Trim Detail**
A straightforward one-color paint job is the cheapest option. Add a contrasting trim color, paint shutters, and detail around decorative woodwork and the price climbs. Victorian-style homes with ornate woodwork can cost 30 to 50 percent more to paint than a simple ranch because of the time required for cutting in around intricate details.

|---|---|---|
| 1,000 sq ft | 1-story | $1,800 to $2,500 |
| 1,500 sq ft | 1-story | $2,800 to $3,800 |
| 2,000 sq ft | 2-story | $4,200 to $6,000 |

These ranges assume moderate prep work, two coats of mid-grade exterior paint, and standard trim painting. Homes in poor condition or with extensive woodwork sit at the high end or beyond.

## State-by-State Cost Differences

Labor costs for painters vary significantly by region. States with higher costs of living, stronger union presence, or year-round demand for exterior painting (like Florida) trend toward the higher end. States with shorter painting seasons (Minnesota, Wisconsin) can be more competitive because painters compete harder during the window they can work outdoors.

| Region | States | Cost Premium/Discount vs. National Average |
|---|---|---|
| Northeast | NY, CT, MA, NJ | 25 to 45% above average |
| Mid-Atlantic | PA, MD, VA, DC | 10 to 25% above average |
| Southeast | FL, GA, SC, NC | At or near average |
| Midwest | OH, IN, MI, WI, MN | 5 to 15% below average |
| South Central | TX, OK, AR, LA | 10 to 20% below average |
| Mountain West | CO, UT, AZ, NM | Near average to 10% above |
| Pacific Northwest | WA, OR | 15 to 30% above average |
| California | CA | 30 to 60% above average |

**Quick example:** That $3,200 national average for a 1,500 sq ft home looks more like $2,700 in Ohio and $4,800 in the San Francisco Bay Area.

## Paint Types: Which One Should You Choose?

| Paint Type | Cost Per Gallon | Lifespan | Best For |
|---|---|---|---|
| Acrylic Latex (standard) | $25 to $45 | 5 to 7 years | Budget jobs, mild climates |
| Acrylic Latex (premium) | $55 to $75 | 8 to 12 years | Most homes, good value |
| 100% Acrylic (top-tier) | $70 to $100 | 10 to 15 years | Harsh climates, long-term value |
| Elastomeric | $60 to $90 | 10 to 15 years | Stucco, masonry, cracked surfaces |
| Oil-based | $35 to $60 | 8 to 12 years | Trim and metal surfaces |

For most American homeowners, a quality acrylic latex paint in the $55 to $75 per gallon range offers the best combination of durability and price. The [EPA recommends low-VOC or zero-VOC exterior paints](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/volatile-organic-compounds-impact-indoor-air-quality) to reduce chemical exposure for your family and neighbors. Many premium exterior paints now meet this standard.

## DIY Vs. Hiring a Pro: The Honest Breakdown

Look, painting your own house sounds like a great way to save $2,000 or more. And it genuinely is, if you go in with open eyes about what the project involves.

**What DIY saves you:** Labor, which is 70 to 85% of the total. On a $4,000 project, you might spend $800 to $1,200 on materials and pocket the rest in sweat equity.

**What DIY costs you:** Time (a 1,500 sq ft home takes a first-timer 3 to 5 full weekends), physical effort (the prep work is grueling), rental equipment (pressure washer: $50 to $80/day, scaffolding: $150 to $300/weekend), and the real risk of a paint job that fails prematurely because prep was rushed.

**The biggest DIY trap:** Homeowners often do excellent work on the sections they can easily reach and rush the high, inconvenient spots. A paint job is only as good as its worst sections.

**When to definitely hire a pro:**
- Your home has two or more stories
- You have a lead paint situation (pre-1978 home)
- There is significant wood rot, damage, or peeling
- Your home has complex architectural details
- You cannot commit two to three full weekends to the project

**When DIY makes sense:**
- Single-story home in good condition
- You are physically able to handle ladders and extended overhead work
- You can rent proper equipment
- You have a helper for at least the first day

## How to Get an Accurate Quote (and Avoid Getting Burned)

Getting [three quotes](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/) is the minimum standard advice, and it is good advice. But knowing what makes a quote legitimate versus a lowball trap is just as important.

**A legitimate exterior painting quote should include:**
- Total square footage of surface area being painted
- Number of coats specified (always two for walls, at least one for primer if needed)
- Specific paint brand and product name
- Prep work spelled out in detail: pressure washing, scraping, caulking, priming
- Warranty on labor (reputable contractors offer 2 to 5 years)
- Timeline and payment schedule
- Contractor license number and proof of insurance

**Red flags that should send you running:**
- A quote that is 40% or more below the other two. This contractor is either planning to skip prep, use cheap paint, or disappear after the deposit.
- Demanding more than 30% upfront. Standard practice is 10 to 30% deposit, with the remainder on completion.
- No written contract. Never, ever proceed without a signed, detailed contract.
- Cannot provide a license number or proof of liability insurance and workers' comp. If a worker is injured on your property and the contractor is uninsured, you may be liable.
- Pressuring you to decide the same day. Legitimate contractors give you time to compare.

To verify a contractor's license, look up your state's contractor licensing board. The [National Association of Home Builders](https://www.nahb.org) has resources for finding vetted professionals.

## The Best Time of Year to Paint Exterior

If you have flexibility, paint your home's exterior when temperatures are consistently between 50 and 90 degrees Fahrenheit with low humidity. That means:

- **Best nationwide:** Late spring (May to June) and early fall (September to October)
- **Southern states (TX, FL, AZ):** Fall and winter, avoiding summer heat and humidity
- **Northern states (MN, WI, ME):** Narrow window of May through September
- **Pacific Northwest:** Summer is ideal; avoid the rainy season

Do not paint when rain is in the forecast within 24 hours of application, when temperatures are below 50°F, or during periods of high humidity (above 85%). If a contractor pushes to paint in cold or rainy conditions to meet a deadline, that is a red flag.

## How to Save Money Without Compromising Quality

Here are legitimate ways to reduce your exterior painting costs without sacrificing results:

**Do your own prep work.** If you are physically capable, pressure wash the house yourself, scrape loose paint, and fill small cracks with exterior caulk. Many contractors will reduce their quote if the prep is already done.

**Get quotes in fall or winter.** Painters are slower in the off-season in northern states and may offer better pricing to fill their schedule.

**Stick to two colors.** Every additional color adds cutting-in time and labor cost.

**Repaint on a longer cycle with better paint.** Spending $800 more on premium paint to get 12 to 15 years instead of 7 years of life is almost always the better financial choice.

**Bundle with carpentry repairs.** If you need minor wood rot repairs or caulking replacement, having your painter handle it while already mobilized can be cheaper than a separate contractor visit.

**Skip the garage and fence for now.** If budget is tight, focus the paint job on the main house and schedule outbuildings separately.

## What Happens If You Delay?

Bare or peeling exterior paint is not just an eyesore. It is a slow leak in your home's envelope. Once moisture gets under failing paint and into bare wood, you are looking at wood rot repair costs of $500 to $2,500 or more on top of the paint job — or [siding replacement](/blog/siding-replacement-cost/) if the underlying material has failed. In extreme cases, structural damage from water infiltration can run into the tens of thousands — and most [homeowners insurance policies](/blog/homeowners-insurance-claims/) won't cover damage caused by deferred maintenance. Exterior painting is genuinely maintenance, not cosmetics. The [CPSC](https://www.cpsc.gov) and building code authorities recognize proper exterior maintenance as a fundamental homeowner responsibility for this reason.

Most painted homes need a fresh coat every 7 to 10 years with quality paint on prepared surfaces, or every 5 to 7 years with standard paint. If you are seeing cracking, peeling, chalking, or fading, your home is telling you the time is now.
