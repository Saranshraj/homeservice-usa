Hiring a plumber costs **$45 to $200 per hour** in 2026, with most US homeowners paying a total of **$175 to $450** for a standard service call. Emergency plumbing (nights, weekends, holidays) runs **$100 to $300 per hour**, and many plumbers charge a flat trip fee of **$50 to $150** just to show up before any work starts. The final bill depends heavily on where you live, the complexity of the job, and whether you are calling at 2 a.m. on a Sunday. The good news: knowing the real numbers before you call puts you in a much stronger position when the bill arrives.

If you are dealing with a burst pipe, sewage backup, or a leak that is actively damaging your home right now, call a plumber immediately and read the details later. For everything else, this guide will help you understand what you should expect to pay and how to avoid common overcharges.

## Plumber Cost by Job Type

Most plumbing calls fall into a handful of common categories. Here are realistic price ranges for each, based on national averages for 2026.

| Job | Typical Cost Range | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Drain cleaning (single drain) | $100 to $350 | More for main sewer line |
| Faucet repair or replacement | $150 to $400 | Includes labor; fixture costs extra |
| Toilet repair | $100 to $250 | Flapper, fill valve, supply line |
| Toilet replacement | $250 to $600 | Labor only; toilet costs extra ($100 to $600) |
| Water heater installation | $300 to $800 | Labor only; see our [water heater cost guide](/blog/water-heater-replacement-cost/) |
| Pipe leak repair | $150 to $550 | Accessible pipes cost less; wall access costs more |
| Garbage disposal installation | $150 to $350 | Labor; disposal costs extra ($75 to $350) |
| Shower or tub installation | $500 to $1,500 | More if tiling or structural work needed |
| Main sewer line cleaning | $250 to $800 | Camera inspection adds $100 to $300 |
| Main sewer line replacement | $3,000 to $15,000 | Varies dramatically by length and depth |
| Water pressure issue | $200 to $500 | Regulator replacement or diagnosis |
| Gas line repair | $300 to $1,000 | Requires licensed plumber in every state |
| Whole-home repiping | $4,000 to $15,000 | Major project; get three quotes |

These are ranges, not ceilings. A drain cleaning in rural Mississippi will cost less than the same job in Manhattan. A pipe buried in concrete costs more to access than one in an open basement. Use these numbers to sanity-check quotes, not as fixed targets.

## Plumber Hourly Rates by Region

Labor is the biggest variable in any plumbing bill. Here is what to expect in different parts of the country.

| Region | Typical Hourly Rate |
|---|---|
| Northeast (NY, MA, CT, NJ) | $100 to $200 per hour |
| West Coast (CA, WA, OR) | $90 to $180 per hour |
| Mid-Atlantic (PA, MD, VA, DC area) | $80 to $160 per hour |
| Midwest (IL, OH, MI, MN) | $65 to $130 per hour |
| Southeast (GA, FL, NC, TN) | $55 to $120 per hour |
| South Central (TX, OK, LA) | $50 to $110 per hour |
| Mountain West (CO, UT, AZ) | $65 to $140 per hour |
| Rural areas (any region) | $45 to $100 per hour |

Cities with high costs of living and strong union plumber representation (New York, San Francisco, Boston, Seattle) sit at the top of these ranges. If your job is straightforward and in a metro area, expect to be billed at the higher end. Suburban and rural markets are typically more competitive.

## How Plumbers Price Their Work

Understanding the pricing structure helps you ask the right questions before you agree to anything.

### Hourly Rate Plus Materials

This is the most common model. The plumber charges by the hour for labor, plus the cost of any parts and materials. Some plumbers mark up parts by 20 to 50 percent over what you would pay at a hardware store. This is standard practice and not necessarily a scam, but it is worth asking whether you can supply your own parts for major purchases like a faucet or toilet.

### Flat Rate (Book Rate) Pricing

Some plumbing companies charge a fixed price per job, regardless of how long it takes. A drain cleaning might be $275 flat. A toilet replacement might be $450 flat. This can be good if the job takes longer than expected, but it can also mean you pay full price for a 20-minute fix. Ask upfront which model a company uses.

### Trip or Dispatch Fee

Most plumbers charge a trip fee of **$50 to $150** that covers the cost of driving to your home and initial assessment. This fee is often applied toward the total bill if you hire them, but not always. Clarify this before they arrive.

### Emergency and After-Hours Rates

Expect to pay a significant premium for plumbing calls outside of normal business hours (typically evenings after 5 p.m., weekends, and holidays). After-hours rates commonly run **1.5 to 2 times** the standard hourly rate. A plumber who charges $90 per hour during the day might charge $150 to $180 at midnight on a Saturday.

## When to Call a Plumber vs. DIY

Not every plumbing problem requires a professional. Here is an honest breakdown.

### You can likely DIY:
- Replacing a flapper or fill valve in a running toilet (parts cost $5 to $15, takes about 20 minutes)
- Unclogging a slow drain with a plunger or hand snake
- Replacing a showerhead
- Installing a new faucet aerator
- Tightening supply line connections under a sink
- Replacing a P-trap under a sink (the curved pipe under the drain)

### Call a plumber:
- Any gas line work. This is non-negotiable. Gas leaks kill people, and every US state requires a licensed professional for gas work.
- A burst pipe or one that is actively flooding your home.
- Sewer line problems (sewage smell, multiple drains backing up at once, gurgling sounds from multiple fixtures — see our [sewer line repair cost guide](/blog/sewer-line-repair-cost/) to understand what you may be facing).
- Water heater replacement if you are switching fuel types or the installation requires new connections.
- Any work that requires a permit. Plumbing permits are required for new installations and major changes in virtually every US municipality.
- Lead pipe replacement. Homes built before 1986 may have lead service lines. The [EPA](https://www.epa.gov/ground-water-and-drinking-water/basic-information-about-lead-drinking-water) has guidance on identifying and replacing lead pipes, and this work should always be done by a licensed pro.

The [Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov/) estimates that DIY plumbing mistakes cause significant property damage every year. The line is simple: if you are not confident, or if the job involves gas, sewage, or permit-required work, call a licensed plumber.

## How to Avoid Overpaying for Plumbing Work

Plumbing is one of the trades most prone to upselling and price inflation, especially during an emergency. Here is how to protect yourself.

### Get a written quote before work starts

Never let a plumber start work before you have a written estimate that breaks down labor and materials separately. Verbal estimates are not enforceable. A two-minute phone quote is not a binding number.

### Verify their license

Every state requires plumbers to be licensed. In most states, there are different license levels: journeyman (works under supervision) and master plumber (licensed to pull permits and run a business). You can check your state's licensing board directly. In California, verify through the Contractors State License Board. In Texas, use the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners. For most states, a search for "[your state] plumber license lookup" gets you there in under a minute.

The [National Association of Home Builders](https://www.nahb.org/) recommends verifying license, insurance, and at least three references before hiring any contractor, including plumbers. Our guide to [finding a reliable home contractor](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/) covers the full vetting process with a step-by-step checklist that applies equally well to plumbers.

### Get three quotes for big jobs

For anything over $500, get at least three quotes. You may be surprised by the range. A whole-home repiping quote can legitimately vary by $5,000 or more between companies.

### Ask if the trip fee applies toward the job

Some plumbers deduct the trip fee from the total if you hire them. Others do not. Knowing upfront prevents the unpleasant surprise of a bill that is $100 higher than the quote you were given.

### Check for rebates on efficiency upgrades

If you are replacing a water heater or installing a high-efficiency fixture, check the [ENERGY STAR Rebate Finder](https://www.energystar.gov/rebate-finder) for available utility rebates. Many utilities offer $50 to $500 off qualifying upgrades.

## Drain Cleaning Cost: A Closer Look

Drain cleaning is one of the most common plumbing calls, and the price range is wide enough to be confusing — we cover it in full detail in our [drain cleaning cost guide](/blog/drain-cleaning-cost/), but here is a quick summary.

**Plunger or hand auger (DIY):** $0 to $30 in parts. The right move for a single slow or clogged drain.

**Professional single drain cleaning:** $100 to $350. A plumber uses a motorized drain snake (also called an auger) to clear the blockage. Kitchen drains clogged with grease take longer and cost more than a simple hair clog in a bathroom.

**Main sewer line cleaning (hydro-jetting):** $250 to $800. For main line clogs or tree root intrusion, plumbers use a high-pressure water jet to clear the line. This is a major difference from a simple drain snake and justifies the higher price.

**Sewer camera inspection:** $100 to $300 added to the cleaning cost. For persistent clogs or older homes, a camera inspection lets the plumber see exactly what is causing the problem. This is often worth it before spending money on repeated cleanings.

A note on chemical drain cleaners: Products like Drano or Liquid-Plumr can dissolve hair and soap scum in minor clogs, but they cause real damage to older pipes and should never be used in pipes with known issues or if you have cast iron or galvanized steel plumbing. For minor clogs, try a plunger or drain snake first.

## Emergency Plumber Cost

Emergency plumbing calls are unavoidably expensive. Here is what to expect and how to minimize the damage.

A burst pipe, sewage backup, or gas leak requires an immediate call regardless of cost. The cost of water damage to floors, walls, and structural components can reach **$3,000 to $30,000**, making a $400 emergency plumber call look cheap in comparison.

For less urgent after-hours situations, consider whether you can safely manage the issue until regular business hours:
- A slow drip from a faucet that is not getting worse can wait until morning.
- A toilet that runs constantly is annoying but not an emergency; shut the water supply valve behind the toilet and call in the morning.
- A single clogged drain with no sewage backup can usually wait.

If you do need an emergency plumber, **ask the dispatching company for the after-hours rate before they send someone**. Legitimate companies will tell you upfront. If they refuse to give you a rate before arrival, that is a red flag.

## Understanding Your Plumbing: What Homeowners Should Know

Knowing your home's plumbing basics saves you money and helps you communicate clearly with a plumber.

**Know where your main shutoff valve is.** If a pipe bursts, the first thing you do is turn off the main water supply. Most homes have the shutoff near where the water line enters the house: typically the basement, garage, or utility room. The [Federal Emergency Management Agency](https://www.fema.gov/) recommends every household member know where this valve is and how to operate it before an emergency occurs.

**Know your pipe material.** Homes built before 1970 often have galvanized steel or cast iron pipes that corrode over time. Homes built between 1978 and 1995 may have polybutylene pipe, which is notorious for failing and was the subject of a class action settlement. Homes built after 1995 typically have copper or PVC/CPVC. Your pipe material affects repair costs, compatibility of parts, and the likelihood of future problems.

**Test your water pressure.** Normal household water pressure runs 40 to 80 PSI (if you're consistently reading below 40, our guide to [diagnosing low water pressure](/blog/low-water-pressure-house/) walks through the most common causes and fixes). Above 80 PSI can stress pipes and fixtures; a pressure reducing valve (PRV) costs $200 to $500 installed and protects your entire system. You can buy a water pressure gauge for $15 at any hardware store.

**Look for signs before a small problem becomes big.** Water stains on ceilings or walls, musty smells in cabinets under sinks, and higher-than-normal water bills are early warning signs of hidden leaks. Catching them early keeps a $200 repair from becoming a $5,000 remediation.
