Calling an exterminator to deal with cockroaches typically costs between **$100 and $600 for a single treatment**, with most homeowners paying around **$250 to $400** for a moderate infestation in an average-sized home. If the problem is severe or spread across multiple rooms, expect to pay **$600 to $1,200 or more** for a comprehensive multi-visit treatment plan. The good news: professional cockroach treatment is highly effective, and most homes see significant results within just a few days of the first visit.

If you have cockroaches, you are not alone. The [U.S. Environmental Protection Agency estimates](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/cockroaches) that cockroaches are one of the most common household pests in the country, especially in warm, humid climates and dense urban areas. Getting ahead of an infestation quickly saves both money and stress, so let's break down exactly what you can expect to pay.

## What Does a Cockroach Exterminator Cost?

The price you pay depends heavily on the type of cockroach, the size of your home, the severity of the infestation, and the treatment method used. Here is a snapshot of typical costs:

| Situation | Typical Cost |
|---|---|
| Small apartment, minor infestation | $100 to $200 |
| Average home, moderate infestation | $250 to $400 |
| Large home or severe infestation | $500 to $1,200 |
| Ongoing monthly pest control plan | $40 to $80 per month |
| German cockroach (hardest to eliminate) | $300 to $800+ |
| American or Oriental cockroach | $150 to $400 |
| Fumigation (severe, whole-home) | $1,500 to $3,000+ |

Most exterminators charge either a **flat fee per visit** or a **monthly or quarterly service plan**. A one-time treatment handles a contained problem, while a service plan makes more sense if cockroaches keep coming back or if you live in a region where they are extremely common.

## Types of Cockroaches and Why It Matters

Not all cockroaches are equal, and the species in your home dramatically affects both the difficulty of treatment and the final price you pay.

**German cockroaches** are the small, tan-colored roaches you often see in kitchens and bathrooms. They reproduce extremely fast, one female can produce up to 400 offspring in her lifetime, and they are notoriously resistant to many pesticides. Eliminating them typically requires multiple visits and costs $300 to $800 or more. According to the [EPA's integrated pest management guidelines](https://www.epa.gov/managing-pests-schools/introduction-integrated-pest-management), German roaches respond best to a combination of gel baits, insect growth regulators, and thorough sanitation.

**American cockroaches** are the large, reddish-brown ones (sometimes called palmetto bugs in the South). They typically live outdoors and wander inside through gaps around pipes, doors, and foundations. They are easier to control, and a single treatment often costs $150 to $400.

**Oriental cockroaches** are dark brown or black, moisture-loving roaches that prefer basements and drains. Treatment is similar to American cockroaches in cost and difficulty.

**Brown-banded cockroaches** are smaller and spread throughout the home rather than concentrating in the kitchen. They can be trickier to find and treat, pushing costs toward $300 to $600.

## What Drives the Cost Up or Down?

### Size of the Infested Area

Exterminators often charge by square footage or by the number of rooms treated. A 1,000-square-foot apartment costs less than a 3,000-square-foot house. If the infestation is confined to one room, you may pay less. If it has spread throughout the whole structure, expect higher costs.

### Severity of the Infestation

A light infestation caught early might require just one visit with gel baiting. A severe infestation, especially German cockroaches, could require three or more visits spaced two weeks apart, plus follow-up inspections. Each additional visit adds $75 to $150 to your bill.

### Treatment Method

| Method | Cost | Best For |
|---|---|---|
| Gel bait | $100 to $300 | German cockroaches, kitchens |
| Spray/insecticide | $150 to $400 | General treatment, perimeter |
| Insect growth regulator | $100 to $250 | Interrupting reproduction cycle |
| Boric acid dusting | $80 to $200 | Wall voids, basements |
| Heat treatment | $500 to $1,500 | Severe infestations, chemical-free |
| Fumigation | $1,500 to $3,000+ | Extreme, whole-structure cases |

Modern pest control professionals often use a combination of methods, particularly gel baits plus an insect growth regulator, which interrupts the cockroach life cycle so eggs do not hatch. This combination approach is more expensive upfront but tends to produce lasting results.

### Your Location

Pest control costs vary considerably by state. Here is a general breakdown by region:

| Region | Average Treatment Cost |
|---|---|
| Northeast (NY, NJ, MA, CT) | $300 to $600 |
| Southeast (FL, GA, SC, LA) | $200 to $500 |
| Midwest (OH, IL, MI, MN) | $150 to $350 |
| Southwest (TX, AZ, NM) | $175 to $400 |

Florida and other warm, humid states tend to have higher infestation rates, which can push labor costs up due to demand. Urban areas like New York City, Chicago, and Los Angeles also carry a premium because of higher operating costs for exterminators.

### One-Time vs. Service Contract

A one-time treatment is cheaper upfront but risky if the infestation returns. Many pest control companies offer monthly or quarterly service plans ranging from $40 to $80 per month. These plans make financial sense if:

- You live in a warm climate where cockroaches are prevalent year-round
- You have had repeat infestations
- You live in a multi-family building where neighbors' units can re-introduce pests

## How Exterminators Price Their Services

Most professional pest control companies use one of three pricing models:

**Per-visit flat fee.** A single treatment for a specific pest in a specific area, usually $100 to $500. Good for one-time problems.

**Per-square-foot pricing.** Some companies charge $0.10 to $0.25 per square foot. A 2,000-square-foot home could cost $200 to $500 per visit.

**Annual contract with multiple visits.** Many companies bundle quarterly or bi-monthly visits for $400 to $900 per year, which breaks down to roughly $100 to $225 per visit at a discounted rate.

Always ask for a written estimate that breaks down the cost per visit, the number of visits included, and what happens if the infestation persists beyond the included visits. Reputable companies will often re-treat at no extra charge if the problem is not resolved within the agreed timeframe.

## DIY vs. Hiring a Professional

Can you handle cockroaches yourself? Sometimes, but it depends on the species and severity.

**DIY works best for:**
- American or Oriental cockroaches with limited entry points
- Early-stage infestations where you have seen only a few roaches
- Preventive treatments as a supplement to professional work

**Common DIY products and their costs:**
- Gel baits (Advion, Combat Max): $10 to $25
- Boric acid powder: $8 to $15
- Diatomaceous earth: $10 to $20
- Over-the-counter sprays: $8 to $20
- Insect growth regulator concentrates: $20 to $50

The total cost of a thorough DIY treatment runs $50 to $100. The challenge is that most consumer-grade products are less concentrated than what professionals use, and German cockroaches in particular are skilled at avoiding baits placed incorrectly. The [Consumer Product Safety Commission](https://www.cpsc.gov) reminds homeowners to always read pesticide labels carefully and keep all products away from children and pets.

**Professional treatment is worth the cost if:**
- You have German cockroaches (they are very difficult to eliminate without professional-grade products)
- The infestation has spread beyond one room
- You have tried DIY approaches and the problem persists
- You have young children, elderly family members, or pets who may be more sensitive to pesticide exposure

## What to Expect During a Professional Treatment

Here is a typical timeline when you hire an exterminator:

**Day 1: Inspection and initial treatment.** The technician inspects your home, identifies the cockroach species, and determines the extent of the infestation. They apply gel baits, dust wall voids with boric acid or silica gel, and may apply a residual spray around the perimeter and under appliances. This visit takes 1 to 2 hours.

**Days 3 to 7:** You will likely see more cockroaches than usual as they come out to feed on the bait. This is normal. Do not clean up the gel bait or spray areas during this period.

**Week 2 to 4:** Cockroach activity should decrease significantly. For German cockroaches, a follow-up visit is typically needed 2 to 4 weeks after the first treatment.

**Follow-up visit:** The technician checks bait stations, re-applies where needed, and confirms the infestation has been resolved. This is usually included in the original price.

## Tips for Getting the Best Price

**Get at least 3 quotes.** Prices vary widely between companies, so shopping around can save you $100 to $200. Local independent pest control companies are often 20 to 30 percent cheaper than national chains.

**Ask about guarantees.** Look for companies that offer a free re-treatment guarantee if the problem is not resolved within 30 days. This protects you from paying twice for the same infestation.

**Check licensing.** Every state requires pest control operators to be licensed. You can verify a company's license through your state's department of agriculture or department of environmental protection. Working with a licensed professional ensures they are using approved products correctly.

**Bundle with a service plan.** If you live in a cockroach-prone area, an annual service plan is often cheaper than paying for two or three one-time treatments over the course of a year.

**Ask about Integrated Pest Management (IPM).** The EPA recommends IPM approaches that combine sanitation, exclusion, and targeted pesticide use. IPM-certified companies typically achieve better results with lower pesticide loads, which is better for your family and the environment.

## Preventing Future Infestations

The most cost-effective cockroach strategy is preventing them from coming back after treatment. Here are the steps that make the biggest difference:

- Seal gaps around pipes, electrical conduits, and baseboards with caulk or steel wool
- Fix leaky faucets and plumbing immediately, cockroaches need water to survive
- Store food in sealed containers and never leave dirty dishes overnight
- Empty trash daily and use bins with tight-fitting lids
- Keep cardboard boxes to a minimum, cockroaches love to nest in cardboard
- Pull appliances away from the wall periodically to clean behind them

Your exterminator can also apply a preventive perimeter treatment at each service visit to keep new cockroaches from entering. For apartments and condos, work with your building manager to address shared spaces like utility chases, laundry rooms, and garbage rooms, which can serve as ongoing entry points no matter how clean your individual unit is.

## When to Be Concerned About Health

Cockroaches are more than just unpleasant. The [EPA notes](https://www.epa.gov/indoor-air-quality-iaq/cockroaches) that cockroach allergens are a known trigger for asthma and allergies, particularly in children. Research published by the [National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences](https://www.niehs.nih.gov) found that inner-city children with high cockroach allergen exposure had significantly higher rates of asthma hospitalizations.

If anyone in your home has asthma or allergies, treating a cockroach infestation quickly is not just a matter of comfort but of health. Discuss this with your exterminator so they can prioritize low-allergen, gel-based treatment methods over sprays that can temporarily increase airborne allergen levels.
