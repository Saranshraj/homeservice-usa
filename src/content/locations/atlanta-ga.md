Atlanta homeowners deal with a climate that demands strong air conditioning for much of the year, combined with a heavily wooded metro area that creates real storm and tree-damage exposure most homeowners underestimate until it affects them directly.

## Why Atlanta Is Different

Atlanta's humid subtropical climate means summers are hot and consistently humid, with heat index values that often exceed the actual temperature. This drives sustained AC use from roughly May through September and, combined with Atlanta's mild but real winters, means HVAC systems here need to perform well across a genuinely wide seasonal range. See our [Atlanta HVAC guide](/locations/atlanta-ga/hvac/).

Atlanta sits within an active severe thunderstorm corridor, and the metro area sees regular hail events, high wind, and tornado risk during spring storm season. Atlanta's famously dense tree canopy, one of the largest urban forests of any major US city, adds a layer of storm risk that is unique to this market: falling limbs and trees are a leading cause of roof damage claims in the metro area, in addition to hail. See our [Atlanta roofing guide](/locations/atlanta-ga/roofing/).

Atlanta's older, established neighborhoods (particularly inside the Perimeter, in areas like Buckhead, Decatur, and East Atlanta) have significant tree cover and homes ranging from early 20th century construction through the postwar boom, meaning older roofing and HVAC infrastructure is common. Newer suburban growth further out skews toward homes built from the 1990s onward.

## Georgia Contractor Licensing

Georgia requires state licensing for HVAC contractors, plumbers, and electricians through the [Georgia State Licensing Board for Residential and General Contractors](https://sos.ga.gov/georgia-state-licensing-board-residential-and-general-contractors) and related trade boards under the Secretary of State's office. Roofing-specific licensing is not separately required in Georgia beyond general contractor rules for larger jobs, which makes verifying insurance and reputation especially important after storm events, when unlicensed storm-chasing crews are a known issue. See our [contractor vetting guide](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/).
