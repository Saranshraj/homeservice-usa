Miami homeowners face a home-service environment shaped almost entirely by two things: hurricane risk and relentless humidity. Both drive costs, material choices, and timing decisions in ways that do not apply to most of the country, and both make Miami one of the most demanding markets in the US for roofing and HVAC work specifically.

## Why Miami Is Different

Florida has the strictest residential building codes in the country for wind resistance, a direct response to Hurricane Andrew in 1992. Since then, the Florida Building Code has required increasingly rigorous roofing and window standards, and Miami-Dade County enforces some of the toughest versions of that code anywhere in the US. This means roofing work in Miami is more expensive and more heavily regulated than almost anywhere else, but it also means a properly permitted roof is meaningfully safer. See our [Miami roofing guide](/locations/miami-fl/roofing/) for what this means for cost.

Year-round humidity, combined with near-constant AC use, means HVAC systems in Miami work harder than almost anywhere in the continental US outside of Phoenix. Humidity also creates a persistent mold risk that ties directly into HVAC maintenance, poorly maintained systems in Miami's climate can develop mold growth inside ductwork far faster than in drier climates. See our [Miami HVAC guide](/locations/miami-fl/hvac/).

Hurricane season runs June through November, and Miami is one of the highest hurricane-exposure metros in the US. This affects everything from roofing material choices (impact-resistant shingles and metal roofing are far more common here than nationally) to insurance costs (Florida homeowners insurance premiums are among the highest in the country, driven largely by roof and storm risk).

Miami's housing stock includes a large share of homes built from the 1950s through the 1980s, many of which have been re-roofed multiple times since original construction due to hurricane damage and code-driven upgrade requirements. Newer construction, particularly post-2002, must meet the current Florida Building Code's wind-resistance standards.

## Florida Contractor Licensing

Florida requires state-level licensing for most home service trades through the [Florida Department of Business and Professional Regulation (DBPR)](https://www.myfloridalicense.com). Roofing contractors need either a Certified Roofing Contractor or Registered Roofing Contractor license. HVAC (referred to as "air conditioning contractor" in Florida) and plumbing also require DBPR licensing. Given the high stakes of hurricane-related roofing work, verifying license status is especially important in Miami, our [contractor vetting guide](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/) explains what to check.
