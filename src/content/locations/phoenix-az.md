Phoenix homeowners live with one of the most extreme climates in the country, and it shapes home maintenance more than almost anywhere else in the US. Summer highs regularly exceed 110°F, with more than 100 days a year above 100°F. Air conditioning failure in Phoenix is not an inconvenience, it is a genuine safety emergency, which is why HVAC reliability matters more here than in nearly any other US market.

## Why Phoenix Is Different

Phoenix has the highest per-household air conditioning demand of any major US metro. AC systems here run nearly continuously from May through September, which means Phoenix homes typically replace HVAC systems on a shorter timeline than the national average, often 10 to 12 years instead of 15 to 20. See our [Phoenix HVAC guide](/locations/phoenix-az/hvac/) for what this means for repair versus replace decisions specific to this climate.

Extreme heat and intense UV exposure also take a heavy toll on roofing materials. Asphalt shingles in Phoenix age faster than the national average due to sustained high temperatures and UV degradation, and many Phoenix homeowners are shifting toward tile and reflective "cool roof" materials designed for desert conditions. See our [Phoenix roofing guide](/locations/phoenix-az/roofing/).

Phoenix also experiences a monsoon season from roughly June through September, bringing sudden intense thunderstorms, high winds, and occasional dust storms (haboobs) that can cause real roof and exterior damage despite the desert climate. This is a common source of confusion for homeowners who assume "desert" means no storm risk.

Phoenix's housing stock is a mix of older mid-century homes in central neighborhoods and large tracts of newer construction from the 1990s through today, driven by decades of rapid suburban growth. Many homes in the metro area are now in the 20 to 30 year range where original HVAC and roofing systems are at or past end of life.

## Arizona Contractor Licensing

Arizona requires a state contractor license for most home service trades, issued and searchable through the [Arizona Registrar of Contractors (ROC)](https://roc.az.gov). This applies to HVAC, roofing, plumbing, and electrical contractors. Always verify a contractor's ROC license number before hiring, and check for any complaint history, the ROC's public database makes this straightforward. See our [contractor vetting guide](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/) for the full verification process.
