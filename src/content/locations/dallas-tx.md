Dallas homeowners deal with genuine four-season weather swings that most of Texas does not see, and that variability drives home service demand in a few specific directions: heavy summer AC use, real winter freeze risk, and some of the most frequent hail activity anywhere in the United States.

## Why Dallas Is Different

Dallas-Fort Worth sits within what meteorologists sometimes call "Hail Alley," a corridor stretching from Texas through the Plains states that sees more large-hail events than almost anywhere else in the country. This directly drives roofing demand, hail-damaged roofs are one of the most common insurance claims in North Texas, and many Dallas homeowners replace roofs on a shorter timeline than the national average simply due to storm damage rather than age. See our [Dallas roofing guide](/locations/dallas-tx/roofing/).

Unlike Houston or Miami, Dallas gets genuinely cold winters some years, including the same February 2021 winter storm that caused widespread pipe bursts across North Texas. Because homes here are built primarily for heat rather than sustained cold, freeze-related plumbing failures are a real and recurring risk. See our [Dallas plumbing guide](/locations/dallas-tx/plumbing/) and our national guide on [winterizing outdoor faucets](/blog/winterize-outdoor-faucets/).

Summers in Dallas are hot and humid, with more than 100 days a year above 90°F, meaning air conditioning runs heavily from May through September. Because Dallas also needs functional heating in winter, unlike Houston or Phoenix, HVAC systems here typically need to handle both extremes well, which affects sizing and system type decisions. See our [Dallas HVAC guide](/locations/dallas-tx/hvac/).

The Dallas-Fort Worth metro has grown rapidly since the 1980s, and housing stock is a mix of older established neighborhoods and large amounts of newer suburban construction. Many homes are now in the window where original roofing and HVAC systems need replacement, compounded by hail damage that accelerates that timeline.

## Texas Contractor Licensing

As in Houston, Texas has no statewide general contractor license, but HVAC contractors must be licensed through the [Texas Department of Licensing and Regulation (TDLR)](https://www.tdlr.texas.gov/ac/ac.htm), and plumbers through the [Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners](https://www.tsbpe.texas.gov). Roofing contractors are not state-licensed in Texas, which matters a great deal in a hail-heavy market like Dallas where storm-chasing roofing scams are a documented, recurring problem. See our [contractor vetting guide](/blog/how-to-find-good-contractor/) for how to protect yourself.
